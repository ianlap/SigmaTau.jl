Spectrum

The Spectrum page shows the record in the frequency domain: the spectrum of the fractional frequency, $S_y(f)$, above the phase noise $ℒ(f)$ at a carrier frequency or the spectrum of the phase, $S_x(f)$. The two plots share the frequency axis, and a zoom in one zooms the other.

What is computed

Every run computes the two spectra of each clock, from the record as prepared on the Data page and within its analysis range. The estimator choice of the panel has no part in them.

Both are one-sided densities by Welch's method, as psd_y and psd_x compute them: the record is cut into segments that overlap by half, each segment is tapered with a Hann window, and the squared transforms are averaged. The segment is the largest power of two up to a quarter of the record. The frequencies run from the reciprocal of the segment's duration to $1/(2τ_0)$.

Upper$S_y(f)$, fractional frequency1/Hz
Lower$ℒ(f)$ at the carrierdBc/Hz
Lower$S_x(f)$, phase as times²/Hz
The low end of the lower plot

The phase spectrum of FM noise is steep, and the taper then leaks power from the lowest frequencies into the bins above them. In simulation the estimate of $S_x(f)$ is within 2 % from the eighth bin on for white and flicker FM, and on random-walk FM it is still twice too high at the 16th bin (psd_x). On a record with such noise, read the low frequencies from $S_y(f)$, which is less steep by $f^2$. Its first bin should not be read either (psd_y).

Settings

The settings change what is shown, and they apply at once, without a run.

Show power-law slope guidesLabels the stretches of $S_y(f)$ whose slope is close to that of a power law

The phase noise follows from the phase spectrum as $ℒ(f) = \frac{1}{2}(2πν_0)^2 S_x(f)$, in decibels (IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eqs. (A.13) and (A.15)). The record gives $S_x(f)$, and the carrier only scales it: the phase noise of one record reads 20 dB higher at 100 MHz than at 10 MHz.

Slope guides

With one clock, a label such as f⁰ white FM marks a stretch of $S_y(f)$ with the slope of one noise type. The log-binned spectrum is cut into three stretches of equal numbers of points, a line is fitted to each on logarithmic axes, and a stretch is labeled when its slope is within 0.35 of a whole number from −2 to 2.

The stretches are fixed, so one that spans two noise types is labeled by its mean slope. The record of the figure above has white PM, white FM and random-walk FM, and no flicker noise: its first and last stretches each span two of them.

Power-law noise relates these slopes to those of the deviations.

Several clocks

With several clocks, the spectra are overlaid in the colors of the clocks, the settings move to a bar above the plots, and no slope guides are drawn.

Records with gaps

The spectra need a record without missing samples. A run analyzes a record with gaps without its spectrum, and the page then shows No spectrum: the record has gaps in place of the plots. Beside other clocks, a line above the plots names the clock that has none. Fill the gaps on the Data page, or choose an analysis range without them.

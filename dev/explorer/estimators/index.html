Estimators and noise types

This page is a short reference for the names the explorer shows. The Background pages give the definitions and their sources, and Choosing an estimator says which estimator answers which question.

Estimators

Every estimator works on the phase of the record, at averaging times $τ = mτ_0$ from the τ grid of the side panel. A frequency record is integrated to phase first.

The frequency estimators give $σ_y(τ)$, without unit:

EstimatorNameWhat it gives
ADEVOverlapping Allan deviation, adevThe standard measure of frequency stability
MDEVModified Allan deviation, mdevSeparates white PM from flicker PM, which ADEV does not
HDEVOverlapping Hadamard deviation, hdevDoes not respond to linear frequency drift
MHDEVModified Hadamard deviation, mhdevSeparates the two phase noises and does not respond to drift
PDEVParabolic deviation, pdevThe deviation of the readings of an Ω counter. It separates the two phase noises as MDEV does
TOTDEVTotal deviation, totdevADEV with more degrees of freedom at long averaging times
MTOTDEVModified total deviation, mtotdevThe total form of MDEV
HTOTDEVHadamard total deviation, htotdevThe total form of HDEV
MHTOTDEVModified Hadamard total deviation, mhtotdevThe total form of MHDEV
THEO1Thêo1, theo1An estimate of ADEV out to three quarters of the record
THEOHThêoH, theohADEV at short averaging times joined to Thêo1, with its bias removed, at long ones

The time estimators give $σ_x(τ)$, in seconds:

EstimatorNameWhat it gives
TDEVTime deviation, tdevMDEV times $τ/\sqrt{3}$
HTDEVHadamard time deviation, htdevMHDEV times $τ/\sqrt{10/3}$, a statistic defined in this package
TTOTDEVTime total deviation, ttotdevMTOTDEV times $τ/\sqrt{3}$
MTIEMaximum time interval error, mtieThe largest peak-to-peak time error within any interval of length $τ$
TIE rmsRms time interval error, tiermsThe rms change of the time error over $τ$

ADEV, MDEV, HDEV, MHDEV, TDEV, HTDEV, MTIE and TIE rms leave out the terms that touch a missing sample. The other estimators need a record without gaps.

MTOTDEV, HTOTDEV, MHTOTDEV, TTOTDEV and the two Thêo estimators take seconds on a record of $10^5$ samples. The others take a fraction of a second.

Noise types

Clock noise is modeled as a sum of power laws, $S_y(f) = \sum_α h_α f^α$. The label at an averaging time names the term that dominates there. PM stands for phase modulation and FM for frequency modulation:

LabelIn exportsNoise$α$ADEVMDEV
WPMWHPMWhite PM2$τ^{-1}$$τ^{-3/2}$
FPMFLPMFlicker PM1$τ^{-1}$, nearly$τ^{-1}$
WFMWHFMWhite FM0$τ^{-1/2}$$τ^{-1/2}$
FFMFLFMFlicker FM−1$τ^{0}$$τ^{0}$
RWFMRWFMRandom-walk FM−2$τ^{1/2}$$τ^{1/2}$
FWFMFWFMFlicker-walk FM−3nonenone
RRFMRRFMRandom-run FM−4nonenone

The last two columns give the slope of the deviation on logarithmic axes. HDEV follows ADEV, MHDEV and PDEV follow MDEV, and the total and Thêo estimators follow the deviation they extend. The Allan variances do not converge for the last two noise types. The Hadamard family does, with the slopes $τ^1$ and $τ^{3/2}$, and these two labels appear for it alone. Power-law noise has the full table.

The label comes from the noise identification of the run, unless the option Noise type states it. It sets the equivalent degrees of freedom, and with them the confidence interval, and it selects the bias factor of the total and Thêo estimators.

Confidence intervals

The interval of a deviation is that of a $χ^2$ variable with the equivalent degrees of freedom (EDF) of the estimate, at the level of the option Confidence intervals. The EDF depends on the estimator, the noise type, the averaging time and the length of the record, and it falls toward long averaging times, where the intervals widen. The level of 68.3 % is that of one standard deviation of a normal variable. Confidence intervals gives the formulas and says how well the intervals hold.

MTIE and TIE rms have no interval.

Bias correction

The total estimators and Thêo1 reuse the record to gain degrees of freedom, and each then differs from the deviation it stands for by a factor that depends on the noise type. The option Bias correction chooses the factors:

ChoiceFactors
ExactThose of this package, for every noise type and averaging factor
Published tablesThose of SP1065 and of the papers that define the estimators
As Stable32Those that Stable32 applies to each estimator
NoneNo correction, as allantools returns

Bias correction lists the factors and their sources, and shows what each choice does to each estimator.

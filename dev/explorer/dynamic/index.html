Dynamic

The Dynamic page shows whether the stability of a clock changes during the record. It computes the Allan or the Hadamard deviation in a window that slides along the record and draws the result as a map of time against averaging time (Galleani and Tavella (2009)). A single curve of $σ_y(τ)$ averages over the whole record. The map shows a step in the noise level, a burst or a disturbed stretch where it happens.

Settings

SettingEffect
EstimatorDADEV, the dynamic Allan deviation of dadev, or DHDEV, the dynamic Hadamard deviation of dhdev, which does not respond to linear frequency drift within a window
Window lengthA quarter, an eighth, a sixteenth or a thirty-second of the record, and 16 samples at least. The line below gives the samples in a window and the number of windows
ViewMap, or 3D waterfall, a surface that a drag turns

The window moves by half its length, so a window of one eighth of the record gives 15 windows. The averaging times are those of the τ grid of the side panel that fit in one window. A shorter window follows changes more closely, reaches shorter averaging times only and scatters more, since each cell rests on the samples of one window.

The page computes its maps when it is shown and again when the record, its preparation, its analysis range or a setting changes. It needs no run, and the estimators of the panel have no part in it. The record must have no missing samples.

The map

Each row is a window, placed at the time of its center, and each column an averaging time. The color gives the deviation on a logarithmic scale, from the color of the card for the lowest value to the strongest color for the highest.

A clock whose noise does not change shows the same color down each column, within the scatter of the estimate. A change during the record shows as a change down the columns.

Hovering over a cell shows its value, and a click pins it. Selected cell then lists the time of the window's center, the averaging time, the deviation in that window, and under Whole record the ADEV or HDEV of the whole record at that averaging time, from the last run. That line is empty when the estimator was not in the last run or the averaging time not on its grid.

The map carries no confidence intervals. Each row is the deviation of one window of samples, so its interval is that of adev or hdev on a record of the window's length.

Several clocks

With several clocks, each has its own map, on one color scale, and the settings move to a bar above the maps. There is no 3D view and no selected cell.

Compare at τ, below the maps, plots the deviation of every clock against the time of the window, at one averaging time. A dotted line gives the value of each clock over the whole record, from the last run. The menu of the card or a click on a column of a map changes the averaging time. A clock with another sample interval is shown at its nearest averaging time, within half an octave, which the legend then names.

Stability

The Stability page plots the deviations of the last run against the averaging time, on logarithmic axes, and lists their values. The explorer opens on this page.

The plot

Frequency σy(τ) and Time σx(τ) switch between the two kinds of estimator: the fractional frequency deviations, which have no unit, and the time deviations, in seconds. The page shows the estimators of one kind at a time. Estimators and noise types lists which is which.

With one clock, each estimator is a line in its own color, named in the legend. With several clocks the color is that of the clock and the line style that of the estimator, and the status bar lists the line styles.

The line beside the two buttons states the settings of the side panel: the confidence level, the τ grid, the bias correction when an estimator that takes one is in the list, and the noise type when it is not identified at each averaging time.

ControlEffect
Noise IDShows the noise type identified at each averaging time in a strip above the plot
CI bandDraws the confidence intervals as a shaded band. Without it they are error bars
FitDraws the noise model and lists its coefficients
Pin runKeeps the curves of this run on the plot, as pinned runs

Mask menuOn the time axis, draws a telecom mask

Hovering over a point shows its averaging time and value, with the EDF and the noise type behind its interval. MTIE and TIE rms have no interval.

Noise ID and CI band need the confidence intervals, since the noise types are identified for them. Both are disabled while Confidence intervals is off in the panel.

Noise identification

The strip above the plot shows the noise type that the run identified at each averaging time, joined into stretches of one type. It comes from the first estimator in the list that reports noise types, and it is shown on the frequency axis only. Noise types explains the labels, and Noise identification says how they are found.

A label is what the test reads at that averaging time, and not always a noise that the record holds. The record of the figure above has white PM, white FM and random-walk FM. Its stretches of FPM and FFM lie where two of them cross, and there the test reads the type between them (How far to trust a label).

With one clock, a dashed line along each stretch has the slope of that noise type in that estimator, drawn a little above the curve, and dotted vertical lines mark where the type changes. A curve that leaves its guide has a mixture of noises there, or a label that does not fit. With several clocks there is one strip for each clock, outlined in its color, and no guides.

The table

With one clock, Per-τ results lists the values of the estimator chosen in its menu:

ColumnHolds
τ (s)The averaging time
σThe deviation
EDFThe equivalent degrees of freedom of the estimate
noiseThe noise type at that averaging time

Hovering over a row shows its confidence interval and the number of terms in the estimate. Every value is in the TSV and JSON exports.

The noise model

Fit draws a power-law model of the noise of the record, $S_y(f) = \sum_α h_α f^α$, as a dotted line under each curve of ADEV, MDEV, HDEV, MHDEV and TDEV: the deviation that the model gives in that estimator. Under the table, Noise model lists the coefficients $h_α$ above zero and the ADEV that the model gives at 1 s, 10 s, 100 s, 1000 s, $10^4$ s and one day, as far as half the span of the record.

The model is fitted to the MHDEV of the record on the octave grid, by nonnegative least squares over the five noise types from white PM to random-walk FM, as powerlaw_fit does. It does not depend on the estimators in the list or on the τ grid. A noise that the record does not have comes out small and positive, so compare the dotted lines with the curves before using a coefficient.

A model built by hand

Edit opens a list of components that takes the place of the automatic fit. Choose a noise type, give the span of averaging times over which it dominates the MHDEV, and press Add. An empty field leaves that end of the span open. The level of the noise is fitted over its span to what the other components leave of the MHDEV, and every level is fitted again when a component comes or goes, as add_component! does. The explorer runs the analysis after each change.

While the list is open, a dashed gray line shows the MHDEV that the components leave. Undo removes the last component, and Back to the automatic fit removes them all. Each of the seven noise types enters once.

The choice From the fitted model of the option Noise type uses the automatic fit, also when a model has been built by hand.

The card, and the label that appears when the pointer is on the line, name the edition and the tables of the recommendation that the limits were read from. TELECOM_MASKS lists them. The recommendations measure MTIE and TDEV through a 10 Hz low-pass filter, or at one sample per second without a filter for the 1 PPS output of a time clock. The explorer applies no filter and compares the record as it was measured, as mask_check does.

Pinned runs

Pin run keeps the curves of the last run on the plot, as thin broken lines under those of every later run. Each pin is a chip beside the button, Pin 1, Pin 2, and hovering over it lists what its run was: the estimators, the τ grid, the analysis range and the drift removal. × removes the pin.

A pin shows what a change does: pin, change the drift removal, the range or the bias correction, and run again. Pins are part of the plot and of its image. They are not in the other exports or in a saved session.

A phase-noise table

Phase noise… opens a file with two columns, the offset frequency in Hz and the phase noise $ℒ(f)$ in dBc/Hz, as a phase-noise analyzer exports it or as a data sheet lists it. Lines of text above the numbers are skipped. The explorer converts the table to ADEV, MDEV, TDEV, HDEV and MHDEV with spectrum_sigma, at the carrier frequency set on the Spectrum page, and draws those of the estimators in the list as broken lines, like a pinned run. Its chip is ℒ(f) 1, and × removes it.

A table covers a band of offset frequencies, and the curves are the deviations of the noise inside that band. They run from the shortest averaging time that the band resolves, one over its highest offset, to a tenth of one over its lowest. Hovering over the chip gives the band, how much the noise below it would add to the Allan variance at the longest averaging time, and whether ADEV and HDEV depend on the bandwidth of the measurement: on white and flicker PM they do, and the curves then hold for a bandwidth equal to the highest offset of the table. Power-law noise has the conversion.

A table of a few offsets beside a record measured at one-second intervals puts the short and the long averaging times of one oscillator on one plot. Like a pin, the table is not in a saved session.

Several clocks

With several clocks the table is Compare at τ: the value of each clock at one averaging time, for the first three estimators of the list on the axis shown. The lowest value in each column is in bold, which marks the most stable clock at that averaging time. A click on the plot moves the averaging time to the nearest one of the run, and a dashed line marks it. The menu of the card does the same.

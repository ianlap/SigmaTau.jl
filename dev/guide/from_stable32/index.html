Coming from Stable32

Stable32 and SigmaTau compute the same statistics from the same definitions (NIST SP1065), and on the shipped fixture they agree to Stable32's five printed figures (Validation). This page maps a Stable32 session onto SigmaTau and lists the few defaults that differ.

Reading the file

Stable32's .phd / .frd files, and text files with its header (Tau: lines closed by # Header End), read with no keywords:

using SigmaTau
p = read_data("clock.phd")          # PhaseData, τ₀ from the Tau: line
y = read_data("clock.frd")          # FrequencyData
p = read_phase("export.dat")        # a Stable32 text export

The modified Hadamard class (mhdev, htdev, mhtotdev) has no Stable32 counterpart.

Averaging times

Stable32's octave spacing is SigmaTau's Octave, the default; Decade, AllTaus, HalfOctave and the others are TauModes, and an explicit list of averaging factors works too:

adev(p)                    # τ = 1, 2, 4, … × τ₀
adev(p, Decade)
adev(p, [1, 10, 100])      # m, so τ = m·τ₀

The longest τ follows each estimator's own limit, as in Stable32.

Confidence intervals and noise identification

Every call identifies the noise type at each τ (lag-1 autocorrelation, with the B1 and R(n) ratios where those apply) and returns χ² intervals from the Greenhall–Riley EDF, the method Stable32 uses. The default level is 68.3 % (Stable32's 1σ); confidence=0.95 changes it and ci=false skips the intervals for speed.

r = adev(p; confidence=0.95)
r.tau, r.dev, ci_lower(r), ci_upper(r), r.noise_type, r.edf

The detrending choices change the identified noise type only at long τ, where few averaged samples remain; when it flips, the EDF and the interval at that τ follow it, and for HTOTDEV the bias factor too. Validation shows each case on the shipped fixture.

The flicker-PM row shows at medium τ. On a pure flicker-PM record of 99 999 frequency samples made by Stable32's own generator, Stable32's HDEV run labels averaging factors 16, 32 and 128 to 512 white PM and the others up to 4096 flicker PM; SigmaTau labels all of them flicker PM. The deviations agree to 2e-5 at every τ and the intervals to 3e-4 wherever the labels do. Where they do not, Stable32's interval is ±0.34 % against a simulated scatter of ±0.5 % (m = 16) to ±1.8 % (m = 512), and SigmaTau's is ±0.55 % to ±1.7 %.

The FM-limits row shows from medium τ. On decimated phase the lag-1 estimate of flicker FM settles at α = −1.45, next to the −1.5 at which rounding turns it into random-walk FM: in simulation (N = 4096) pure flicker FM keeps its label in 72 % of records at m = 8 and 48 % at m = 64 with rounding, and in 100 % and 87 % with SigmaTau's limits. Random-walk FM keeps its label in 98 % or more of records at those m either way.

The B1 row shows at the longest τ, and there it moves σ as well as the interval for the bias-corrected total estimators. At the record length the B1 limit for random-walk FM sits far above what a few averaged points can reach, so that convention almost never reports it: in simulation (N = 1024, 6 to 24 averaged points) random-walk FM is found in 1 to 2 % of records and flicker FM in 24 to 48 %, against 60 to 75 % and 34 to 79 % at the number of averaged points. White FM is found less often that way, in 33 to 82 % of records against 65 to 88 %. Below 6 averaged points the ratio is close to a coin toss, so SigmaTau reports the label measured at the longest averaging factor that still has 6. On the 8192-point validation fixture Stable32 reads white FM at τ = 1024 s and beyond, where ADEV is flat and then rising and SigmaTau reads flicker FM and random-walk FM; TOTDEV is then 3.1 %, 10.9 % and 26.5 % above Stable32's at 1024, 2048 and 4096 s. Validation has the rows.

Gaps

Stable32 needs gaps filled before most runs. In SigmaTau NaN marks a missing sample; ADEV, MDEV, HDEV, MHDEV, TDEV, HTDEV, MTIE and TIE rms skip the terms that touch it, and fillgaps (or fillgaps=true on the readers) fills the record for the rest (Choosing an estimator).

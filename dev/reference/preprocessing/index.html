Preprocessing

These functions prepare a record for the analysis: they remove a trend, fill missing samples, and find or replace outliers. Each returns a new record and leaves its argument as it was. Gaps, outliers and drift says when each step is called for and what it costs.

read_phase and read_frequency apply the first two while they read, with the keywords detrend and fillgaps, and read_data the second.

SigmaTau.detrend — Function
detrend(data; method=:linear) → PhaseData or FrequencyData

Copy of a record with a trend removed from its samples.

methodRemovesOn phase dataOn frequency data
:meanthe meana time offseta frequency offset
:endpointthe line through the first and last samplesa frequency offseta linear frequency drift
:lineara least-squares linea frequency offseta linear frequency drift
:quadratica least-squares parabolaa linear frequency drifta drift that changes linearly
:nonenothing

The least-squares line through phase data is the optimum estimate of a frequency offset under white PM noise, and the least-squares parabola that of a frequency drift. The least-squares line through frequency data is the optimum estimate of a drift under white FM noise (SP1065 §5.18, §5.21 and §5.22). A fit is judged by whether its residuals are random. drift_fit reports the drift, by these fits and by other estimators, and fits the log and diffusion models as well; detrend(data, fit) removes the model of its result.

Missing samples (NaN) stay in place and do not enter the fit, and :endpoint takes the first and last samples that are present.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> x = PhaseData([2.0 + 3k + 0.5k^2 for k in 0:9], 1.0);

julia> maximum(abs, detrend(x; method=:quadratic).x) < 1e-12
true

julia> print(detrend(x; method=:endpoint).x[[1, end]])
[0.0, 0.0]
source
detrend(data, fit::DriftFit) → PhaseData or FrequencyData

Copy of a record with the frequency model of its drift_fit removed: fit.fit from the frequency samples, or its running sum times $τ_0$ from the phase samples, which leaves the first one as it is.

The deviation of the result shows whether the model left white residuals, and it is the deviation that the variance of the drift estimate is read from (Weiss and Hackman 1992). Removing an estimated drift lowers the deviations at the longest averaging times: after :bisection on an odd number of phase samples, the Allan deviation at half the record is zero.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> x = PhaseData([2.0 + 3k + 0.5k^2 for k in 0:10], 1.0);

julia> r = detrend(x, drift_fit(x; method=:bisection));

julia> maximum(abs, r.x .- 2.0) < 1e-12
true

SigmaTau.fillgaps — Function
fillgaps(data; form=:auto) → PhaseData or FrequencyData

Copy of a record with its missing samples (NaN) filled by the reflection method of Howe and Schlossberger (2022), after the script the paper gives for it (Champagne et al. (2021)). The measured samples, the sample interval, the source and the start time are returned as they came, and a record without missing samples is returned as a copy. With fillgaps=true, read_data, read_phase and read_frequency fill a record as they read it.

A gap of $n$ samples takes $n$ of the samples beside it, reversed in time and inverted in sign, and a straight line is added so that the low-pass filtered fill meets the filtered data at both ends. Filling starts at the gap after the longest run of data between two gaps and proceeds to the right, then to the left, so a fill may rest on an earlier one. The samples come from the run just passed, from the run on the other side where that one is shorter than the gap, and half from each side where both are. A gap of one sample takes the mean of its neighbors. An ErrorException is thrown if the gaps are too long for the data between them.

The reflected samples carry the noise of the record only where the series that is filled lies between white noise and a random walk. In a smoother series, such as the phase of flicker or random-walk FM, the joins stand out of the noise. In a rougher one, such as the frequency of white or flicker PM, the error of the level at a join adds up across the gap. The paper prescribes neither the series nor the length of the filter, and this package sets both from the record:

  • Series. form=:auto reads the two-sample deviation of the frequency samples past the gaps, at the averaging factors $m = 1, 2, 4, …$ that at least $8m$ terms measure. The frequency is filled when that deviation has nowhere fallen to $1/\sqrt{2}$ of its value at $τ_0$, the fall of white FM in one octave, or has risen more than 10 % above its lowest value by the last factor: flicker or random-walk FM is then present. Otherwise the phase is filled.
  • Filter. The script's filter averages over about 16 samples. Here it averages over the longest of those factors at which the two-sample deviation of the series being filled is within 10 % of its lowest value.
  • Trend. A least-squares parabola in phase, or line in frequency, is set aside while the series is filled and put back after. The filter mirrors each block of samples at its ends, and a slope would pull the filtered ends off the data. In simulation a phase record with a frequency offset of $100σ_y(τ_0)$ and one gap of 30 %, filled with the script's filter and the trend left in, read 48 times high at $τ_0$.

Keywords

  • form=:auto: the series that is filled: :phase, :frequency, or :auto for the choice above. A phase record filled as frequency keeps its measured phases, because the filled differences are adjusted to sum to the measured change across each gap. In a frequency record filled as phase, the phase step across each gap is not measured and is left free.
A filled record is not the record

Fill only for a function that needs a complete record, such as the spectra, the total, Thêo, parabolic and dynamic deviations and the cross estimators. The Allan and Hadamard deviations, their modified and time forms and tierms leave out the terms a missing sample enters, which does not bias them, and mtie takes the peak-to-peak of the samples measured (see Timestamps and gaps).

In simulation (4096 samples; the five power-law noises and seven sums of two of them; phase and frequency records; one to six gaps removing up to 30 % of the record; with and without a frequency offset and drift; 100 records each) the Allan deviation of the filled record was within 5 % of that of the whole record from $τ_0$ to $256τ_0$, and within 11 % at $256τ_0$ for a gap of 30 % at the end of the record. The modified Allan deviation was within 4 % up to $16τ_0$. A fill does not recover three things:

  • The modified Allan deviation of white PM at long $τ$, which resolves an error of level that the Allan deviation does not. With 30 % of the record missing it read up to 1.3 times high at $256τ_0$, and 1.9 times for a frequency record with six gaps.
  • The phase step across a gap of a frequency record, so that the errors of many gaps add. With gaps of 1 to 12 samples removing a quarter of a frequency record, white PM read 1.3 and 1.9 times high in the Allan deviation at $64τ_0$ and $256τ_0$ (4.8 and 19 times in the modified one), flicker PM 1.5 times high at $256τ_0$ and white FM 6 % high, and white PM with flicker FM read 15 % low there. The same gaps in a phase record left the Allan deviation within 8 %.
  • A periodic term that stands out of the noise. On a phase record of white FM with a sine in the frequency, of period 512 samples and amplitude $3σ_y(τ_0)$, the Allan deviation read 1.15 times high and the modified one 1.40 times at $256τ_0$ across one gap of 30 %, where the frequency is filled (1.00 with form=:phase). Past gaps of 1 to 12 samples, where the phase is filled in nine records in ten, it read 1.42 times high at $τ_0$ (1.00 with form=:frequency), and 1.05 times at a third of that amplitude. Remove such a term before filling and put it back after.

The choice of series matters most where gaps are many. Random-walk FM filled as phase read 1.4 times high at $τ_0$ with one gap of 30 % and 12 times with the short gaps above. A frequency record of white PM filled as frequency read 1.9 and 5 times high at $256τ_0$.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> x = PhaseData([0.0, 1.0, NaN, 3.0, 4.0], 1.0);

julia> print(fillgaps(x).x)
[0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0]

SigmaTau.find_outliers — Function
find_outliers(data; nsigma=5) → Vector{Int}

Indices of the outlying samples of a record, by the median absolute deviation test of SP1065 §10.11 and of the Check function of Stable32. A fractional frequency sample $y_i$ is an outlier when

\[\lvert y_i - m \rvert > n_σ \frac{\mathrm{MAD}}{0.6745}, \qquad m = \operatorname{median}(y), \quad \mathrm{MAD} = \operatorname{median} \lvert y - m \rvert .\]

The factor 0.6745 makes the scaled MAD equal to the standard deviation of normally distributed data. The default nsigma=5 is the limit that SP1065 calls common and the default of Stable32.

For a FrequencyData record the test is made on its samples. For a PhaseData record it is made on the first differences, because a phase jump is a spike in frequency, and a flagged difference $x_{i+1} - x_i$ marks phase sample $i + 1$, where the jump lands. A single displaced phase sample $x_j$ therefore marks samples $j$ and $j + 1$.

Missing samples are left out of the median and of the MAD and are never marked. The test is applied once; the automatic removal of Stable32 repeats it until no outlier is left. The result is empty when no sample exceeds the limit, and when the MAD is zero, as in a coarsely quantized record where more than half of the samples are equal.

source
SigmaTau.remove_outliers — Function
remove_outliers(data; nsigma=5) → PhaseData or FrequencyData

Copy of a record with the outliers found by find_outliers replaced by filled values from fillgaps. A record without outliers is returned as it is.

An isolated outlier takes the mean of its two neighbors. A run of them is filled by reflection, with the reservations stated for fillgaps.

A PhaseData record is cleaned as frequency: it is differenced, the outlying frequency samples are replaced, and the result is summed back to phase from the first sample. A phase jump is one outlying frequency sample, so this takes the jump out, where replacing the phase sample it lands on would leave it in.

Samples that were missing stay missing. Only the outliers are replaced, and fillgaps fills the gaps when that is wanted.

SP1065 §10.11 and Stable32 replace an outlier by a gap, which the estimators then skip. For a frequency record that is y[find_outliers(fd)] .= NaN on a copy of its samples.

Examples

fd = read_frequency("counter_log.csv")
clean = remove_outliers(fd)                # limit of 5
strict = remove_outliers(fd; nsigma=3)

Frequency drift

drift_fit fits a model to the fractional frequency of a record and reports the drift rate with its standard error. A linear drift has five estimators, each suited to a noise. It measures the drift and removes nothing: detrend removes a line or a parabola fitted to the samples, or the model of a fit.

SigmaTau.drift_fit — Function
drift_fit(data; model=:linear, method=:frequency_fit) → DriftFit

Fit of a frequency drift model to a record. The result gives the parameters, their standard errors and the drift rate $dy/dt$ at the end of the record, per day.

Models

  • :linear, $y(t) = a + bt$: a constant drift $b$.
  • :quadratic, $y(t) = a + bt + ct^2$: a drift that changes linearly with time.
  • :log, $y(t) = a\ln(1 + bt) + c$: the stabilization of an oscillator.
  • :diffusion, $y(t) = a + b\sqrt{t + c}$.

The linear, log and diffusion models are the frequency drift models of SP1065 §5.22 and of the Drift function of Stable32; the constant $c$ of the log model is in the form its test suite reports. $t$ runs from 0 at the first sample, so $a$ of the linear model is the frequency there. Stable32 counts the samples from 1 and reports the intercept one sample interval earlier; the slope is the same.

Methods

method chooses the estimator of the linear model; the other models take :frequency_fit. With $N$ phase samples $x_n$ at the interval $τ_0$ and $M = N - 1$ frequency samples $y_n$:

methodDrift $b$Least variance on
:frequency_fitslope of the least-squares line through $y$white FM
:phase_fit$2c$ of the least-squares parabola $a + bt + ct^2$ through $x$white PM
:bisectiondifference of the mean frequencies of the two halves of the record, over half its length
:second_differencemean of the second differences of $x$, over $τ_0^2$random-walk FM
:four_pointthe four-point estimator, below

These are the Linear Fit and Bisection Fit of Stable32 for frequency data and its Quadratic Fit, Avg of 2nd Diffs and 3-Point Fit for phase data (SP1065 §5.21 and §5.22); the three-point fit and the bisection are one estimator, $4[x_N - 2x_{mid} + x_1]/T^2$ on a record of length $T$ with an odd number of phase samples (Weiss and Hackman 1992, Eq. (1)). The four-point estimator of Greenhall 1997, Eq. (3) is not in Stable32. With $w_n = \sum_{j \le n} x_j$, $n_1$ the integer nearest $N/10$ and $r = n_1/N$, it is

\[b = \frac{6}{N^3 τ_0^2\, r(1 - r)} \left[w_N - w_0 - \frac{w_{N-n_1} - w_{n_1}}{1 - 2r}\right].\]

Every method returns the true drift on a record without noise. On a noisy record the variance of the estimate depends on the noise, and Greenhall 1997, Table 1 gives it for each method and power-law noise. The variance of the bisection is within a factor 1.4 of the least on white FM, flicker FM and random-walk FM. That of the four-point estimator is within a factor 1.6 of the least on all five noises, white PM to random-walk FM. At a given record length, the variances of the bisection and of the frequency fit on white PM, and that of the mean second difference on white FM, grow with the number of samples.

Standard errors

The standard errors of :frequency_fit and :phase_fit are those of least squares, from the scatter of the residuals, and that of :second_difference is the standard deviation of the mean of the second differences (Barnes 1983, §II to §IV). Each assumes the noise on which its method has the least variance. On a redder noise it is too small, as on a record with flicker FM or random-walk FM: on the quartz oscillators of Barnes 1983, Table 6, about 17 times for the fit to phase and 5 times for the fit to frequency. On a whiter noise it is too large. The deviation of the residuals shows whether they are white.

:bisection and :four_point have no standard error computed from the record (NaN). On a record of length $T$ their variances are

\[\frac{8}{T^2}\,σ_y^2\!\left(\frac{T}{2}\right) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{A^2}{T^2}\,\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_y^2\!\left(\frac{T}{3}\right), \quad 3.14 \le A \le 3.80,\]

for any of the five noises (Weiss and Hackman 1992, Eq. (7); Greenhall 1997, §5), with the deviations of the record less its true drift. The record itself cannot measure them at these averaging times, so they are extrapolated from the deviations at shorter ones, by judgment of the noise type.

Gaps

A PhaseData record is differenced to frequency first, except for :phase_fit and :four_point, and missing samples are skipped. :four_point needs a record without gaps, and so does :phase_fit of a FrequencyData record, which is integrated to phase.

Examples

A drift of 2 × 10⁻¹⁵ per second is 1.728 × 10⁻¹⁰ per day:

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> y = FrequencyData(1e-9 .+ 2e-15 .* (0:999), 1.0);

julia> f = drift_fit(y);

julia> round(f.rate; sigdigits=4)
1.728e-10

julia> round(drift_fit(y; method=:four_point).rate; sigdigits=4)
1.728e-10

SigmaTau.DriftFit — Type
DriftFit

Result of drift_fit: a frequency drift model fitted to $y(t)$, with the standard errors of its parameters and the drift rate at the end of the record. The residuals are f.y .- f.fit, and detrend(data, f) is the record without the fitted model.

coef holds the parameters of the model, with $t$ in seconds from the first sample:

model$y(t)$coef
:linear$a + bt$[a, b]
:quadratic$a + bt + ct^2$[a, b, c]
:log$a\ln(1 + bt) + c$[a, b, c]
:diffusion$a + b\sqrt{t + c}$[a, b, c]

Fields

  • model::Symbol: Model fitted: :linear, :quadratic, :log or :diffusion.

  • method::Symbol: Estimator of the parameters, one of the methods of drift_fit.

  • coef::Vector{Float64}: Parameters of the model, as in the table above.

  • stderr::Vector{Float64}: Standard error of each parameter; NaN where the method gives none.

  • rate::Float64: Drift rate $dy/dt$ at the end of the record, per day.

  • rate_stderr::Float64: Standard error of rate, per day; NaN where the method gives none.

  • t::Vector{Float64}: Sample times, in seconds from the first sample.

  • y::Vector{Float64}: Fractional frequency; NaN at a missing sample.

  • fit::Vector{Float64}: The model at each t.

  • rms::Float64: Rms of the residuals y − fit over the measured samples.

Preprocessing

Clean a record before the analysis: remove a trend, fill gaps and replace outliers. Each function returns a new record and leaves its input untouched. read_phase and read_frequency can apply detrend and fillgaps while reading.

SigmaTau.detrend — Function
detrend(pd::PhaseData; method::Symbol=:linear) → PhaseData
detrend(fd::FrequencyData; method::Symbol=:linear) → FrequencyData

Return a new PhaseData / FrequencyData with a detrended sample vector. The original is left untouched.

method is one of :linear (least-squares straight line, default), :quadratic (least-squares parabola; on phase data this removes a frequency drift, the preprocessing SP1065 prescribes before the total estimators), :endpoint (line through first and last samples), :mean (zero-mean), or :none (returns a copy unchanged). On phase data :linear removes a frequency offset, not a drift. Gaps (NaN) stay where they are and do not enter the fit.

Examples

pd = PhaseData(cumsum(randn(1024)) .+ 1e-3 .* (0:1023), 1.0)
pd_clean = detrend(pd)                    # :linear default
pd_zero  = detrend(pd; method=:mean)

SigmaTau.fillgaps — Function
fillgaps(data::PhaseData;     form=:auto) → PhaseData
fillgaps(data::FrequencyData; form=:auto) → FrequencyData

Fill the missing (NaN) samples of an equispaced record by the reflection method of D. A. Howe and N. Schlossberger, IEEE Trans. UFFC 69(2), 468–472 (2022), doi:10.1109/TUFFC.2021.3137425, as their script has it (C. Champagne, D. A. Howe and N. Schlossberger, fillgaps.py, doi:10.5281/zenodo.5594587), so that an estimator which cannot skip gaps can run. The measured samples are returned as they came, with the same tau0, source and start. The readers put timestamped samples on their grid first, so read_data(path; fillgaps=true) fills the readings a file is missing.

A gap of n samples takes the n samples beside it (the run before it, the run after it when that one is too short, half from each when neither is long enough), reflected and inverted, and a straight line is added so that the low-pass-filtered fill meets the filtered data at both ends. A gap of one sample takes the mean of its neighbours. Gaps are filled outward from the longest run, so a fill can serve the next gap; a layout with no run long enough to mirror throws an ErrorException.

The reflected samples carry the noise of the record only where the series being filled lies between white and random walk. A smoother series shows the added line as a step at each join (the phase of random-walk FM read 10 times high at τ₀ after a 30 % fill), and in a rougher one the error of the level at the joins is integrated across the gap (the frequency of white PM read 12 times high at 256 τ₀). Two things the paper leaves open are therefore set from the record:

  • form, the series that is filled. :auto reads the two-sample deviation of the frequency samples past the gaps, at the octaves of m samples that at least 8m terms measure. It fills the frequency when that deviation nowhere falls to 1/√2 of its value at τ₀, the fall of white FM in one octave, or turns up again by the last octave: flicker or random-walk FM is then present and the phase too smooth. It fills the phase otherwise. :phase and :frequency force the choice. A phase record filled as frequency has the filled differences corrected to sum to the measured phase change across each gap; a frequency record filled as phase leaves the phase step across each gap free.
  • The span the filter averages over (16 samples in the script): the longest of those octaves at which the two-sample deviation of the series being filled is within 10 % of its lowest, so the level beside a gap is averaged for as long as averaging improves it.

The trend of the series, a least-squares parabola in phase or line in frequency through the measured samples, is set aside while it is filled. The filter mirrors each block at its ends, so a slope would pull the filtered endpoints off the data: with a frequency offset of 100 σ_y(τ₀) a phase record filled as scripted read 48 times high at τ₀.

On simulated records of 4096 samples (twelve mixes of the five power-law noises, as phase and as frequency records, one to six gaps removing up to 30 %, with and without offset and drift, 100 records each) the Allan deviation of the filled record stayed within 5 % of the original's from τ₀ to 256 τ₀, or 11 % at 256 τ₀ for a 30 % gap at the end of the record, which has data on one side only; the modified Allan deviation stayed within 4 % to 16 τ₀. Three things a fill does not recover:

  • MDEV at the longest τ where white PM dominates: 1.1 to 1.3 times high, and 1.9 for a frequency record with six gaps. Averaging m phases resolves a level error that ADEV does not see.
  • The phase step across a gap in a frequency record, which is not measured, so the errors of many gaps add. With gaps of 1 to 12 samples removing a quarter of a frequency record, white PM read 1.3 and 1.9 times high in ADEV at 64 and 256 τ₀ (4.8 and 19 in MDEV), flicker PM 1.5 at 256 τ₀ and white FM 1.06; the same gaps in a phase record stayed within 8 % in ADEV.
  • A periodic term that stands out of the noise, which neither form carries in every layout. A sine in the frequency (period 512 samples, amplitude 3 σ_y(τ₀)) on white FM read 1.15 high in ADEV and 1.40 in MDEV at 256 τ₀ across one 30 % gap, where the frequency is filled and reflection inverts the sine (1.00 with form=:phase), and 1.42 at τ₀ past gaps of 1 to 12 samples, where the phase is filled (1.00 with form=:frequency; 1.05 at a third of the amplitude). Remove such a term before filling and put it back after.

The estimators that skip gaps (adev, mdev, hdev, mhdev and their time forms, on the record with its NaNs) need no fill and have none of these errors. The choice of form and span is this package's, checked by those simulations; the paper prescribes neither.

SigmaTau.find_outliers — Function
find_outliers(data::Union{PhaseData,FrequencyData}; nsigma::Real=5) → Vector{Int}

Return the (sorted, unique) indices of outlying samples using the Stable32-style median-absolute-deviation test (Riley, Jul 2008): a sample is an outlier when

|yᵢ − median(y)| / (MAD / 0.6745) > nsigma

where MAD = median(|y − median(y)|) and 0.6745 rescales the MAD into an estimate of the standard deviation for normally distributed data. nsigma=5 is Stable32's default deglitching threshold.

For FrequencyData the test runs directly on the samples y. For PhaseData it runs on the first differences Δᵢ = x[i+1] − x[i] (a phase glitch appears as a step in frequency), and each flagged difference Δᵢ flags phase sample i+1, the sample where the step lands. An isolated phase glitch at x[j] therefore produces two flagged differences (Δⱼ₋₁ up, Δⱼ down) and flags samples j and j+1.

NaN samples (existing gaps) are excluded from the median/MAD estimates and are never flagged. Returns an empty vector when nothing exceeds the threshold, or when the MAD is zero (more than half the samples equal, as in a coarsely quantized record), which leaves no scale to test against.

See also remove_outliers, fillgaps.

SigmaTau.remove_outliers — Function
remove_outliers(data::Union{PhaseData,FrequencyData}; nsigma::Real=5)
    → PhaseData | FrequencyData

Detect outliers with find_outliers and return a new record with the flagged samples replaced by imputed values from fillgaps (Howe & Schlossberger's reflect-and-filter algorithm, which preserves the local noise character). The tau0 and source fields carry over; the input is left untouched. When nothing is flagged the input is returned as-is. Samples already missing stay missing: only the outliers are replaced, and fillgaps fills the rest when that is wanted.

PhaseData is cleaned in frequency space: the record is differenced to yᵢ = (x[i+1] − x[i]) / τ₀, the flagged frequency samples are gapped and filled, and the result is integrated back to phase from x[1]. A phase step is a single bad frequency sample, so this removes the step itself rather than refilling the phase sample it lands on (which would leave the step in place).

This is the Stable32 deglitching idiom (Riley, Jul 2008): flag samples more than nsigma scaled MADs from the median, gap them, and fill.

Examples

fd = read_frequency("counter_log.csv")
fd_clean = remove_outliers(fd)               # 5-sigma default
fd_strict = remove_outliers(fd; nsigma=3)

See also find_outliers, fillgaps.

Frequency drift

drift_fit fits Stable32's drift models to the frequency and reports the aging per day with its standard error. Compare the fits and the residuals before removing anything; detrend and the CLI's --remove-drift remove a polynomial fitted to the phase.

SigmaTau.drift_fit — Function
drift_fit(data; model=:linear) → DriftFit

Fit a frequency drift model to a record, as Stable32's Drift function does: :linear and :quadratic polynomials, the MIL-PRF-55310 :log aging model a·ln(1 + b·t) + c, or the :diffusion model a + b·√(t + c). Phase records are differenced to frequency first. NaN samples are skipped.

The standard errors are the least-squares ones, from the residual scatter. They assume white residuals, so they are too small when the record has flicker or random-walk FM; compare the residuals' σ(τ) with the clock's to judge how far.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> y = 1e-9 .+ 2e-15 .* (0:999);   # 2e-15 per second

julia> f = drift_fit(FrequencyData(collect(y), 1.0));

julia> round(f.rate; sigdigits=4)        # per day
1.728e-10

SigmaTau.DriftFit — Type
DriftFit

Result of drift_fit: a frequency drift model fitted to y(t), with standard errors and the drift rate. The residuals are f.y .- f.fit.

coef holds the model parameters, with t in seconds from the first sample:

modely(t)coef
:lineara + b·t[a, b]
:quadratica + b·t + c·t²[a, b, c]
:loga·ln(1 + b·t) + c[a, b, c]
:diffusiona + b·√(t + c)[a, b, c]

  • model::Symbol: Model name, one of :linear, :quadratic, :log, :diffusion.

  • coef::Vector{Float64}: Model parameters; see the table above.

  • stderr::Vector{Float64}: One-σ standard error of each parameter.

  • rate::Float64: Drift rate dy/dt at the end of the record, per day.

  • rate_stderr::Float64: One-σ standard error of rate, per day.

  • t::Vector{Float64}: Sample times in seconds from the first sample.

  • y::Vector{Float64}: Fractional frequency (NaN at gaps).

  • fit::Vector{Float64}: Fitted model at each t.

  • rms::Float64: RMS of the residuals y − fit over the measured samples.

Dead time

A counter that measures for a time $τ_0$ and then waits before it measures again leaves dead time between its readings. The readings are frequency averages over $τ_0$ that start every $T_0 > τ_0$, they cannot be summed to phase, and their two-sample variance is not the Allan variance. For power-law noise the two differ by a known factor, given by the bias functions of Barnes (1969) and Barnes and Allan (1990). This page defines the functions, corrects an Allan deviation with them, and says where the correction holds.

The bias functions

Write $σ^2(N, T, τ)$ for the sample variance, with divisor $N - 1$, of $N$ frequency averages over $τ$ that start every $T$, and $r = T/τ$. The Allan variance is the expected $σ^2(2, τ, τ)$. On noise whose Allan variance goes as $τ^μ$:

FunctionRatio of expected variancesUse
bias_b1(N, r, μ)$σ^2(N, T, τ)$ over $σ^2(2, T, τ)$$N$ samples against two
bias_b2(r, μ)$σ^2(2, T, τ)$ over $σ^2(2, τ, τ)$dead time against none
bias_b3(N, M, r, μ)$σ^2(N, M, T, τ)$ over $σ^2(N, T, τ)$dead time spread among $M$ averaged readings against the same dead time in one block

In $B_3$ the samples are averages of $M$ readings, so that $τ = Mτ_0$ and $T = MT_0$. The exponent is $μ = -2$ for white and flicker PM, −1 for white FM, 0 for flicker FM and 1 for random-walk FM, and the functions take any $-2 ≤ μ ≤ 2$ or the noise type.

The two-sample deviation of $M$-reading averages, which is what adev computes from the readings at averaging factor $M$, estimates $\sqrt{B_2(r, μ)\,B_3(2, M, r, μ)}$ times $σ_y(Mτ_0)$. For 100 s measured once an hour, $r = 36$:

using SigmaTau, Printf

noises = [:WHPM, :WHFM, :FLFM, :RWFM]
println(rpad("M", 6), join(lpad.(noises, 8)))
for M in (1, 4, 16, 64)
    println(rpad(M, 6), join(map(n -> @sprintf("%8.3f", sqrt(bias_b2(36, n) * bias_b3(2, M, 36, n))), noises)))
end
M         WHPM    WHFM    FLFM    RWFM
1        0.816   1.000   1.915   7.314
4        1.633   1.000   1.273   6.090
16       3.266   1.000   1.072   6.006
64       6.532   1.000   1.018   6.000

White FM needs no correction. On white PM the uncorrected deviation reads low at $M = 1$ and high beyond it, and falls as $τ^{-1/2}$, the slope of white FM. On flicker FM it reads high by an amount that fades once $M > r$, and on random-walk FM by a factor that settles there, where $B_3$ tends to 2/3 (Barnes and Allan (1990), Eq. (8)).

Correcting an Allan deviation

Give the readings to FrequencyData with the averaging time of one reading as tau0, and the time not measured between readings as dead_time. The noise type has to be stated. Here a flicker FM record is read for 16 s out of every 64 s:

using Random

y = noise_gen(FrequencyData, 2^17, 1.0; sigma1 = Dict(:FLFM => 1e-12),
              rng = MersenneTwister(7)).y
readings = FrequencyData([sum(@view y[i:i+15]) / 16 for i in 1:64:length(y)-15], 16.0)

ms = [1, 2, 4, 8, 16]
uncorrected = adev(readings, ms; ci = false).dev
corrected = adev(readings, ms; dead_time = 48, noise = :FLFM, ci = false).dev
whole = adev(FrequencyData(y, 1.0), 16 .* ms; ci = false).dev
println("   τ (s)   uncorrected     corrected  whole record")
for i in eachindex(ms)
    @printf("%8d  %12.3e  %12.3e  %12.3e\n", 16ms[i], uncorrected[i], corrected[i], whole[i])
end
   τ (s)   uncorrected     corrected  whole record
      16     1.170e-12     8.116e-13     8.372e-13
      32     9.929e-13     8.136e-13     8.312e-13
      64     9.056e-13     8.180e-13     8.240e-13
     128     8.792e-13     8.359e-13     8.133e-13
     256     8.321e-13     8.117e-13     8.122e-13

The result is $σ_y(τ)$ at $τ = m\,τ_0$, the time measured, not at the $m\,T_0$ that the readings span. On the command line the same correction is sigmatau analyze --freq --devs adev --tau0 16 --dead-time 48 --noise FLFM, where --tau0 has to be given: the time column of such a file holds the period $T_0$. In Python it is the dead_time keyword of st.adev.

Where the correction holds

The functions are those of the two-sample variance on one power-law noise type, and the correction is offered where that describes the data (SP1065 §5.15). Stable32 draws the same line: it corrects the normal and overlapping Allan deviations when the noise type is selected by hand (Stable32 manual, "Dead Time").

  • adev alone takes dead_time, in its overlapping and non-overlapped forms. No such functions exist for the modified, Hadamard, total or Thêo estimators, and deviation refuses the keyword for them. stability, sigmatau report and the explorer do not apply it.
  • The noise type is not identified. The slope of the uncorrected deviation is not that of the noise, as white PM shows above, and the lag-1 test assumes adjacent samples. State it with noise.
  • The factor is that of the stated type at each $τ$. A record with two noise types of comparable size is corrected for only one of them.
  • The interval is the uncorrected one scaled by the same factor. Its EDF is that of a record of the same length without dead time, for which no published correction exists, so take it as approximate.
  • A record with NaN gaps is refused, and so is phase data, which has no dead time.
  • Flicker PM shares $μ = -2$ with white PM, and the functions are those of white PM. In simulation ($2^{20}$ samples, readings of 8 or 16 samples, $r$ = 1.5, 4 and 36) the corrected deviation on flicker PM read 3 to 6 % high at $m = 1$ and 20 to 25 % low at $m = 16$. On white PM, white FM, flicker FM and random-walk FM it was within 1 % of the deviation of the whole record at every $m$ up to 16.

The functions describe ideal averages of a continuous process. A reading that spans a single step of a simulated record is not one: with one sample to a reading the same simulation gave a random-walk FM deviation 15 % low at $m = 1$.

Agreement with the published tables

The functions reproduce 2935 of 2958 entries read from the tables of the two notes to the four figures printed: $B_2$ complete, $B_1$ at $r$ = 0.001, 0.01, 1.01 and 2, and $B_3$ at $r$ = 0.01, 0.03, 1.01, 2 and 32. Fifteen entries differ by one unit in the last figure. Eleven of those are in the $B_2$ table of the 1969 note, at places where the 1990 note prints the value computed here.

The remaining eight are in the white PM row at $r = 0.01$. There an average starts exactly where the one 100 places before it ends, the two share a phase sample, and the published expressions give, for instance, $B_3(2, 128, 0.01, -2) = 150$. The tables print the value that holds at any other ratio, $M = 128$, and Barnes and Allan (1990), Table 1 lists $B_1(N, r, -2) = 1$ and $B_3(2, M, r, -2) = M$ without that exception. The functions return the value of the expressions. The case needs overlapping averages, $r = 1/k$, and does not arise with dead time.

Two printed values

SP1065, Fig. 24 gives $\sqrt{B_2} = 1.92$ for flicker FM at $r = 36$; the expression gives 1.915. Barnes and Allan (1990), Table 1 lists $B_1(N, 1, μ) = 1$ for $μ < 0$ and a sum over $n^μ$ for $μ > 0$. Those are the limits of $B_1$ at large $r$, not its values at $r = 1$, which the line above it in the table gives.

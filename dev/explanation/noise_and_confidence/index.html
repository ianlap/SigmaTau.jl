Noise, EDF and confidence

A deviation computed from a finite record is an estimate, and how far it can be trusted depends on the kind of noise in the clock. This page explains what SigmaTau does between the raw deviation and the interval it reports: τ grids, noise identification, equivalent degrees of freedom, χ² intervals and bias correction.

Phase, frequency and τ

A clock record is either phase (time error) x(t) in seconds or fractional frequency y(t), dimensionless, sampled every τ₀ seconds. They are two views of one signal: y is the slope of x, $y_k = (x_{k+1} - x_k)/\tau_0$. SigmaTau's estimators work on phase, so a FrequencyData record is integrated first.

A deviation is evaluated at averaging times τ = m·τ₀, where the integer m is the averaging factor. You choose the factors explicitly (adev(p, [1, 10, 100])) or through a named grid, a TauMode: Octave (powers of two, the default), HalfOctave, QuarterOctave, Decade, HalfDecade (steps of √10) or AllTaus. A grid stops at the largest m the estimator supports on the record length: tau_values shows the factors it gives.

Power-law noise

Clock noise is modelled as a sum of power laws in the frequency spectrum, $S_y(f) = \sum_\alpha h_\alpha f^\alpha$. Five values of α cover almost every oscillator:

LabelNoiseα in S_y(f) ∝ f^ασ_y²(τ) ∝ τ^μTypical source
:WHPMWhite phase modulation2μ = −2Measurement noise, short τ
:FLPMFlicker phase modulation1μ = −2Amplifiers and electronics
:WHFMWhite frequency modulation0μ = −1The floor of passive atomic standards
:FLFMFlicker frequency modulation−1μ = 0The flicker floor
:RWFMRandom-walk frequency modulation−2μ = 1Environment, long-term wander

The slope of σ_y(τ) on a log-log plot is μ/2, which is why the noise mix can be read off a stability plot; Choosing an estimator tabulates the slopes. noise_gen synthesizes records with any mix of these noises, which is how the examples on this site are built.

Synthesized noise

noise_gen draws each component with the Kasdin–Walter discrete fractional filter: white Gaussian noise convolved with the impulse response of $(1 - z^{-1})^{\alpha/2}$. It also covers the two steeper noises that the Hadamard-class estimators converge on, flicker-walk FM (:FWFM, α = −3) and random-run FM (:RRFM, α = −4). A component requested by h_α has the spectrum

\[S_y(f) = h_\alpha \left(\frac{\sin \pi f \tau_0}{\pi \tau_0}\right)^{\alpha},\]

which is $h_\alpha f^\alpha$ below about a tenth of the sampling rate and rolls off as $\sin$ toward Nyquist. For α ≤ −1 the process is not stationary; its d-th difference is, with spectrum $(2 \sin \pi f \tau_0)^{2d} S_y(f)$. The test suite generates 40 records per α from +2 to −4, differences where needed, and checks the averaged Welch spectrum against this formula bin by bin across the band: the band-average ratio is 1 to within 1 % and no bin is off by more than 12 %, the scatter expected of the estimate.

Noise identification

With ci=true (the default) every estimator except MTIE and TIE rms identifies the dominant noise type at each τ (identify_noise), because the interval depends on it. SigmaTau uses the lag-1 autocorrelation of the phase decimated by m, after a quadratic detrend of each decimated series, which is reliable while at least 30 averaged samples remain. Beyond that it falls back to the B1 and R(n) ratios. The result is always one of the five labels above: estimates outside the range are clamped to WHPM or RWFM, and a τ where the estimate fails takes the type of its nearest neighbour.

Stable32 does not detrend before the lag-1 estimate. The difference only matters on records with drift; identify_noise(...; remove_drift=false) follows Stable32, and Validation quantifies the effect.

Equivalent degrees of freedom

An estimated variance from a record with Gaussian noise follows a scaled χ² distribution. Its number of degrees of freedom, the EDF, measures how much independent information went into the estimate, and it depends on the estimator, the noise type, m and the record length N. It falls as τ grows, because a longer τ leaves fewer independent averages; that is why the last points of a stability plot carry wide intervals. SigmaTau computes it per τ (calculate_edf):

  • ADEV, MDEV, HDEV, MHDEV: the Greenhall–Riley algorithm (Greenhall and Riley, 2003), which evaluates the EDF exactly from the noise spectrum rather than from fitted approximations.
  • Total family: the published EDF coefficients of NIST SP1065 (Riley, Jul 2008) and Howe and Tasset, with the Greenhall–Riley value where no coefficients exist (white and flicker PM for TOTDEV and HTOTDEV). For MHTOTDEV, which is new in SigmaTau, the coefficients were measured by Monte Carlo; see MHTOTDEV bias and EDF.
  • Thêo1 and ThêoH: the empirical per-noise formulas of SP1065 Table 3.
  • PDEV: the PVAR model of Vernotte, Chen and Rubiola.
  • MTIE and TIE rms have no χ² model and carry no interval.

r.edf holds the values, and r.neff the number of terms actually averaged at each τ.

Confidence intervals

Given the deviation σ̂ and the EDF ν, the interval at confidence level p is

\[\sigma_\mathrm{lo} = \hat\sigma \sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\chi^2_{(1+p)/2}(\nu)}}, \qquad \sigma_\mathrm{hi} = \hat\sigma \sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\chi^2_{(1-p)/2}(\nu)}}\]

(confidence_intervals). The default level is 68.3 % (DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE), the ±1σ convention of Stable32; pass confidence=0.95 for a 95 % interval. The interval is asymmetric about σ̂, wider above than below, because the χ² distribution is skewed at small ν. Where the EDF is below 1 (a single average at the longest τ), SigmaTau falls back to a symmetric Gaussian interval floored at zero.

Bias correction

The total estimators and Thêo1 are biased for some noise types: their expected value differs from the true Allan-family variance by a known factor that depends on the noise and on τ/T. With correct_bias=true (the default) SigmaTau divides it out using the identified noise type (bias_correction):

EstimatorCorrection
TOTDEVHowe and Walter: τ/T-dependent, FLFM and RWFM only
MTOTDEV, TTOTDEVSP1065 Table 11 (MTOT overstates MVAR)
HTOTDEVHowe and Tasset, FM noises
MHTOTDEVmeasured by Monte Carlo, τ/T-dependent
Thêo1the ThêoBR ratio to ADEV, which needs no noise ID

correct_bias=false returns the raw statistic. Stable32 corrects TOTDEV and HTOTDEV the same way and reports MTOT and Thêo1 raw; the validation page shows both against Stable32.

Drift

A linear frequency drift D adds a term Dτ/√2 to ADEV, which grows as τ¹ and hides everything else at long τ. The Hadamard family takes a third difference and does not see it. Otherwise remove it before the analysis: detrend with method=:quadratic fits and removes the quadratic phase term, which is the frequency drift. MHTOTDEV removes it by default (remove_drift=true), because its reflected record would otherwise let it back in.

Bias correction

The total estimators and Thêo1 gain their degrees of freedom by reusing the record, and they pay for it with a bias: the expected value of each differs from that of the variance it stands in for, its parent, by a factor that depends on the noise type. The package divides the factor out by default. This page lists the factors and their sources, shows how well the corrected estimators agree with their parents, and gives the options that reproduce other conventions.

The factors

The bias factor is the ratio of expected values,

\[B = \frac{E[\text{total variance}]}{E[\text{parent variance}]} ,\]

and an estimator divides its deviation by $\sqrt{B}$. The parents are the Allan variance for totdev, the modified Allan variance for mtotdev and ttotdev, the Hadamard variance for htotdev and the modified Hadamard variance for mhtotdev. bias_correction returns $B$:

using SigmaTau, Printf

five = [:WHPM, :FLPM, :WHFM, :FLFM, :RWFM]
seven = [five; :FWFM; :RRFM]
function row(name, kind, m, noises)
    B = bias_correction(noises, kind, fill(m, length(noises)), 1025)
    println(rpad(name, 20), join(map(b -> @sprintf("%7.3f", b), B)))
end
println(rpad("", 20), join(lpad.(seven, 7)))
row("totdev, τ = T/8", :totvar, 128, five)
row("totdev, τ = T/2", :totvar, 512, five)
row("mtotdev, ttotdev", :mtot, 64, five)
row("htotdev", :htot, 64, seven)

SP1065 §5.12 lists modified total bias factors of 1.06 to 1.31 and describes them as the ratio of the expected modified total variance to the modified Allan variance. The estimator reads low, so that ratio is below 1, and those values are not used.

Averaging factors below 16

The published factors of the modified and Hadamard totals are stated for $τ ≥ 16τ_0$. Below that the expectation depends on the averaging factor, and the package divides by its exact value at each $m$ from 2 to 15, computed as on the page Total estimators on PM noise. For the modified total:

ms = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 16]
println(rpad("m", 6), join(lpad.(ms, 7)))
for n in five
    B = bias_correction(fill(n, length(ms)), :mtot, ms, 1025)
    println(rpad(n, 6), join(map(b -> @sprintf("%7.3f", b), B)))
end

The Hadamard total has the same rows two noise types down, from white FM to random-run FM. White PM dips at odd $m$, by 6 % at $m = 3$, and white FM is 6 % above its limit at $m = 2$. The two flicker rows are the mean of two flicker models, each within 2.4 % of it at $m = 2$ and within 1 % from $m = 5$.

At $m = 1$ nothing is approximate. The modified total variance is half the modified Allan variance on any record, and the modified Hadamard total is 124/225 of the modified Hadamard variance. The total and Hadamard total equal the Allan and Hadamard variances term for term.

Accuracy of the corrected estimators

In simulation (1024 points, 2000 records per noise type) with the noise type stated, the corrected variance of each of the four total estimators averages within 2 % of its parent up to $τ = T/8$ and within 4 % at the longest averaging times, on every noise type.

A default call identifies the noise type, and the factor follows the label. With 64 averaged points or more the result is the same within 2 %, and down to 8 averaged points within 5 %. With fewer the label is uncertain (Noise identification), and a record given the wrong label is divided by the wrong factor. Mean of the corrected variance over the parent variance at $τ = T/4$, with 4 averaged points left:

The modified Hadamard total is the least exposed, because its factors lie within 15 % of each other, and its 4 % on white PM is there with the noise type stated as well. Thêo1

Thêo1 is unbiased against the Allan variance on white FM only (Howe (2006), §4). theo1 removes the bias in the form called ThêoBR: it multiplies the Thêo1 variance by the mean ratio of the Allan variance to Thêo1 at the same averaging time, measured on the record itself at averaging times from $9τ_0$ up to half the record (SP1065 §5.2.16, Eq. (33)). No noise type is needed. theoh uses the corrected value above a fifth of the record and the Allan deviation below.

The method has three limits. The first two show in simulation on records of one noise type (1024 points, 2000 records per noise type).

It is one number. On PM noise the bias varies with $m$ throughout (Howe (2006), Table 3): on white PM the definition gives an expected value of $H_{m/2}$ times the Allan variance at the same $τ$, where $H_n$ is the $n$th harmonic number, 2.45 at $m = 12$ and 5.9 at $m = 400$. One ratio cannot follow it. On these records the corrected variance is 0.48 of the Allan variance at $9τ_0$ on white PM and 0.55 on flicker PM, right near a fifth of the record, and 1.2 times the Allan variance at $T/2$ on both. Do not use Thêo1 where PM noise dominates.

It is an estimate. The last terms of the mean are ratios at averaging times near half the record, where the Allan variance rests on a few differences, and each counts as much as the first. On FM noise the ratio scatters by 26 to 32 % in variance, at every record length tried from 256 to 16 384 points, and the corrected deviation carries that scatter at every averaging time. Its rms error is 14 to 15 % at $9τ_0$, where that of the Allan deviation is 5 to 7 %. It is the more precise of the two only from about a tenth of the record on: 22 to 34 % against 27 to 35 % at $T/5$, and 40 to 60 % against 62 to 64 % at $T/2$. The corrected variance averages 6 to 9 % below the Allan variance between $48τ_0$ and $T/5$, and 8 to 14 % above it at $T/2$. The interval includes the scatter of the ratio, through an EDF of its own (Confidence intervals).

It assumes one noise type. One ratio is applied at every averaging time. Its size depends on the noise type: in the fit of Howe (2006), Table 3, reprinted as SP1065 Table 12, it tends to 2.70 on random-walk FM and to 1.87 on flicker FM, is 1 on white FM, and falls with $m$ toward 0.14 on flicker PM and 0.09 on white PM. Where one noise type gives way to another within the record, the mean mixes the ratios of both, and the corrected values at the long averaging times come out low. The error grows toward the longest averaging times: where white PM gives way to flicker FM at $0.1T$ to $0.3T$, the corrected variance is 0.46 to 0.55 of the true one at $0.7T$. On such a record, take the long averaging times from totdev or htotdev, which reach half the record with a factor for the noise type at each averaging time.

The values in parentheses are those of correct_bias=:published, which averages the ratio up to a tenth of the record only. That is the range of the sum in the papers that define the method (Howe (2006), Eq. (6); Taylor and Howe (2010), Eq. (1)). SP1065 and IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.37) print half the record, the range that Stable32 uses and the default here. The two fail in different places. The shorter range measures the ratio where the first noise type holds and applies it where the second does, and the corrected variance falls to a quarter of the true one in the table. On a record of one FM noise type it is the better of the two: its ratio scatters by 7 to 14 %, the corrected variance averages within about 2 % of the Allan variance from $48τ_0$ up, and from there on the rms error of the deviation is below that of the Allan deviation, with 28 to 53 % at $T/2$.

correct_bias=:stable32 takes the default range on at most 3000 samples of the record, as Stable32 does (theo1 has the details). On records of up to 3000 samples it agreed with the default within 0.3 % in simulation. correct_bias=false returns Thêo1 as defined.

Other conventions

The factors derived in this package are in no other program, so correct_bias also takes the convention of each reference:

:published takes each correction as the paper that derived it prints it, :stable32 is what Stable32 applies to each estimator, and false is what allantools returns. The modified Hadamard total exists in neither program and has no published factor.

The setting changes the deviation and the interval that scales with it. It does not change the noise identification or the EDF, so where this package and Stable32 find a different noise type the factors still differ (Coming from Stable32). The command line names the four --bias exact, published, stable32 and none, the explorer lists them under Bias correction, and Python takes True, False, "published" and "stable32".

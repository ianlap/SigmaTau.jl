Clock ensembles

A timescale starts from several clocks read against one reference at the same instants: a counter with a channel per clock, or a switch that steps through them. ClockComparisons holds those readings, and simulate_ensemble makes a set with known true states, each clock from its own ClockModel, to check the algorithms against.

ms = [ClockModel(; tau0=1.0, q1=1e-24), ClockModel(; tau0=1.0, q1=4e-24, q2=1e-30)]
cc, truth = simulate_ensemble(ms, 86400)
adev(cc[2, 1])       # clock 2 − clock 1, free of the reference
SigmaTau.ClockComparisons — Type
ClockComparisons(x::Matrix, tau0; names, reference="")
ClockComparisons(records::Vector{<:PhaseData}; names, reference="")

Phase readings of several clocks against one reference, sampled together: column i of x is clock i minus the reference, in seconds, one row per sample, NaN where a reading is missing. The reference may be one of the clocks (a switch or counter measuring each against clock 1, whose column is then zero) or an outside one, named by reference.

comparisons[i] is clock i's record as PhaseData, and comparisons[i, j] the difference clock i − clock j, which no longer depends on the reference.

  • x::Matrix{Float64}: Phase of each clock against the reference (s), one column per clock.

  • tau0::Float64: Sample interval τ₀ in seconds.

  • names::Vector{String}: Clock names, one per column.

  • reference::String: What the clocks were read against.

SigmaTau.simulate_ensemble — Function
simulate_ensemble(models::Vector{ClockModel}, N; reference=1, rng=nothing)
    → (ClockComparisons, Array{Float64,3})

N samples of an ensemble whose clock i follows models[i], read against clock reference (or, with reference = 0, against a perfect outside reference). Each reading carries its clock's white PM R. Returns the comparisons and the true states, truth[k, s, i] for sample k, state s and clock i (phase, frequency, and drift for a model with drift; zero beyond a clock's own states). The models must share one τ₀.

Timescales

timescale averages the clocks' predicted phases into one scale. :ewfa gives every clock the same weight, the baseline any weighting has to beat. :at1 is the NIST algorithm as Weiss & Weissert describe it (Weiss and Weissert, 1991): it learns each clock's prediction-error variance and weights by its inverse, which on clocks of steady noise converges to the optimal inverse-variance weights. The equation numbers in the docstring of timescale are the paper's. Three choices are the paper's and can be swapped: the bias term K that makes up for each clock being part of the scale (bias=:paper, or the exact bias=:exact), no cap on the weights (wmax), and no outlier test (outlier). Each clock's frequency constant comes from its own τmin, which `tauminsets and which is otherwise read from the record. The paper leaves its starting values open, so these are this package's choices: the τ_min estimate, the starting variance τ²σ_y²(τ) taken from the record's first part, each clock's starting frequency (its fitted slope), and a variance memory of 20 days (memory) capped at a quarter of the record. [timescale_phase`](@ref) returns the scale against the reference, for the usual stability analysis.

r = timescale(cc; algorithm=:at1)
r.weights[end, :]                     # each clock's weight now
adev(timescale_phase(r))              # the scale's stability

AT1's weights are optimal for the interval between readings, since that is what each clock's prediction error measures; at much longer averaging times, with clocks whose long-term behaviour differs from their short-term, the scale can be worse than the best clock.

:rkt is the reduced Kalman timescale. One Kalman filter tracks every clock's phase and frequency from the clock models; after each update the phase part of its covariance is reduced about a weighted mean of the corrected clocks, which keeps the frequency estimates and makes each step of the scale as small as it can be. Unlike AT1 it needs a model per clock, and it uses each clock's white PM level R.

timescale(cc; algorithm=:rkt, models=ms)
SigmaTau.timescale — Function
timescale(cc::ClockComparisons; algorithm=:at1, …) → TimescaleResult

Form a timescale from the clocks in cc. At each sample every clock's phase against the scale is predicted from the last one and its estimated frequency, and the scale is placed so that the weighted prediction errors sum to zero. A clock without a reading keeps its prediction and drops out of the average for that sample; a clock joins at its first reading. Each clock's frequency starts at its least-squares slope over the first 10 % of the record, less the mean slope.

  • :ewfa, equal-weight frequency averaging: every clock weighs the same. Each clock's frequency is an exponential average of its phase steps over tau_y seconds (default 100 τ₀). With every clock read at every sample the scale is the plain mean of the clocks' phases.
  • :at1, the NIST scale as Weiss & Weissert describe it (Weiss and Weissert, 1991) (equation numbers are the paper's): weights $w_i = ε_x²/ε_i²$ (3), with $ε_x² = (Σ 1/ε_i²)⁻¹$ (4), where $ε_i²$ is an exponential average (7) of the squared prediction error estimate $ε̂_i = |x̂_i − x_i| + K_i$ (5), the bias $K_i = 2ε_x²/√(2πε_i²)$ (6) making up for each clock being part of the scale. Each clock's frequency is filtered (9) with its own constant from its τ_min (10).
  • :rkt, the reduced Kalman timescale in Greenhall's IEMx form (Greenhall, 2003; Greenhall, 2006): one Kalman filter over every clock's phase and frequency (and drift), with one ClockModel per clock in models, whose R is the clock's white PM against the reference. After each update the phase part of the covariance is reduced about the implicit ensemble mean, a weighted mean of the corrected clocks; this keeps the frequency estimates and minimizes each step of the scale, so the scale is quiet at short τ as well as long. The weights are those of that mean. The scale's frequency against the reference settles at a constant set by the start, which comes from a line through each clock's first samples (Greenhall, 2007).

AT1 keywords:

  • tau_min: each clock's τ_min in seconds, a number or one per clock; the paper takes it from the clock's characterization. Default: the lag of the smallest ADEV of each clock, estimated from the record (the reference's share taken out).
  • memory: the time constant of the variance average in seconds, the paper's $N_τ$ (20 days for cesium clocks, and the default; at most a quarter of the record).
  • bias: :paper (default) for $K_i$ above, or :exact for the squared error divided by $1 − w_i$, which is exact when the clocks' own errors are independent and the weights optimal.
  • wmax (default 1, no cap): the largest weight any clock may have.
  • outlier (default Inf, off): a clock whose prediction error passes outlier ε_i is left out of that sample and of its variance; if that would leave none, all stay.

The weights are optimal for the interval of the readings: ε_i² is the clock's prediction error over one interval, so for τ well beyond it, and with clocks of different long-term behaviour, the scale can be worse than the best clock. With two clocks the comparisons alone cannot tell which is better, and the weights wander toward either; cap them with wmax or use EWFA. The frequency drift term of the paper's prediction (1) is not used.

SigmaTau.TimescaleResult — Type
TimescaleResult

Output of timescale.

  • algorithm::Symbol: Algorithm that formed the scale (:ewfa, :at1, :rkt).

  • comparisons::ClockComparisons: The clocks the scale was formed from.

  • t::Vector{Float64}: Sample times in seconds from the first sample.

  • x::Matrix{Float64}: Each clock minus the scale (s), one column per clock.

  • error::Matrix{Float64}: Each clock's prediction error (s); NaN where it had no reading.

SigmaTau.timescale_phase — Function
timescale_phase(r::TimescaleResult) → PhaseData

The scale against the comparisons' reference: reference − clock i plus clock i − scale, the same for every clock read at that sample (NaN where none was). Its stability is the scale's, as far as the reference is better than it; against an ideal reference (as from simulate_ensemble with reference = 0) it is exact.

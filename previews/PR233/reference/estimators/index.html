Estimators

Every estimator takes a PhaseData or FrequencyData record and has three call forms:

adev(data)                    # octave τ grid up to the estimator's limit
adev(data, Decade)            # a TauMode grid; see Suites, τ grids and streaming
adev(data, [1, 10, 100])      # explicit averaging factors m, τ = m·τ₀ (a range works too)

They return a StabilityResult. The common keywords are

  • ci=true: identify the noise type and compute the EDF and confidence interval at each τ. ci=false returns the deviation and neff only, and is faster (with correct_bias=true the total estimators still identify the noise for the bias correction).
  • confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE (0.683): the interval's confidence level.
  • correct_bias=true (total family and theo1 only): divide out the estimator's known bias. :published keeps to the published tables, :stable32 does what Stable32 does for that estimator, and false returns the raw statistic, as allantools does; see Noise, EDF and confidence.

Choosing an estimator compares them.

Allan and Hadamard

SigmaTau.adev — Function
adev(data, m_values; ci=true, overlapping=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Overlapping Allan deviation σ_y(τ) of a phase record, per IEEE 1139-2022 §C.2. The EDF for the χ² confidence interval uses the truncated-sum algorithm of Greenhall & Riley 2003.

overlapping=false gives the original non-overlapped Allan deviation (NIST SP1065 §5.2.2 (Riley, Jul 2008); Stable32's "Normal" Allan, allantools' adev): one second difference every m samples, with the EDF of the same algorithm for non-overlapped samples. It estimates the same σ_y(τ) with fewer degrees of freedom, and needs a gap-free record.

m_values selects the analysis-interval factors (τ = m·τ₀). When ci=true, the result populates per-τ noise type, χ²-based confidence bounds, and equivalent degrees of freedom.

noise states the noise type in place of identifying it from the record (:auto): one type for every τ (noise=:WHFM), a vector with one per averaging factor, or a PowerLawFit, whose strongest component at each τ is taken. It sets the EDF and, in the total family, the bias factor, and every estimator that reports intervals takes it. State the noise when the labels do not fit what is known of the clock: the test reads each τ on its own, and on a measured record a slow periodic or wandering term can mislead it where few averaged points remain.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData(collect(1.0:10.0), 1.0);

julia> r = adev(p, [1, 2]; ci=false);

julia> round.(r.dev; sigdigits=4)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.0

SigmaTau.tdev — Function
tdev(data, m_values; ci=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Time deviation σx(τ) = (τ/√3) · Mod σy(τ), per IEEE 1139-2022 §C.4. A σ_x quantity in seconds. Confidence bounds are MDEV's scaled by the same factor. TDEV is the usual measure of time stability for time transfer and network timing; for drifting clocks use htdev.

Like every estimator, tdev also takes FrequencyData, a TauMode grid in place of m_values (tdev(data, Decade)), or no grid at all (tdev(data), octave-spaced up to the estimator's limit). See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

source
SigmaTau.hdev — Function
hdev(data, m_values; ci=true, remove_drift=true, overlapping=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Overlapping Hadamard deviation of a phase record, per NIST SP1065 §5.2.9. The third difference removes linear frequency drift, so hdev is the estimator to use on rubidium, quartz or any clock with a frequency drift that would turn ADEV into a τ¹ ramp. It cannot separate white from flicker PM; use mhdev for that.

remove_drift=true (default) identifies the noise type at each τ on the record less its least-squares frequency drift, so a drift the statistic ignores cannot bias the EDF; the deviation itself does not change. On that record the B1 ratio of identify_noise is not used: with fewer than 30 averaged points an FM type is the one identified at the longest τ that has 30, so a change from one FM type to another beyond it needs noise. remove_drift=false identifies it on the record as given, as Stable32 does.

overlapping=false gives the non-overlapped Hadamard deviation (SP1065 §5.2.8; Stable32's "Normal" Hadamard, allantools' hdev), one third difference every m samples, as adev does.

Like every estimator, hdev also takes FrequencyData, a TauMode grid in place of m_values (hdev(data, Decade)), or no grid at all (hdev(data), octave-spaced up to the estimator's limit). See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

source
SigmaTau.mhdev — Function
mhdev(data, m_values; ci=true, remove_drift=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Modified Hadamard deviation of a phase record: the phase-averaged third difference, drift-immune like hdev and able to separate white PM from flicker PM like mdev. Not defined by SP1065; the EDF uses the Greenhall–Riley algorithm with the MHDEV filter. remove_drift works as in hdev.

Like every estimator, mhdev also takes FrequencyData, a TauMode grid in place of m_values (mhdev(data, Decade)), or no grid at all (mhdev(data), octave-spaced up to the estimator's limit). See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

SigmaTau.htdev — Function
htdev(data, m_values; ci=true, remove_drift=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Hadamard time deviation σx,HT(τ) = τ/√(10/3) · σy,MH(τ), the σx form of mhdev in seconds, as TDEV is of MDEV. The construction is original to this package; SP1065, IEEE 1139-2022 and NBS TN-1337 do not define it. Use it in place of tdev when the clock drifts. Confidence bounds are MHDEV's scaled by the same factor, and `removedrift` passes through to it.

Like every estimator, htdev also takes FrequencyData, a TauMode grid in place of m_values (htdev(data, Decade)), or no grid at all (htdev(data), octave-spaced up to the estimator's limit). See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

SigmaTau.pdev — Function
pdev(data, m_values; ci=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Parabolic deviation σ_PDEV(τ) of a phase record, per Vernotte–Lenczner– Bourgeois–Rubiola (IEEE T-UFFC 63(4), 2016) and Vernotte–Chen–Rubiola 2020. PDEV is built from a least-squares parabolic fit and is the recommended estimator when evaluating the uncertainty of an ω-averaged frequency measurement. At τ = τ₀ (i.e. m = 1) PDEV reduces to overlapping ADEV.

When ci=true, the result populates per-τ noise type, EDF, and χ²-based confidence bounds using the PVAR EDF model of Vernotte–Chen–Rubiola 2020 (arXiv:2005.13631), ν ≈ 35 / (A(α)·(m/M) − 12·(m/M)²) with M = N − 2m. At m = 1 the EDF is ADEV's (the PVAR(τ₀) ≡ AVAR(τ₀) identity); the large-τ region (m ≳ N/4) uses the paper's semi-log interpolation down to ν = 1 at m = N/2. The approximation is accurate to ≈ ±10 % except at m = 2, where it reads high. PVAR is an unbiased wavelet variance, so no bias correction is applied.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData(collect(1.0:10.0), 1.0);

julia> r = pdev(p, [1, 2]; ci=false);

julia> round.(r.dev; sigdigits=4)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.0

Total

SigmaTau.totdev — Function
totdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Total deviation of a phase record, per NIST SP1065 eq. 25 (Howe 1995), with the whole-record mean-flip extension.

correct_bias=true (default) applies the noise-type-dependent unbias correction σ_unbiased = σ_raw / √B, where B = E[TOTVAR]/AVAR is the variance-scale bias factor from bias_correction. As τ approaches T, TOTVAR reads low on FLFM and RWFM (SP1065) and high on white and flicker PM (see "Total estimators on PM noise" in the docs). correct_bias=:published or :stable32 applies the FM correction and none on PM, as SP1065 and Stable32 do; false returns the raw kernel value, which is what allantools reports.

source
SigmaTau.mtotdev — Function
mtotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Modified total deviation of a phase record, per NIST SP1065 §5.2.12, with the per-subsegment detrend and time-reverse extension of Howe and Vernotte.

correct_bias=true (default) applies the Howe and Peppler (2001, Table 2) correction σ_unbiased = σ_raw / √B, where B = 1 + a ∈ {0.995, 0.851, 0.771, 0.717, 0.679} for α ∈ {2, 1, 0, -1, -2}. MTOT is biased low (a < 0), so the correction raises σ, by 0.3 % for white PM and 21 % for random-walk FM. The table holds from τ = 16 τ₀; below it the exact factor at each m is used. At m = 1 the estimator is exactly MDEV/√2 for any record, and B = 1/2. correct_bias=:published applies the table at every m > 1; false or :stable32 returns the raw kernel, which is what Stable32 and allantools report.

ttotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

SigmaTau.htotdev — Function
htotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, remove_drift=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Hadamard total deviation of a phase record, per NIST SP1065 §5.2.14, with the per-subsegment detrend and time-reverse extension of Howe et al. 2000.

correct_bias=true (default) applies the Howe and Peppler (2001, Table 3) unbias correction σ_unbiased = σ_raw / √B, where B = 1 + a ∈ {0.995, 0.851, 0.771, 0.717, 0.679} for α ∈ {0, -1, -2, -3, -4}. HTOT is biased low (a < 0) on FM, so the correction raises σ, substantially for divergent FM. Those values hold from m = 16; below it the exact factor at each m is used, where Stable32 keeps the published one. On white and flicker PM it reads high, by 25 to 38 % and 4 to 28 % in variance, and the factor is its exact expectation (see "Total estimators on PM noise" in the docs). At m = 1 the estimator is plain overlapping HDEV and B = 1. correct_bias=:published or :stable32 applies the published FM factor at every m > 1 and none on PM, as Stable32 does; false returns the raw kernel value, which is what allantools reports.

remove_drift works as in hdev. Here the noise type also selects the bias factor, so the keyword can change σ where the two labels differ.

source
SigmaTau.mhtotdev — Function
mhtotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, remove_drift=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Modified Hadamard total deviation of a phase record: the total (boundary- extended) form of mhdev. Each window of 4m samples has its own least-squares quadratic removed before the time-reverse extension, so the estimator is drift-immune like MHDEV and converges for every noise type MHDEV does, down to random-run FM (α = −4). Original to this package.

remove_drift=true (default) also removes the record's least-squares frequency drift before the analysis, so the per-τ noise identification runs on the drift-removed record; the deviation itself does not change. Pass remove_drift=false to identify noise on the record as given.

correct_bias=true (default) divides out the statistic's exact bias, σ_unbiased = σ_raw / √B with B from bias_correction(…, :mhtot, …) (B = 0.78 to 0.90: the per-window detrend absorbs part of the variance). Pass correct_bias=false for the raw kernel value; :published and :stable32 change nothing here, since no reference defines the estimator. The EDF model is fitted to the exact EDF of the statistic; see the "MHTOTDEV bias and EDF" page for both.

Thêo1 and ThêoH

SigmaTau.theo1 — Function
theo1(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Thêo1 deviation per NIST SP1065 (Riley 2008) §5.2.15 eq. 30, a two-sample variance that mimics the Allan variance while extending the usable averaging-time range to 75 % of the record span (see _theo1_core for the kernel).

Conventions (SP1065 / Stable32):

  • m_values are Thêo1 averaging factors and must be even with 2 ≤ m ≤ N − 1; other entries produce NaN rows. SP1065 states the statistic for m ≥ 10. Use tau_values(mode, N, :theo1) for an even-rounded grid.
  • The reported r.tau is the effective averaging time τ = 0.75·m·τ0 (SP1065 §5.2.15), as plotted by Stable32. allantools' theo1 computes the same deviation values (eq. 30 with the Howe & Peppler 0.75 normalization, which SP1065's printed eq. 30 omits, although its own Table 2 requires it) but reports them at τ = m·τ0; SigmaTau resolves that definitional offset in favor of SP1065/Stable32, so r.dev matches allantools at the same m while r.tau is allantools' τ scaled by 0.75.
  • correct_bias=true (default) applies the ThêoBR automatic bias removal (Howe, 2006): the Thêo1 variance is multiplied by the average of the AVAR(m=9+3i)/Theo1(m=12+4i) ratios over i = 0…⌊N/30 − 3⌋ (matched-τ ladders over the first tenth of the record), removing the noise-dependent Thêo1 bias without explicit noise identification. SP1065 eq. 33 prints ⌊N/6 − 3⌋, a noisier and biased ladder (see _theobr_ratio). On long ladders the ratio computes Thêo1 at 48 of the factors and interpolates the rest, within 0.5 % of the full average (see _theobr_ratio); this takes it from O(N³) to about 1 s at N = 65 536. ThêoBR needs N ≥ 19 samples; shorter records yield NaN rows. correct_bias=false or :stable32 gives raw Thêo1, which Stable32 reports as its "Theo1" run; :published is ThêoBR.

The EDF for the χ²-based CI uses the empirical per-noise-type Thêo1 formulas of SP1065 §5.2.15 Table 3 (with r = 0.75m), for both the raw and bias-removed forms. The kernel's double sum is O(N·m) per τ, far costlier than the O(N) Allan kernels at large m.

source
SigmaTau.theoh — Function
theoh(data, m_values; ci=true, noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

ThêoH ("hybrid-ThêoBR") per NIST SP1065 (Riley 2008) §5.2.16 eq. 34: a composite σ_y(τ) curve that is overlapping ADEV at short averaging times and the bias-removed Thêo1 (ThêoBR, see theo1) beyond, extending a single continuous stability plot from τ0 out to 75 % of the record span.

m_values are τ-grid factors (target τ = m·τ0, any positive integers; unlike theo1 they need not be even). Each point is assigned per eq. 34's crossover k = 20 % of the record span T = (N−1)·τ0:

  • m·τ0 ≤ k: overlapping ADEV at factor m; reported at τ = m·τ0.
  • m·τ0 > k: ThêoBR at the even Thêo1 factor nearest 4m/3 (exact whenever m is divisible by 3); reported at its true effective τ = 0.75·m_theo·τ0.

r.tau therefore always holds the realized τ of each point, which on the Thêo1 segment can differ from the requested m·τ0 by up to one even-factor rounding step. The ThêoBR scaling needs N ≥ 19; shorter records yield NaN on the Thêo1 segment. CIs use the ADEV (Greenhall–Riley) EDF on the ADEV segment and the SP1065 Table 3 Thêo1 EDF on the Thêo1 segment. ThêoBR/ThêoH always carry the bias removal; there is no correct_bias switch (raw Thêo1 is available via theo1(...; correct_bias=false)).

Time interval error

SigmaTau.mtie — Function
mtie(data, m_values; ci=true, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Maximum time interval error (MTIE) of a phase record, per ITU-T G.810. For each averaging factor m, returns the largest peak-to-peak phase excursion observed in any sliding window of m+1 samples (spanning τ = m·τ₀).

MTIE is a σx quantity (units of seconds), reported as a single deterministic envelope rather than a statistical σ; there is no published EDF / χ² confidence model for it, so `noisetype,ci, andedfare returned empty even whenci=true(neffis still populated).ciandconfidence` are accepted for API uniformity with the other deviations but are no-ops here.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData([0.0, 1.0, 0.5, 2.0, 1.5], 1.0);

julia> r = mtie(p, [1, 3]);

julia> r.dev
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.5
 2.0

SigmaTau.tierms — Function
tierms(data, m_values; ci=true, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

RMS time interval error (TIE rms) of a phase record, per NIST SP1065 §5.2.18 eq. 37 (Riley, Jul 2008). For each averaging factor m, returns the root-mean-square phase change over all N − m spans of τ = m·τ₀:

\[\mathrm{TIE\,rms}(m\tau_0) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-m} \sum_{i=1}^{N-m} \bigl(x_{i+m} - x_i\bigr)^2}\]

This is the estimator Stable32 and allantools (tierms) compute. TIE rms is a σ_x quantity (units of seconds) used by the telecommunications industry alongside MTIE; for a record with no frequency offset it approaches the standard deviation of the fractional-frequency fluctuations multiplied by τ, similar in behavior to TDEV.

Like mtie, TIE rms has no published EDF / χ² confidence model, so noise_type, ci, and edf are returned empty even when ci=true (neff carries the N − m span count). ci and confidence are accepted for API uniformity with the other deviations but are no-ops here.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData([0.0, 1.0, 0.5, 2.0, 1.5], 1.0);

julia> r = tierms(p, [1, 3]);

julia> round.(r.dev; sigdigits=4)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.9682
 1.458

Telecom masks

The ITU-T recommendations specify a clock's or an interface's wander as limits on MTIE and TDEV over the observation interval τ. mask_check compares an mtie or tdev result with one; sigmatau analyze --mask and the explorer's Stability page draw the same limits. Measure the way the recommendations do: phase against a reference better than the mask, sampled at 1/30 s or faster through a 10 Hz low-pass filter.

SigmaTau.TELECOM_MASKS — Constant

Names of the telecom wander masks telecom_mask and mask_check know, each with its MTIE and TDEV limit:

  • :G811: primary reference clock, ITU-T G.811 (ITU-T, Sep 1997).
  • :G813_opt1, :G813_opt2: SDH equipment clock (SEC), ITU-T G.813 (ITU-T, Mar 2003), options 1 (2048 kbit/s networks) and 2 (1544 kbit/s).
  • :G8262_opt1, :G8262_opt2: synchronous Ethernet equipment clock (EEC), ITU-T G.8262 (ITU-T, Oct 2024); the same limits as G.813.
  • :G823_PRC, :G823_SSU, :G823_SEC, :G823_PDH: ITU-T G.823 network limits for wander at PRC, SSU, SEC and PDH synchronization interfaces (ITU-T, Mar 2000).

The clock masks are wander generation in the locked mode at constant temperature; the recommendations measure through a 10 Hz first-order low-pass filter, sampling at 1/30 s or faster.

source

telecom_mask(mask, stat, τ) → limit in seconds

The limit of mask (one of TELECOM_MASKS) for stat (:mtie or :tdev) at observation interval τ in seconds, or a vector of limits for a vector of τ. NaN outside the τ range the recommendation covers.

SigmaTau.mask_check — Function
mask_check(r::StabilityResult, mask) → (; mask, tau, limit, pass, margin)

Compare an mtie or tdev result with a telecom mask (one of TELECOM_MASKS). limit[i] is the mask at r.tau[i] (NaN where the mask does not reach). pass is true when every value is at or under its limit, and margin is the smallest limit / value: above 1 passes, and its reciprocal is how far a failing record is over. Dynamic (time-resolved)

dadev and dhdev slide a window across the record and return σ(t, τ) as a DynamicStabilityResult. Plotting shows how to draw the map.

SigmaTau.dadev — Function
dadev(data, m_values=Octave; window, step=window÷2) → DynamicStabilityResult

Dynamic Allan deviation σy(t, τ), a time-resolved stability map (Galleani & Tavella 2009; McKelvy et al. 2025 (McKelvy et al., 2025)). An analysis window `W(tc)ofwindowphase samples slides across the record in increments ofstep` samples, and the overlapping Allan deviation is computed inside each window:

\[\sigma_y^2(t_c, \tau) = \frac{1}{2(N_w - 2m)(m\tau_0)^2} \sum_{i \in W(t_c)} (x_{i+2m} - 2x_{i+m} + x_i)^2 .\]

m_values selects the averaging factors (τ = m·τ₀) shared by every window; entries the window cannot support (window − 2m < 2) yield NaN columns. Pass a TauMode instead to get a grid clamped to the window length (tau_values(mode, window, :adev)), not the record length. The result's rows are windows (centered at the times in t), columns are τ. window defaults to the largest power of two up to N ÷ 8 (at least 16 samples) and step to half the window; with no m_values the octave grid is used, so dadev(pd) gives a map with about fifteen windows.

No confidence intervals are produced: the literature gives no EDF model for the time-resolved map (each window's value alone is an ordinary ADEV with window samples; use adev on a slice when a CI is needed). Each window is evaluated by a full _adev_core call on a copied buffer, so runtime scales with the window count: halving step doubles the cost.

source
SigmaTau.dhdev — Function
dhdev(data, m_values=Octave; window, step=window÷2) → DynamicStabilityResult

Dynamic Hadamard deviation: the same sliding-window construction as dadev with the overlapping third-difference (Hadamard) kernel inside each window, so the map rejects linear frequency drift within each window (a drifting clock shows its noise floor, not its drift ramp). Entries the window cannot support (window − 3m < 2) yield NaN columns. Pass a TauMode for a grid clamped to the window length (tau_values(mode, window, :hdev)).

Like dadev, no confidence intervals are produced; there is no published EDF model for the time-resolved map.

Clock ensembles

SigmaTau.ncornered_hat — Function
ncornered_hat(pairwise::AbstractMatrix{StabilityResult}) → Vector{StabilityResult}
ncornered_hat(r_ab, r_bc, r_ca) → Vector{StabilityResult}

N-cornered-hat noise separation (the classic Gray–Allan three-cornered hat at N = 3; see NIST SP1065 §10.7 (Riley, Jul 2008)). Given the pairwise deviations between every pair of N ≥ 3 clocks, estimates each clock's individual deviation under the assumption that the clock noises are statistically independent, so the measured pairwise variances satisfy s²ᵢⱼ(τ) = σ²ᵢ(τ) + σ²ⱼ(τ).

With Rᵢ = Σ_{j≠i} s²ᵢⱼ (clock i's row sum) and S = Σ_{j<k} s²ⱼₖ (the sum over all pairs), the separation is

\[\hat\sigma^2_i(\tau) = \frac{1}{N-2}\left( R_i - \frac{S}{N-1} \right)\]

At N = 3 this is the classic three-cornered hat, σ̂²_A = ½(s²_AB + s²_AC − s²_BC).

pairwise[i, j] (for i < j; the transposed entry is also accepted, so fill either triangle, or both) is the deviation computed on the difference record between clocks i and j. All entries must share the same deviation_type and the same τ grid; otherwise an ArgumentError is thrown. The 3-argument convenience form takes the three pairwise results in the cyclic order A−B, B−C, C−A and returns [A, B, C] (the orientation of each difference record does not matter).

Returned conventions (one StabilityResult per clock, in clock order):

  • deviation_type is kept from the inputs.
  • Non-positive variance estimates (common when one clock dominates or the independence assumption fails) are reported as NaN in dev rather than clamped to zero.
  • When every input carries edf (computed with ci=true), edf and ci are filled at the inputs' confidence level. Each pairwise variance is χ² with its own edf; two pairs that share clock c covary by 2σ_c⁴/√(ν₁ν₂). Propagating these through the separation gives the variance of each σ̂²ᵢ, reported as the equivalent edf = 2σ̂⁴ᵢ / Var(σ̂²ᵢ) (the covariance model of (Vernotte and Lantz, 2019)). The intervals widen sharply for a clock much quieter than the others.
  • noise_type is empty; neff is the elementwise minimum over the inputs.

See also groslambert, which needs the difference records rather than their deviations, never goes negative on average, and tolerates noise common to two measurement channels.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> x1, x2, x3 = randn(256), randn(256), randn(256);

julia> r(a, b) = adev(PhaseData(a .- b, 1.0), [1, 2, 4]);

SigmaTau.groslambert — Function
groslambert(pairwise::AbstractMatrix{<:AbstractTimingData}, m_values=Octave;
            confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE, interval=:chi2) → Vector{StabilityResult}
groslambert(x_ab, x_bc, x_ca, m_values=Octave; confidence, interval) → Vector{StabilityResult}

Groslambert covariance (Vernotte et al., IEEE Trans. UFFC 63, 2016): each clock's Allan deviation from the cross Allan variance of two difference records that share it. For clock i and two others j, k, the second differences of x_i − x_j and x_i − x_k have only clock i in common, so

σ²_i(τ) = ⟨Δ²(x_i − x_j) · Δ²(x_i − x_k)⟩ / (2τ²),

averaged over every pair (j, k) of the other clocks. Unlike the ncornered_hat it does not subtract variances, so its estimates scatter about the true value rather than turning negative for a clock much quieter than the rest, and noise shared by two measurement channels enters only the clock both channels see.

pairwise[i, j] is the difference record x_i − x_j (PhaseData or FrequencyData, same τ₀ and length throughout); the transposed entry, negated, stands in where one is missing. The 3-argument form takes the records A−B, B−C, C−A and returns [A, B, C].

Each result is an :adev StabilityResult with an EDF from the covariance model of (Vernotte and Lantz, 2019): each record's Allan variance carries its EDF for the identified noise type (Greenhall and Riley, 2003) (as in adev), the products of Gaussian second differences give the variance of every covariance estimate in closed form, and edf = 2σ̂⁴/Var(σ̂²) sets a χ² interval ci (none below 1 EDF, where the χ² model degenerates). A non-positive covariance gives a NaN estimate.

The χ² interval holds for a clock that dominates its two records. For a clock quieter than the others it is too narrow once the clock is no longer resolved, with its lower bound above the truth: in a Monte Carlo with one clock at a third of the others' level, its 95 % interval covered that clock's true value 94 % of the time at 10 τ₀ and 60 % at 100 τ₀.

With three clocks, interval = :klts gives the KLTS interval of (Lantz et al., 2019) instead: the quantiles of the posterior of the three clock variances given the sample covariance of the three records, under a log-uniform prior from 10⁻⁵ times the smallest to 10³ times the largest estimate, with the measurement noise read from the closure of the records and the geometric mean of their EDF. It is given also where the estimate is negative, and a lower bound of 0 means the data do not exclude zero (the bound would otherwise follow the prior's lower limit). In the same Monte Carlo it covered 92 % at 10 τ₀, 86 % at 20 τ₀, where the clock is barely resolved and the upper bound follows the prior, and 99–100 % from 50 τ₀ on. Each τ costs about 0.1 s.

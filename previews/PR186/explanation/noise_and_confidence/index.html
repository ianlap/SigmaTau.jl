Noise, EDF and confidence

A deviation computed from a finite record is an estimate, and how far it can be trusted depends on the kind of noise in the clock. This page explains what SigmaTau does between the raw deviation and the interval it reports: τ grids, noise identification, equivalent degrees of freedom, χ² intervals and bias correction.

Phase, frequency and τ

A clock record is either phase (time error) x(t) in seconds or fractional frequency y(t), dimensionless, sampled every τ₀ seconds. They are two views of one signal: y is the slope of x, $y_k = (x_{k+1} - x_k)/\tau_0$. SigmaTau's estimators work on phase, so a FrequencyData record is integrated first.

A deviation is evaluated at averaging times τ = m·τ₀, where the integer m is the averaging factor. You choose the factors explicitly (adev(p, [1, 10, 100])) or through a named grid, a TauMode: Octave (powers of two, the default), HalfOctave, QuarterOctave, Decade, HalfDecade (steps of √10) or AllTaus. A grid stops at the largest m the estimator supports on the record length: tau_values shows the factors it gives.

Power-law noise

Clock noise is modelled as a sum of power laws in the frequency spectrum, $S_y(f) = \sum_\alpha h_\alpha f^\alpha$. Five values of α cover almost every oscillator:

LabelNoiseα in S_y(f) ∝ f^ασ_y²(τ) ∝ τ^μTypical source
:WHPMWhite phase modulation2μ = −2Measurement noise, short τ
:FLPMFlicker phase modulation1μ = −2Amplifiers and electronics
:WHFMWhite frequency modulation0μ = −1The floor of passive atomic standards
:FLFMFlicker frequency modulation−1μ = 0The flicker floor
:RWFMRandom-walk frequency modulation−2μ = 1Environment, long-term wander

The slope of σ_y(τ) on a log-log plot is μ/2, which is why the noise mix can be read off a stability plot; Choosing an estimator tabulates the slopes. noise_gen synthesizes records with any mix of these noises, which is how the examples on this site are built.

Synthesized noise

noise_gen draws each component with the Kasdin–Walter discrete fractional filter: white Gaussian noise convolved with the impulse response of $(1 - z^{-1})^{\alpha/2}$. It also covers the two steeper noises that the Hadamard-class estimators converge on, flicker-walk FM (:FWFM, α = −3) and random-run FM (:RRFM, α = −4). A component requested by h_α has the spectrum

\[S_y(f) = h_\alpha \left(\frac{\sin \pi f \tau_0}{\pi \tau_0}\right)^{\alpha},\]

which is $h_\alpha f^\alpha$ below about a tenth of the sampling rate and rolls off as $\sin$ toward Nyquist. For α ≤ −1 the process is not stationary; its d-th difference is, with spectrum $(2 \sin \pi f \tau_0)^{2d} S_y(f)$. The test suite generates 40 records per α from +2 to −4, differences where needed, and checks the averaged Welch spectrum against this formula bin by bin across the band: the band-average ratio is 1 to within 1 % and no bin is off by more than 12 %, the scatter expected of the estimate.

Noise identification

With ci=true (the default) every estimator except MTIE and TIE rms identifies the dominant noise type at each τ (identify_noise), because the interval depends on it. SigmaTau uses the lag-1 autocorrelation of the phase decimated by m, after a quadratic detrend of each decimated series, which is reliable while at least 30 averaged samples remain. Beyond that it falls back to the B1 and R(n) ratios (SP1065 §5.5.2 (Riley, Jul 2008)). B1 is the ratio of the standard variance of the averaged frequency to its Allan variance, and it is compared with the B1(N, μ) of each noise type at the number of averaged points N, the length the ratio is defined for. With 8 to 24 points no test separates neighbouring noise types better than about two times in three, so labels at the longest τ are weak evidence; b1_length=:record gives the labels closer to Stable32's, which almost never include random-walk FM there. Below 6 averaged points the ratio says almost nothing, and SigmaTau reports the label measured at the longest averaging factor that still has 6; that value comes from simulation, not from the literature. Decimation whitens flicker PM: its lag-1 correlation fades as m grows, and from m ≈ 16 the test reads it as white PM, whose ADEV has several times the EDF. A white-PM result is therefore confirmed with R(n), the ratio of MVAR to AVAR that SP1065 §5.5.2 uses to separate the two PM noises, and becomes flicker PM when R(n) says so. The result is always one of the five labels above, or for the Hadamard family one of seven (see below): estimates outside the range are clamped to its ends, and a τ where the estimate fails takes the type of its nearest neighbour.

Decimation also moves the FM noises. The estimator of (Riley and Greenhall, Apr 2004) turns the lag-1 autocorrelation r₁ of the differenced series into α and rounds to the nearest integer, which is exact for discrete fractional noise. Phase decimated by m is instead a sampled continuous process. The second differences of sampled flicker FM and random-walk FM correlate at r₁ = (9 ln 3 − 16 ln 2)/(8 ln 2) = −0.217 and r₁ = 1/4, from their generalized autocovariances t² ln|t| and |t|³ (Greenhall and Riley, 2003), and the estimator reads those as α = −1.446 and −2.4 where discrete noise gives −1 and −2. Simulated records reach those values by m ≈ 8. Rounding at −1.5 therefore splits flicker FM almost evenly between its own label and random-walk FM. For m ≥ 2 SigmaTau places the limits halfway between the readings 0, −1.446 and −2.4, at −0.72 and −1.92; at m = 1 the record is not decimated and the published rounding stands. A measured record is itself a sampled process, so flicker FM that dominates at τ₀ can read near −1.45 there too, close to the rounding limit, where simulated discrete noise reads −1.

Stable32 does not detrend before the lag-1 estimate. The difference only matters on records with drift; identify_noise(...; remove_drift=false) follows Stable32, and Validation quantifies the effect.

The Hadamard family (hdev, mhdev, htdev, htotdev, mhtotdev) goes one step further, because it is the family used on drifting clocks. A frequency drift inflates the B1 ratio, which then reads random-walk FM at long τ whatever the noise, and the EDF and the HTOTDEV bias factor follow the wrong label. These estimators therefore identify noise on the record less its least-squares frequency drift (remove_drift=true), as SP1065 §5.5.6 (Riley, Jul 2008) has deterministic components removed before noise identification; the deviation itself does not change. The removal also takes some of the slowest noise with it, so on a record without drift flicker FM near the longest τ is read as white FM more often. remove_drift=false identifies the noise on the record as given, which is what Stable32 does.

The third difference of the Hadamard family also converges for flicker-walk FM (:FWFM, α = −3) and random-run FM (:RRFM, α = −4), and these estimators identify the two by letting the lag-1 estimator difference a third time (Riley and Greenhall, Apr 2004). The third differences of sampled random-walk, flicker-walk and random-run FM correlate at r₁ = −1/3, (128 ln 2 − 81 ln 3)/(27 ln 3 − 32 ln 2) = −0.035 and 13/33, which the estimator reads as α = −3.0, −3.93 and −4.57, so for m ≥ 2 the limits sit at −3.46 and −4.25. HDEV, MHDEV and HTOTDEV then take their EDF at the identified α (Greenhall and Riley, 2003) and HTOTDEV its bias factor (Howe and Peppler, 2001), and MHTOTDEV its own bias and EDF for each. Read as random-walk FM, random-run FM had an HDEV EDF 26 % too high and an HTOTDEV 6 % too low. The price is paid by random-walk FM at long τ: its second differences sit at ρ = 0.2, next to the 0.25 at which the estimator differences again, and in simulation it keeps its label in 91 % of records with 64 averaged points left and 81 % with 32, where two differences alone keep 98 % and 93 %. A random-walk FM record labelled flicker-walk FM has an HDEV EDF 2 % lower and an HTOTDEV 3.7 % higher.

Equivalent degrees of freedom

An estimated variance from a record with Gaussian noise follows a scaled χ² distribution. Its number of degrees of freedom, the EDF, measures how much independent information went into the estimate, and it depends on the estimator, the noise type, m and the record length N. It falls as τ grows, because a longer τ leaves fewer independent averages; that is why the last points of a stability plot carry wide intervals. SigmaTau computes it per τ (calculate_edf):

  • ADEV, MDEV, HDEV, MHDEV: the Greenhall–Riley algorithm (Greenhall and Riley, 2003), which evaluates the EDF exactly from the noise spectrum rather than from fitted approximations.
  • Total family: the published EDF coefficients of NIST SP1065 (Riley, Jul 2008) and Howe and Peppler (Howe and Peppler, 2001), with the Greenhall–Riley value where no coefficients exist (white and flicker PM for TOTDEV and HTOTDEV). For MHTOTDEV, which is new in SigmaTau, the model is fitted to the exact EDF of the statistic; see MHTOTDEV bias and EDF.
  • Thêo1 and ThêoH: the empirical per-noise formulas of SP1065 Table 3.
  • PDEV: the PVAR model of Vernotte, Chen and Rubiola.
  • MTIE and TIE rms have no χ² model and carry no interval.

r.edf holds the values, and r.neff the number of terms actually averaged at each τ.

Confidence intervals

Given the deviation σ̂ and the EDF ν, the interval at confidence level p is

\[\sigma_\mathrm{lo} = \hat\sigma \sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\chi^2_{(1+p)/2}(\nu)}}, \qquad \sigma_\mathrm{hi} = \hat\sigma \sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\chi^2_{(1-p)/2}(\nu)}}\]

(confidence_intervals). The default level is 68.3 % (DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE), the ±1σ convention of Stable32; pass confidence=0.95 for a 95 % interval. The interval is asymmetric about σ̂, wider above than below, because the χ² distribution is skewed at small ν. The formula holds for any ν > 0, so a point whose EDF falls below 1 keeps a χ² interval, a very wide one. Where an EDF model gives no positive value (SP1065's Thêo1 formula for random-walk FM beyond τ ≈ 0.63 T), the point has no interval and its bounds are NaN.

Bias correction

The total estimators and Thêo1 are biased for some noise types: their expected value differs from the true Allan-family variance by a known factor that depends on the noise and on τ/T. With correct_bias=true (the default) SigmaTau divides it out using the identified noise type (bias_correction):

EstimatorCorrection
TOTDEVHowe and Walter: τ/T-dependent, FLFM and RWFM only
MTOTDEV, TTOTDEVHowe and Peppler: MTOT reads 0.3 % (white PM) to 18 % (random-walk FM) low
HTOTDEVHowe and Peppler, FM noises
MHTOTDEVits exact expectation: 10 % (white PM) to 22 % (flicker FM) low in variance
Thêo1the ThêoBR ratio to ADEV, which needs no noise ID

The MTOT factors are Table 2 of Howe and Peppler (Howe and Peppler, 2001): the expected Mod Totvar is 0.995, 0.851, 0.771, 0.717 and 0.679 of MVAR from white PM to random-walk FM, as first simulated by Howe and Vernotte (Howe and Vernotte, Dec 1999), and the test suite checks the kernel against them. SP1065 Table 11 prints factors of 1.06 to 1.31 under a caption that reads as the same ratio; applied as printed they lower a statistic that already reads low. At τ = τ₀ MTOT is exactly half of MVAR for any record, and the correction is that factor of 2.

correct_bias=false returns the raw statistic. Stable32 corrects TOTDEV and HTOTDEV the same way and reports MTOT and Thêo1 raw; the validation page shows both against Stable32.

Drift

A linear frequency drift D adds a term Dτ/√2 to ADEV, which grows as τ¹ and hides everything else at long τ. The Hadamard family takes a third difference and does not see it. Otherwise remove it before the analysis: detrend with method=:quadratic fits and removes the quadratic phase term, which is the frequency drift. MHTOTDEV removes a quadratic from every window before reflecting it, so drift never reaches its reflected record.

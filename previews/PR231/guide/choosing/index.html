Choosing an estimator

Start with the question you are asking of the clock, then pick the estimator that answers it with the least bias and the narrowest interval. When in doubt, run the default suite, stability(p) (ADEV, MDEV, HDEV and MHDEV), and compare the curves.

Quick guide

Your situationUseWhy
A first look, or a number for a spec sheetadevThe standard measure σ_y(τ); every datasheet quotes it.
Short τ dominated by phase noise (white or flicker PM)mdevADEV falls as τ⁻¹ for both white and flicker PM; MDEV separates them (τ⁻³ᐟ² vs τ⁻¹).
The clock drifts (rubidium, quartz, uncorrected hydrogen maser)hdev, mhdevThe third difference removes linear frequency drift, which turns ADEV into a τ¹ ramp.
Long τ, where few averages remain and the intervals are widetotdev, mtotdev, htotdev, mhtotdevTotal estimators extend the record by reflection, so the last τ values carry more degrees of freedom.
The longest τ the record can supporttheo1 (ThêoBR), theohThêo1 reaches 75 % of the record span; ThêoH joins it to ADEV at short τ. Slow on long records.
Time error of a clock or a timing link, in secondstdev, htdev, ttotdevσ_x(τ): the time deviation scaled from MDEV, MHDEV or MTOTDEV.
Telecom timing masks (ITU-T G.810 family)mtie, tiermsMaximum and RMS time interval error over each window.
Data from an averaging (Λ-type) frequency counterpdevThe parabolic deviation is the matched estimator for averaged frequency readings.
Does the stability change during the record?dadev, dhdevσ(t, τ) over sliding windows; see Plotting.
Several clocks compared in pairs, and you want each clock alonencornered_hatThe N-cornered hat separates the pairwise variances, assuming independent clocks.
Live data, updated sample by sampleStreamingStability, LiveStabilityADEV, MDEV, HDEV and MHDEV at O(1) cost per sample; equals the batch call at every step. LiveStability takes instrument readings (phase, frequency or Hz) from an InstrumentSource.

The estimators

estimators() returns the same list as data (name, label, family, axis and a one-line description), and deviation(:mdev, p, m) runs any of them by name.

σ_y(τ): fractional-frequency stability

FunctionNameNote
adevAllan deviation (overlapping)The standard. Cannot tell white PM from flicker PM; diverges under drift.
mdevModified Allan deviationAverages phase over each τ, which separates white and flicker PM.
hdevHadamard deviation (overlapping)Insensitive to linear frequency drift.
mhdevModified Hadamard deviationDrift-insensitive and separates white and flicker PM.
pdevParabolic deviationLeast-squares frequency estimate per window; matched to averaging counters.
totdevTotal Allan deviationADEV with a reflected record: more EDF at long τ.
mtotdevTotal modified Allan deviationMDEV with per-window drift removal and reflection.
htotdevTotal Hadamard deviationHDEV with reflection: drift-insensitive with good long-τ confidence.
mhtotdevTotal modified Hadamard deviationMHDEV with reflection. New in SigmaTau; see MHTOTDEV bias and EDF.

theohThêoHADEV at short τ joined to ThêoBR at long τ: one curve over the full range.

Reading the slope

On a log-log σ_y(τ) plot each power-law noise has its own slope, which is how you read the noise mix off a curve (and how SigmaTau's noise identification works; see Noise, EDF and confidence):

NoiseADEV slopeMDEV slope
White PMτ⁻¹τ⁻³ᐟ²
Flicker PMτ⁻¹τ⁻¹
White FMτ⁻¹ᐟ²τ⁻¹ᐟ²
Flicker FMτ⁰ (flat)τ⁰
Random-walk FMτ¹ᐟ²τ¹ᐟ²
Linear frequency driftτ¹τ¹

HDEV and MHDEV follow the ADEV and MDEV slopes for these noises, but a linear drift does not appear in them at all.

Practical notes

  • Drift. If ADEV turns up as τ¹ at long τ, either use the Hadamard family or remove the drift first with detrend(p; method=:quadratic) (on phase, the quadratic term is the frequency drift).
  • Bias correction. The total estimators and Thêo1 are bias-corrected by default. correct_bias=false gives the raw statistic, as allantools does, and correct_bias=:stable32 what Stable32 reports for each.
  • Speed. The Allan and Hadamard estimators are O(N) per τ. The total family is heavier but multithreaded (start Julia with --threads=auto). Thêo1 and ThêoH are O(N²) overall, so they are off by default in the explorer.
  • Gaps. adev, mdev, tdev, hdev, mhdev, htdev, tierms and mtie skip NaN gaps, as allantools' gradev does for ADEV: on phase, a term counts when every sample it reads was measured; on frequency, when no missing sample falls inside its span. neff is the number of terms used and the EDF follows from it. The total estimators, Thêo1, PDEV and the dynamic deviations still need a gap-free record: fill it with fillgaps first. Filling changes the noise inside the gap, so prefer skipping where the estimator allows it.
  • Glitches. Remove phase steps or frequency spikes with remove_outliers before the analysis; see Reading and saving data.

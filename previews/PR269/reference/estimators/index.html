Estimators

Every estimator takes a PhaseData or a FrequencyData record and returns a StabilityResult. The averaging factors $m$, with $τ = mτ_0$, come second, in one of three forms:

adev(data)                  # the octave grid, up to the limit of the estimator
adev(data, Decade)          # another grid, a TauMode
adev(data, [1, 10, 100])    # the averaging factors, as a vector or a range

The estimators share their keywords, shown here with their defaults. The docstring of adev describes the common ones in full:

KeywordTaken byEffect
ci=trueallIdentify the noise type at each $τ$ and report the equivalent degrees of freedom and the confidence interval. false returns the deviation and the number of terms alone
confidence=0.683allThe level of the interval
noise=:autoall but mtie and tiermsThe noise type behind the interval and the bias factor: identified at each $τ$, or stated
correct_bias=truethe total estimators and theo1The bias factors divided out: those of this package, :published, :stable32, or none with false
remove_drift=truehdev, mhdev, htdev, htotdev, mhtotdevIdentify the noise type on the record less its frequency drift. false identifies it on the record as given, as Stable32 does
overlapping=trueadev, hdevfalse gives the non-overlapped estimator

mtie and tierms report no interval and accept ci and confidence without effect.

Choosing an estimator compares the estimators. Noise identification, Confidence intervals and Bias correction explain what the keywords select. Some estimators leave out the terms that a missing sample enters and the others need a complete record: Timestamps and gaps lists them.

Allan family

SigmaTau.adev — Function
adev(data, m_values; ci=true, overlapping=true, noise=:auto,
     confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Overlapping Allan deviation $σ_y(τ)$ (ADEV), the standard time-domain measure of frequency stability.

For $N$ phase samples $x_i$ at sample interval $τ_0$ and averaging time $τ = mτ_0$,

\[σ_y^2(τ) = \frac{1}{2τ^2(N-2m)} \sum_{i=1}^{N-2m} \left(x_{i+2m} - 2x_{i+m} + x_i\right)^2 .\]

This is the estimator of IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.23) and SP1065 §5.2.4, Eq. (11) for the two-sample variance of Allan (1966). The standard recommends it over the non-overlapped form, which has the same expectation and fewer degrees of freedom.

Arguments

  • data: a PhaseData or FrequencyData record. Frequency data is integrated to phase first.
  • m_values: the averaging factors $m ≥ 1$, as an integer vector or range; a TauMode grid such as Decade; or nothing, for octave spacing up to the largest $m$ the estimator supports. A factor that leaves fewer than two terms in the sum gives NaN.

Keywords

  • ci=true: identify the noise type at each $τ$ and report the equivalent degrees of freedom (EDF) and a χ² confidence interval. The EDF is that of the full algorithm of Greenhall and Riley (2003), §3.6. false computes the deviation alone.
  • overlapping=true: false selects the non-overlapped estimator of SP1065 §5.2.2, Eq. (7), one second difference every $m$ samples (Stable32's "Normal" Allan deviation, allantools' adev). Its EDF comes from the same algorithm with stride 1, and it needs a record without gaps.
  • noise=:auto: the power-law noise type that sets the EDF. :auto identifies it at each $τ$ with identify_noise. A symbol (:WHPM, :FLPM, :WHFM, :FLFM, :RWFM, :FWFM or :RRFM) states one type for every $τ$, a vector states one per averaging factor, and a PowerLawFit gives its strongest component at each $τ$. A type redder than the estimator converges for counts as the reddest it does. State the noise when the identified types contradict what is known of the clock: the test reads each $τ$ on its own, and a slow periodic or wandering term can mislead it where few averaged points remain.
  • confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE: confidence level of the interval.

Missing samples (NaN) are skipped: a term counts when every sample it reads was measured, and the EDF follows the number of terms summed (see Timestamps and gaps).

The other estimators take data and m_values in the same forms, and those that report an interval share ci, noise and confidence.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData(collect(1.0:10.0), 1.0);

julia> r = adev(p, [1, 2]; ci=false);

julia> round.(r.dev; sigdigits=4)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.0

SigmaTau.mdev — Function
mdev(data, m_values; ci=true, noise=:auto,
     confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Modified Allan deviation (MDEV): the second difference of phase averaged over $m$ adjacent samples.

\[\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_y^2(τ) = \frac{1}{2m^4τ_0^2(N-3m+1)} \sum_{j=1}^{N-3m+1} \left[\sum_{i=j}^{j+m-1} \left(x_{i+2m} - 2x_{i+m} + x_i\right)\right]^2\]

This is the estimator of IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.27) and SP1065 §5.2.5, Eq. (14), introduced by Allan and Barnes (1981). The averaging narrows the measurement bandwidth as $τ$ grows, so white PM falls as $τ^{-3/2}$ and flicker PM as $τ^{-1}$, two noise types that adev cannot tell apart.

Arguments and keywords are those of adev, less overlapping. Missing samples are skipped in the same way.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData(collect(1.0:10.0), 1.0);

julia> r = mdev(p, [1, 2]; ci=false);

julia> round.(r.dev; sigdigits=4)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.0

SigmaTau.tdev — Function
tdev(data, m_values; ci=true, noise=:auto,
     confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Time deviation $σ_x(τ)$ (TDEV), in seconds: the time-stability form of mdev.

\[σ_x(τ) = \frac{τ}{\sqrt{3}}\,\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_y(τ)\]

(IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.29); SP1065 §5.2.6, Eq. (15)). With this factor, TDEV on white PM is the standard deviation of the $m$-sample mean of the phase. It is the usual measure for time transfer and network timing. For a clock with frequency drift use htdev.

Arguments and keywords are those of mdev. The interval is MDEV's scaled by the same factor, with the same EDF. See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

Hadamard family

SigmaTau.hdev — Function
hdev(data, m_values; ci=true, remove_drift=true, overlapping=true,
     noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Overlapping Hadamard deviation (HDEV): a three-sample deviation built on the third difference of phase.

\[\mathrm{H}σ_y^2(τ) = \frac{1}{6τ^2(N-3m)} \sum_{i=1}^{N-3m} \left(x_{i+3m} - 3x_{i+2m} + 3x_{i+m} - x_i\right)^2\]

This is the estimator of SP1065 §5.2.9, Eq. (20); IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.31) gives the same variance in its non-overlapped form. The third difference cancels linear frequency drift, which adev reads as a term proportional to $τ$, and it converges for noise as red as random-run FM ($α = -4$). Like adev it cannot tell white PM from flicker PM; mhdev can.

Arguments and the keywords ci, noise and confidence are those of adev. Missing samples are skipped in the same way. See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

Keywords

  • remove_drift=true: identify the noise type on the record less its least-squares frequency drift (a quadratic in phase), so that a drift the statistic ignores cannot change the EDF. The deviation is the same either way. On that record the $B_1$ ratio of identify_noise is not used: where fewer than 30 averaged points remain, an FM type is the one found at the longest $τ$ that has 30, and a change from one FM type to another beyond that $τ$ has to be stated with noise. false identifies the noise on the record as given, as Stable32 does.
  • overlapping=true: false selects the non-overlapped estimator of SP1065 §5.2.8, Eq. (18), one third difference every $m$ samples (Stable32's "Normal" Hadamard deviation, allantools' hdev), under the conditions given for adev.
source
SigmaTau.mhdev — Function
mhdev(data, m_values; ci=true, remove_drift=true, noise=:auto,
      confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Modified Hadamard deviation (MHDEV): the third difference of phase averaged over $m$ adjacent samples.

\[\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_H^2(τ) = \frac{1}{6m^4τ_0^2(N-4m+1)} \sum_{j=1}^{N-4m+1} \left[\sum_{i=j}^{j+m-1} \left(x_{i+3m} - 3x_{i+2m} + 3x_{i+m} - x_i\right)\right]^2\]

This is the estimator of SP1065 §5.2.10, Eq. (23), the third-difference case of the modified Hadamard variance of Bregni and Jmoda (2006), Eq. (6). It ignores linear frequency drift as hdev does and separates white PM from flicker PM as mdev does. The EDF is that of the Greenhall and Riley (2003) algorithm for a modified third-difference variance.

Arguments and keywords are those of hdev, less overlapping. Missing samples are skipped in the same way. See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

source
SigmaTau.htdev — Function
htdev(data, m_values; ci=true, remove_drift=true, noise=:auto,
      confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Hadamard time deviation (HTDEV), in seconds: the time-stability form of mhdev, as tdev is of mdev.

\[σ_{x,H}(τ) = \frac{τ}{\sqrt{10/3}}\,\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_H(τ)\]

The statistic is defined in this package: SP1065 and IEEE Std 1139-2022 define no Hadamard time deviation. The factor is chosen as TDEV's is. On white PM the result is the standard deviation of the $m$-sample mean of the phase, so htdev and tdev agree there, and htdev keeps the drift rejection of MHDEV. Use it in place of tdev when the clock drifts.

Arguments and keywords are those of mhdev. The interval is MHDEV's scaled by the same factor, with the same EDF. See Choosing an estimator for when to use it.

Total estimators

SigmaTau.totdev — Function
totdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto,
       confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Total deviation (TOTDEV): an estimator of the Allan deviation with more degrees of freedom at long averaging times.

The record is extended at each end by reflection about the end sample, $x^*_{1-j} = 2x_1 - x_{1+j}$ and $x^*_{N+j} = 2x_N - x_{N-j}$, and a second difference is taken at every interior sample:

\[σ_{\mathrm{total}}^2(τ) = \frac{1}{2τ^2(N-2)} \sum_{i=2}^{N-1} \left(x^*_{i-m} - 2x^*_i + x^*_{i+m}\right)^2\]

(Greenhall et al. (1999), Eqs. (2) and (3); SP1065 §5.2.11, Eq. (25); IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eqs. (A.32) and (A.33)).

Arguments and the keywords ci, noise and confidence are those of adev. The record must have no gaps; fill them with fillgaps first. Each extension holds $N - 2$ samples, so the deviation exists for $m ≤ N - 1$. A larger averaging factor gives NaN with neff 0, and the grids stop at $m = \lfloor (N-1)/2 \rfloor$.

Keywords

  • correct_bias=true: divide the deviation by $\sqrt{B}$, where $B$ is the expected ratio of the total variance to the Allan variance for the noise type at that $τ$ (see bias_correction). The total variance reads low on flicker FM and random-walk FM, $B = 1 - aτ/T$ for a record of length $T$ (Greenhall et al. (1999), Eq. (25)), and high on white and flicker PM (see Total estimators on PM noise). :published and :stable32 correct FM noise only, as SP1065 and Stable32 do. false returns the uncorrected value, as allantools does.

The EDF on FM noise is $b\,T/τ - c$ (Greenhall et al. (1999), Eq. (26), with the coefficients as rounded in SP1065 Table 7). On PM noise it follows the model of Total estimators on PM noise. At $m = 1$ no term reaches the extension, the estimate equals ADEV, and ADEV's EDF is used.

source
SigmaTau.mtotdev — Function
mtotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto,
        confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Modified total deviation (MTOTDEV): the total form of mdev.

Each of the $N - 3m + 1$ subsequences of $3m$ phase samples is treated on its own. A linear trend is removed, with its slope taken from the means of the two halves of the subsequence. The residual is extended at both ends by even reflection to $9m$ samples. The squared second differences of $m$-sample phase means are then averaged over the $6m$ positions of the extension. The variance is the mean over the subsequences, divided by $2τ^2$ (Howe and Vernotte (1999), Eqs. (5) and (7); Howe and Peppler (2001), Eqs. (1) to (5); SP1065 §5.2.12, Eq. (27)).

Arguments and the keywords ci, noise and confidence are those of adev. The record must have no gaps.

Keywords

  • correct_bias=true: divide the deviation by $\sqrt{B}$, where $B$ is the expected ratio of the modified total variance to the modified Allan variance (see bias_correction). The estimator reads low: Howe and Peppler (2001), Table 2 give $B = 0.995$, 0.851, 0.771, 0.717 and 0.679 for white PM, flicker PM, white FM, flicker FM and random-walk FM at $τ ≥ 16τ_0$. Below that the exact value at each $m$ is used. At $m = 1$ the estimate is MDEV/$\sqrt{2}$ for any record and $B = 1/2$. :published applies the table at every $m > 1$. false or :stable32 returns the uncorrected value, which is what Stable32 and allantools report.

The EDF is MDEV's below $m = 16$, as in Stable32, and $(T/τ)/(b_0 + b_1τ/T)$ with the coefficients of Howe and Peppler's Table 2 from there. SP1065 Table 8 lists a different approximation, which is not used.

SigmaTau.ttotdev — Function
ttotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto,
        confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Time total deviation (TTOTDEV), in seconds: the time-stability form of mtotdev, as tdev is of mdev.

\[σ_{x,\mathrm{total}}(τ) = \frac{τ}{\sqrt{3}}\,\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_{\mathrm{total}}(τ)\]

(Howe and Peppler (2001); SP1065 §5.2.13, whose Eq. (28) prints $τ^3/3$ where the variance takes $τ^2/3$).

Arguments and keywords are those of mtotdev. The interval is MTOTDEV's scaled by the same factor, with the same EDF.

SigmaTau.htotdev — Function
htotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, remove_drift=true,
        noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Hadamard total deviation (HTOTDEV): the total form of hdev.

The computation is that of mtotdev applied to the $N - 1$ fractional-frequency values $y_i = (x_{i+1} - x_i)/τ_0$. Each of the $N - 3m$ subsequences of $3m$ values has a linear trend removed, with its slope taken from the means of its two halves, and is extended at both ends by even reflection to $9m$ values. The squared second differences of $m$-sample frequency means are averaged over the $6m$ positions of the extension. The variance is the mean over the subsequences, divided by 6 (Howe et al. (2005), Eqs. (3) to (5); Howe and Peppler (2001); SP1065 §5.2.14, Eq. (29)). At $m = 1$ the estimate is the overlapping Hadamard deviation.

Arguments and the keywords ci, noise and confidence are those of adev. The record must have no gaps.

Keywords

  • correct_bias=true: divide the deviation by $\sqrt{B}$, where $B$ is the expected ratio of the Hadamard total variance to the Hadamard variance (see bias_correction). On FM noise the estimator reads low, $B = 0.995$, 0.851, 0.771, 0.717 and 0.679 for white FM, flicker FM, random-walk FM, flicker-walk FM and random-run FM (Howe et al. (2005), Table I). Those values hold from $m = 16$; below that the exact value at each $m$ is used. On white and flicker PM it reads high, by up to 38 % in variance, and $B$ is its exact expectation (see Total estimators on PM noise). $B = 1$ at $m = 1$. :published or :stable32 applies the published FM factor at every $m > 1$ and none on PM, as Stable32 does. false returns the uncorrected value, as allantools does.
  • remove_drift=true: as in hdev. Here the noise type also selects $B$, so the keyword can change the deviation where the two identifications differ.

The EDF on FM noise is $(T/τ)/(b_0 + b_1τ/T)$ (Howe et al. (2005), Eq. (7)). On PM noise it follows the model of Total estimators on PM noise, and at $m = 1$ it is HDEV's.

source
SigmaTau.mhtotdev — Function
mhtotdev(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, remove_drift=true,
         noise=:auto, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Modified Hadamard total deviation (MHTOTDEV): the total form of mhdev.

Each of the $N - 4m + 1$ windows of $4m$ phase samples has its least-squares quadratic removed and is extended at both ends by even reflection to $12m$ samples. The squared third differences of $m$-sample phase means are averaged over the $8m + 1$ positions of the extension. The variance is the mean over the windows, divided by $6τ^2$. Removing a quadratic from each window makes the estimate insensitive to frequency offset and linear frequency drift, and it converges for every noise type MHDEV does, down to random-run FM ($α = -4$).

The construction is defined in this package. Vernotte and Howe (2000) outline a total form of the same third-difference variance, with a linear trend removed from each subsequence and a quadratic from the whole record, and give no bias or EDF for it. Bias and EDF for the estimator here are derived in MHTOTDEV bias and EDF.

Arguments and the keywords ci, noise and confidence are those of adev. The record must have no gaps.

Keywords

  • correct_bias=true: divide the deviation by $\sqrt{B}$, where $B$ is the expected ratio of this variance to the modified Hadamard variance (see bias_correction): 0.78 to 0.90 from $m = 16$, the exact value at each smaller $m$, and 124/225 at $m = 1$. false returns the uncorrected value. :published and :stable32 act as true, since no reference defines the estimator.
  • remove_drift=true: remove the least-squares frequency drift of the whole record before the analysis, so that the noise type is identified on the record without it, as in hdev. The uncorrected deviation does not depend on it; the corrected one changes only where the identified noise type, which selects $B$, does.

The EDF is a model fitted to the exact EDF of the statistic, and MHDEV's at $m = 1$.

Thêo1 and ThêoH

SigmaTau.theo1 — Function
theo1(data, m_values; ci=true, correct_bias=true, noise=:auto,
      confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Thêo1 deviation, bias-removed by default (ThêoBR): an estimator of the Allan deviation that reaches averaging times up to 75 % of the record length.

For an even averaging factor $m$,

\[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Thêo1}(m, τ_0, N) = {} & \frac{1}{0.75\,(N-m)(mτ_0)^2} \sum_{i=1}^{N-m} \sum_{δ=0}^{m/2-1} \frac{1}{m/2-δ} \\ & \times \left[\left(x_i - x_{i-δ+m/2}\right) + \left(x_{i+m} - x_{i+δ+m/2}\right)\right]^2 \end{aligned}\]

at the effective averaging time $τ = 0.75\,mτ_0$ (Howe and Peppler (2003), Eq. (1); Howe and Tasset (2004), Eq. (1); IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.35)). SP1065 §5.2.15, Eq. (30) prints the sum without the factor 0.75, which the white-FM bias of 1 in its Table 2 requires.

Arguments

  • data: as in adev. The record must have no gaps.
  • m_values: even Thêo1 factors with $2 ≤ m ≤ N - 1$; any other value gives NaN. The published definition takes $m ≥ 10$. A TauMode grid is rounded up to even factors. The result reports $τ = 0.75\,mτ_0$, as Stable32 does; allantools reports the same deviations at $mτ_0$.

Keywords

  • correct_bias=true: apply the ThêoBR correction. The Thêo1 variance is multiplied by the mean ratio of the Allan variance at $m = 9 + 3i$ to Thêo1 at $m = 12 + 4i$, two factors with the same averaging time, over $i = 0, …, n$ with $n = \lfloor N/6 - 3 \rfloor$ (SP1065 §5.2.16, Eq. (33); IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.37)). The ratio is measured on the record, so no noise type is identified. One ratio removes the bias where it changes little with $m$, as on FM noise of one type. On white and flicker PM the bias changes with $m$ throughout (Howe (2006), Table 3), and the corrected variance was 1.04 to 1.25 times the Allan variance at 0.2 to 0.7 of the record length in simulation ($N = 2048$). It needs $N ≥ 19$. false returns Thêo1 uncorrected.

  • correct_bias=:published: the same correction with $n = \lfloor N/30 - 3 \rfloor$, as Howe (2006), Eq. (6) prints it, which stops at a tenth of the record. Where the noise type changes within the record beyond that point, the ratio is that of the short averaging times at which it is measured: with PM giving way to FM the corrected variance at long $τ$ was 0.18 to 0.77 times the Allan variance in simulation.

  • correct_bias=:stable32: the correction of a Stable32 Thêo1 or ThêoH run with automatic noise identification. The Stable32 User Manual gives it as the default ratio, computed from at most 3000 phase samples (its setting MaxTheoBRPts), and names Taylor and Howe (2010) as the method. For a longer record this package takes every $b$-th sample, $b = \lceil N/3000 \rceil$, and adds $0.00055\,(b - 1)$ to the ratio. The term is the linear correction that Taylor and Howe fitted on simulated FM noise for a record averaged in blocks of $b$ samples (their Eq. (4)). The deviations agree with Stable32's to the five digits that it prints, at each of the 181 averaging factors of a Thêo1 run on its 1001-point record PHASE.DAT ($b = 1$) and at the two Thêo1 points of a ThêoH run on an 8192-point record ($b = 3$), where block averages give deviations 6.5 % higher. That Stable32 takes every $b$-th sample, and the value of $b$, are inferred from that agreement, and no other $b$ has been compared.

    With a noise type entered by hand, Stable32 applies the bias that its manual tabulates for the type, which is that of SP1065 Table 2. It is 1 for white FM, so correct_bias=false matches a Stable32 run made for white FM. This package has no option for the tabulated bias of the other noise types.

  • ci, noise, confidence: as in adev. The EDF is that of the empirical formulas of SP1065 Table 3, with or without the correction.

Length of the ThêoBR average

SP1065 Eq. (33), the Stable32 manual and IEEE Std 1139-2022 Eq. (A.37) print $n = \lfloor N/6 - 3 \rfloor$, which runs the Allan variance terms out to half the record. Howe (2006), Howe and Tasset (2004), Eq. (4), McGee and Howe (2007) and Taylor and Howe (2010), Eq. (1) all give $n = \lfloor 0.1N/3 - 3 \rfloor$, which keeps them within the first tenth. This package takes the longer average, and the shorter one under correct_bias=:published. In simulation ($N = 2048$) the two fail in different places. Where white or flicker PM gave way to FM within the record, the shorter average read 0.18 to 0.77 of the Allan variance at long $τ$ and the longer 0.56 to 1.12. On a record of one FM noise type the last terms of the longer average hold one or two Allan variance samples each: its ratio scattered 2.5 times more (white FM, 5 to 95 %: 0.58 to 1.41 against 0.83 to 1.16) and averaged 7 to 9 % low, at every record length tried, so ThêoBR at short $τ$ is less certain than ADEV there.

The double sum costs $O(Nm)$ per averaging factor. On long records the ThêoBR ratio computes Thêo1 at 48 of its $n + 1$ factors and interpolates the rest, which stays within 0.7 % of the full average; the longer average still makes the default call about 2.5 times slower than :published on very long records. The :stable32 ratio computes every factor.

source
SigmaTau.theoh — Function
theoh(data, m_values; ci=true, noise=:auto,
      confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

ThêoH: the overlapping Allan deviation at short averaging times joined to ThêoBR, the bias-removed Thêo1, at long ones, so that one curve runs from $τ_0$ to 75 % of the record length (Howe (2006), Eq. (7); SP1065 §5.2.16, Eq. (34)).

Each requested factor $m$, any positive integer, is a target averaging time $mτ_0$ and is computed according to its place in the record of length $T = (N-1)τ_0$:

  • $mτ_0 ≤ 0.2\,T$: adev at $m$.
  • $mτ_0 > 0.2\,T$: ThêoBR (see theo1) at the even Thêo1 factor nearest $4m/3$, reported at its own effective averaging time, which can differ from $mτ_0$ by one rounding step.

The crossover at 20 % of the record is that of SP1065. Howe (2006) places it at 10 %. The interval uses the EDF of ADEV below the crossover and of Thêo1 above it. The bias removal is always applied, with the default ratio of theo1, and needs $N ≥ 19$. For uncorrected Thêo1, or for the ratio of a Stable32 ThêoH run, call theo1 with correct_bias=false or correct_bias=:stable32.

data and the keywords are as in adev, less overlapping. The record must have no gaps.

Parabolic deviation

SigmaTau.pdev — Function
pdev(data, m_values; ci=true, noise=:auto,
     confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Parabolic deviation (PDEV): the two-sample deviation of frequencies estimated by linear regression on the phase, as an Ω counter measures them (IEEE Std 1139-2022, A.3.4).

For $m ≥ 2$,

\[σ_P^2(τ) = \frac{72}{m^4τ^2(N-2m)} \sum_{i=1}^{N-2m} \left[\sum_{k=0}^{m-1} \left(\frac{m-1}{2} - k\right)\left(x_{i+k} - x_{i+k+m}\right)\right]^2\]

(Vernotte et al. (2016), Eq. (15)), and at $m = 1$ it is the Allan deviation by definition (their Eq. (14)). Like mdev, PDEV falls as $τ^{-3/2}$ on white PM and $τ^{-1}$ on flicker PM. It is the more efficient of the two on white PM, because linear regression is the best estimate of a frequency in white phase noise.

The EDF follows Vernotte et al. (2021): $ν ≈ 35/(A\,m/M - 12\,(m/M)^2)$ with $M = N - 2m$ up to $m ≈ N/4$ (their Eqs. (16) and (18)), and their interpolation in $\ln m$ down to $ν = 1$ near $m = N/2$ beyond (Eqs. (22) to (24)). The authors give its accuracy as ±10 % except at $m = 1$ and 2. At $m = 1$ the EDF of adev is used.

Arguments and keywords are those of adev, less overlapping. The record must have no gaps.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData(collect(1.0:10.0), 1.0);

julia> r = pdev(p, [1, 2]; ci=false);

julia> round.(r.dev; sigdigits=4)
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.0

Time interval error

SigmaTau.mtie — Function
mtie(data, m_values; ci=true, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Maximum time interval error (MTIE), in seconds: the largest peak-to-peak phase excursion within any observation interval $τ = mτ_0$, a window of $m + 1$ samples.

\[\mathrm{MTIE}(mτ_0) = \max_{1 \le k \le N-m} \left(\max_{k \le i \le k+m} x_i - \min_{k \le i \le k+m} x_i\right)\]

(ITU-T G.810, clause 4.5.15 and Appendix II.5; SP1065 §5.2.17, Eq. (35)).

MTIE is a peak statistic, not a variance: one outlier or phase step sets it, and it has no χ² interval. The fields noise_type, ci and edf of the result are empty. The keywords ci and confidence are accepted, so that every estimator can be called alike, and ignored. With missing samples, each window gives the peak-to-peak of the samples measured in it. Compare the result with a network limit using mask_check.

data and m_values are as in adev.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> p = PhaseData([0.0, 1.0, 0.5, 2.0, 1.5], 1.0);

julia> r = mtie(p, [1, 3]);

julia> r.dev
2-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.5
 2.0

SigmaTau.tierms — Function
tierms(data, m_values; ci=true, confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE) → StabilityResult

Root-mean-square time interval error (TIE rms), in seconds: the rms phase change over an interval $τ = mτ_0$.

\[\mathrm{TIE}_{\mathrm{rms}}(mτ_0) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N-m} \sum_{i=1}^{N-m} \left(x_{i+m} - x_i\right)^2}\]

(SP1065 §5.2.18, Eq. (37); ITU-T G.810, Appendix II.4). Stable32 and allantools (tierms) compute the same estimator.

Unlike tdev, TIE rms includes any frequency offset in the record, which contributes the offset times $τ$, and it does not converge for flicker FM and random-walk FM (G.810, Appendix II.4). Like mtie it has no χ² interval: noise_type, ci and edf are empty, and the keywords ci and confidence are accepted and ignored. A term with a missing sample is skipped.

data and m_values are as in adev.

Telecom masks

The ITU-T recommendations limit the wander of a clock, or of a synchronization interface, by masks on MTIE and TDEV over the observation interval $τ$. mask_check compares an mtie or a tdev result with one of them, and TELECOM_MASKS lists the masks with the measurement that the recommendations prescribe. The command line checks a result with analyze --mask, and the explorer draws the limits on its Stability page.

SigmaTau.TELECOM_MASKS — Constant
TELECOM_MASKS

Names of the ITU-T wander masks that telecom_mask and mask_check know. Each limits MTIE and TDEV.

  • :G811: wander generation of a primary reference clock (G.811, clause 6.1).
  • :G813_opt1, :G813_opt2: wander generation of an SDH equipment clock in locked mode at constant temperature (G.813, clause 7.1). Option 1 is for networks of the 2048 kbit/s hierarchy, option 2 for the 1544 kbit/s hierarchy.
  • :G8262_opt1, :G8262_opt2: the same for a synchronous equipment clock, with the limits of G.813 (G.8262, clause 8.1).
  • :G823_PRC, :G823_SSU, :G823_SEC, :G823_PDH: network limits for wander at PRC, SSU, SEC and PDH synchronization interfaces (G.823, clause 6.2).

The clock recommendations measure MTIE and TDEV through a 10 Hz first-order low-pass filter at a sample interval of 1/30 s or less, and TDEV over a record of at least $12τ$. This package applies no such filter: the record is compared as it was measured.

Dynamic stability

dadev and dhdev compute a deviation inside a window that slides along the record and return the map $σ_y(t, τ)$ as a DynamicStabilityResult. Dynamic stability draws it.

SigmaTau.dadev — Function
dadev(data, m_values=Octave; window, step=window÷2) → DynamicStabilityResult

Dynamic Allan deviation $σ_y(t, τ)$ (DADEV): the overlapping Allan deviation of a sliding window of the record, as a function of the position of the window.

For a window of $N_w$ samples centered at time $t$,

\[σ_y^2(t, τ) = \frac{1}{2τ^2(N_w-2m)} \sum_{i} \left(x_{i+2m} - 2x_{i+m} + x_i\right)^2 ,\]

summed over the $N_w - 2m$ second differences that fit in the window (Galleani and Tavella (2009), Eq. (34); IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.40)). The map shows changes of stability with time, such as a step in noise level or a burst, that a single $σ_y(τ)$ curve averages away. McKelvy et al. (2026) take it as the target of models that estimate the stability of hydrogen masers from their telemetry.

Arguments

  • data: a PhaseData or FrequencyData record without gaps.
  • m_values: averaging factors shared by every window, or a TauMode grid, which is resolved against the window length and not the record length. A factor the window cannot support ($N_w - 2m < 2$) gives a NaN column.

Keywords

  • window: the window length $N_w$ in samples. The default is the largest power of two up to $N/8$, and at least 16, which gives about fifteen windows.
  • step=window÷2: the number of samples between successive windows. The cost is one Allan deviation of window samples per window, so halving step doubles the run time.

The result holds one row per window and one column per $τ$. No confidence interval is reported. Galleani (2011) derives confidence surfaces for the dynamic Allan variance, which are not implemented here. Each row is an ordinary Allan deviation of window samples, so adev on that slice of the record gives its interval.

source
SigmaTau.dhdev — Function
dhdev(data, m_values=Octave; window, step=window÷2) → DynamicStabilityResult

Dynamic Hadamard deviation (DHDEV): the overlapping Hadamard deviation (hdev) of a sliding window, built as dadev is (SP1065 §5.2.20). The third difference cancels linear frequency drift within each window, so the map of a drifting clock shows its noise and not its drift.

Arguments and keywords are those of dadev. A factor the window cannot support ($N_w - 3m < 2$) gives a NaN column. No confidence interval is reported.

Three-cornered hat

ncornered_hat and groslambert separate the deviations of three or more clocks that are measured against each other. The tutorial Three clocks and the three-cornered hat works through both.

SigmaTau.ncornered_hat — Function
ncornered_hat(pairwise::AbstractMatrix{StabilityResult}) → Vector{StabilityResult}
ncornered_hat(r_ab, r_bc, r_ca) → Vector{StabilityResult}

Deviation of each of $N ≥ 3$ clocks from the deviations measured between every pair of them: the three-cornered hat of Gray and Allan (1974), and its extension to $N$ clocks.

For independent clocks the variance measured between clocks $i$ and $j$ is $s_{ij}^2(τ) = σ_i^2(τ) + σ_j^2(τ)$, and the variance of clock $i$ follows from the $N(N-1)/2$ measured ones as

\[\hat σ_i^2(τ) = \frac{1}{N-2} \left( \sum_{j \ne i} s_{ij}^2 - \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{j<k} s_{jk}^2 \right)\]

(SP1065 §10.14, Eqs. (71) and (72)). For three clocks this is $\hat σ_a^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(s_{ab}^2 + s_{ac}^2 - s_{bc}^2)$.

The estimate can be negative for a clock much more stable than the others, for clocks that are correlated, and where few terms are averaged, most often at long $τ$; dev is NaN there. Noise of the instrument that measured a pair counts as noise of its two clocks. groslambert keeps it out, given the difference records.

Arguments

  • pairwise: an $N × N$ matrix whose entry [i, j] is the result of an estimator on the difference record of clocks $i$ and $j$. One entry per pair is enough, in either triangle. All entries must come from one estimator on one $τ$ grid. The estimator must be one whose variance adds for independent clocks, such as adev, mdev or hdev.
  • r_ab, r_bc, r_ca: for three clocks, the results for A−B, B−C and C−A. The sign of each difference does not matter. The clocks are returned in the order A, B, C.

Returns

One StabilityResult per clock, of the estimator of the inputs.

  • edf and ci are filled when every input has an EDF, at the confidence level of the inputs. The variance of $\hat σ_i^2$ follows from that of each pairwise variance, $2s_{ij}^4/ν_{ij}$, and from the covariance $2σ_c^4/\sqrt{ν_1 ν_2}$ of two pairs that share clock $c$ (Vernotte and Lantz (2019), Appendix B, with the EDF $ν$ of each record in place of their number of independent measurements). edf is the equivalent $2\hat σ_i^4/\mathrm{Var}(\hat σ_i^2)$, and ci its $χ^2$ interval. For three clocks with a common EDF this is the loss of degrees of freedom of SP1065, Eq. (70). The interval is too narrow for a clock that is no longer resolved under the others (see groslambert), and none is given below 1 EDF: the limits are NaN there.
  • noise_type is empty, and neff is the smallest of the inputs' at each $τ$.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau

julia> x1, x2, x3 = randn(256), randn(256), randn(256);

julia> r(a, b) = adev(PhaseData(a .- b, 1.0), [1, 2, 4]);

julia> clocks = ncornered_hat(r(x1, x2), r(x2, x3), r(x3, x1));

julia> length(clocks), length(clocks[1].ci)
(3, 3)

SigmaTau.groslambert — Function
groslambert(pairwise::AbstractMatrix{<:AbstractTimingData}, m_values=Octave;
            confidence=DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE, interval=:chi2) → Vector{StabilityResult}
groslambert(x_ab, x_bc, x_ca, m_values=Octave; kwargs...) → Vector{StabilityResult}

Allan deviation of each of $N ≥ 3$ clocks from the difference records between them, by the Groslambert covariance: the cross Allan variance of two records that share the clock (Vernotte et al. (2016), Eq. (7), after the two-sample cross-variance of Groslambert et al. (1981), Eqs. (12) to (16)).

The second differences of $x_i - x_j$ and $x_i - x_k$ have only clock $i$ in common, so for independent clocks

\[\hat σ_i^2(τ) = \frac{1}{2τ^2} \left\langle Δ^2(x_i - x_j)\; Δ^2(x_i - x_k) \right\rangle , \qquad Δ^2 x_n = x_{n+2m} - 2x_{n+m} + x_n ,\]

averaged over the record and, with more than three clocks, over every pair $(j, k)$ of the others.

On records that close, $(x_i - x_j) + (x_j - x_k) + (x_k - x_i) = 0$, the estimate equals that of ncornered_hat on the Allan variances of the records. The two differ when each record carries noise of its own, such as that of the counter that measured it: that noise biases the hat and averages out of the covariance (Groslambert et al. (1981), Eqs. (9) and (17) to (19); Vernotte et al. (2016), Eqs. (13) and (15)). Like the hat, the estimate can be negative for a clock much more stable than the others; dev is NaN there.

Arguments

  • pairwise: an $N × N$ matrix whose entry [i, j] is the record $x_i - x_j$. One entry per pair is enough, in either triangle. The records are all PhaseData or all FrequencyData, with one sample interval and length and no missing samples.
  • x_ab, x_bc, x_ca: for three clocks, the records A−B, B−C and C−A. The clocks are returned in the order A, B, C.
  • m_values: averaging factors, or a TauMode grid.

Keywords

  • confidence=0.683: confidence level of the interval.
  • interval=:chi2: the interval reported, :chi2 or, for three clocks, :klts.

Returns

One StabilityResult of type :adev per clock, without noise types. edf is the equivalent $2\hat σ_i^4/\mathrm{Var}(\hat σ_i^2)$. The variance of the estimate is that of a mean of products of Gaussian second differences (Vernotte and Lantz (2019), Appendix B). Each record's Allan variance enters it, and the geometric mean of the records' EDF for their identified noise types stands for the number of independent measurements, a choice of this package.

Confidence intervals

With interval=:chi2 the interval is the $χ^2$ interval on edf; none is given below 1 EDF. It holds for a clock that dominates its records. For a clock more stable than the others it becomes too narrow once the clock is no longer resolved, and its lower bound then lies above the true value.

With interval=:klts, for three clocks, the interval is that of Lantz et al. (2019): quantiles of the posterior distribution of the three clock variances, given the sample covariance of the three records. The prior is uniform in the logarithm of each variance, from $10^{-5}$ times the smallest estimate to $10^3$ times the largest. The noise of the measuring instruments is estimated from the closure of the records (their Eq. (9)), and the number of degrees of freedom is the geometric mean of the records' EDF. A lower bound that would depend on the lower limit of the prior is reported as 0: the record does not exclude zero. The interval is given also where the estimate is negative. It is computed on a grid in place of the random draws of the paper, in about 0.1 s for each $τ$, and agrees within 2 % with the KLTS bounds of SigmaTheta on the same inputs.

In simulation (4096 samples; a white FM clock beside two clocks two and three times noisier, with instrument noise in each record; 400 records), the 95 % $χ^2$ interval covered the true value of the most stable clock in 94 % of the records at $10τ_0$. At $100τ_0$ four records in ten gave a negative estimate, and the interval covered the true value in 60 % of the others. The KLTS interval covered it in 92 % of all records at $10τ_0$, in 86 % at $20τ_0$, where the clock is barely resolved and the prior draws the upper bound down, and in 99 % or more from $50τ_0$.

Examples

ab, bc, ca = read_phase("ab.txt"), read_phase("bc.txt"), read_phase("ca.txt")
a, b, c = groslambert(ab, bc, ca; interval=:klts, confidence=0.95)

Two channels

cross_adev estimates the Allan deviation of a device from two simultaneous records that share the device and nothing else: the noise of the two channels, or of the two references, averages toward zero. Two-channel measurements says when the method applies and what it cannot remove. The cross-spectra are on the Spectra page.

SigmaTau.cross_adev — Function
cross_adev(a, b, m_values=Octave) → CrossStabilityResult

Cross Allan variance of two simultaneous records of one device: the overlapping Allan variance with the square of each second difference replaced by the product of the two channels' second differences,

\[σ_{ab}^2(τ) = \frac{1}{2τ^2 (N-2m)} \sum_{i=1}^{N-2m} \left(a_{i+2m} - 2a_{i+m} + a_i\right) \left(b_{i+2m} - 2b_{i+m} + b_i\right), \qquad τ = mτ_0 ,\]

with $a_i$ and $b_i$ the phase samples of the two channels. This is the two-sample covariance that Vernotte et al. (2016), Eq. (7) call the Groslambert covariance.

It estimates the Allan variance of the noise the channels share. Noise in only one channel averages toward zero, to within the uncertainty given at each $τ$. With b equal to a the result is the overlapping Allan variance. The estimate can be negative: covar keeps the sign, and dev is NaN there.

Arguments

  • a, b: two PhaseData or two FrequencyData records with the same sample interval and length, and no missing samples.
  • m_values: averaging factors, or a TauMode grid.

CrossStabilityResult

Result of cross_adev.

Fields

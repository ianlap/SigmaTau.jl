Noise and statistics

Synthesize power-law noise, identify it, and compute the EDF, bias factors and confidence intervals that every estimator applies when ci=true. Noise, EDF and confidence explains the method; these functions are the building blocks, for when you need them outside an estimator call.

Noise

SigmaTau.noise_gen — Function
noise_gen(PhaseData, N, tau0; sigma1=Dict(), h=Dict(), rng=nothing) → PhaseData
noise_gen(FrequencyData, N, tau0; sigma1=Dict(), h=Dict(),
          rng=nothing) → FrequencyData

Simulated clock record of N samples at sample interval tau0 whose fractional-frequency spectrum is a sum of power laws, $S_y(f) = \sum_α h_α f^α$. The first argument selects the kind of record returned.

Each component is white Gaussian noise passed through the fractional differencing filter $(1 - z^{-1})^{α/2}$ of Kasdin and Walter (1992), Eqs. (36) and (42), which is exact at the integer orders: a first difference for $α = 2$, a running sum for $α = -2$. The components are independent.

$α$Noise typeLabelSlope of $S_x(f)$
2white PM:WHPMflat
1flicker PM:FLPM$f^{-1}$
0white FM:WHFM$f^{-2}$
−1flicker FM:FLFM$f^{-3}$
−2random-walk FM:RWFM$f^{-4}$
−3flicker-walk FM:FWFM$f^{-5}$
−4random-run FM:RRFM$f^{-6}$

Keywords

Give sigma1 or h, not both. The keys are the integer $α$ or the label, as in Dict(:WHFM => 1e-12).

  • sigma1: the Allan deviation $σ_y(τ_0)$ of each component, for when a specification quotes a deviation at one averaging time. Each component is rescaled so that its overlapping Allan deviation at $τ_0$ equals the request exactly, in every realization.
  • h: the coefficient $h_α$ of each component. The filter driven by unit white noise has the one-sided spectrum $2τ_0\,|2\sin(πfτ_0)|^α$ (their Eq. (37)), so the component is scaled by $\sqrt{h_α/(2τ_0(2πτ_0)^α)}$ and has $S_y(f) = h_α\,(\sin(πfτ_0)/(πτ_0))^α$, which is $h_α f^α$ well below the Nyquist frequency.
  • rng: a random number generator, such as Xoshiro(1), for a reproducible or an independent stream. The global generator is used by default.

For $α ≤ -1$ the process is not stationary, and the spectrum above is that of its differences: the $d$-th difference of $y$ has the spectrum $(2\sin(πfτ_0))^{2d} S_y(f)$.

The record is a sampled process, not a band-limited continuous one. The Allan variance of an h component follows the usual conversion (SP1065 §7.1, Eq. (67), with the bandwidth $f_h = 1/(2τ_0)$) at every $τ$ for white PM and white FM, and only at long $τ$ for the other types. powerlaw_sigma gives the exact deviation of such a record and states the difference.

Examples

julia> using SigmaTau, Random

julia> p = noise_gen(PhaseData, 4096, 1.0; sigma1=Dict(:WHFM => 1e-12),
                     rng=Xoshiro(1));

julia> adev(p, [1]; ci=false).dev[1] ≈ 1e-12
true

White FM with random-walk FM, by deviation and by coefficient:

p = noise_gen(PhaseData, 8192, 1.0; sigma1=Dict(0 => 1e-12, -2 => 1e-14))
y = noise_gen(FrequencyData, 8192, 1.0; h=Dict(0 => 2e-24, -2 => 1e-28))

SigmaTau.identify_noise — Function
identify_noise(x, m_values; dmin=0, dmax=2, remove_drift=true,
               b1_length=:averaged) → Vector{Symbol}
identify_noise(data, m_values; kwargs...) → Vector{Symbol}

Dominant power-law noise type of a record at each averaging factor: :WHPM, :FLPM, :WHFM, :FLFM or :RWFM, and with dmax=3 also :FWFM (flicker-walk FM, $α = -3$) and :RRFM (random-run FM, $α = -4$). The estimators use it for the noise type that sets their EDF and bias correction.

Method

For each $m$ the phase is decimated by $m$, and the lag-1 autocorrelation $r_1$ of the result gives $δ = r_1/(1 + r_1)$. The series is differenced until $δ < 0.25$ or dmax differences have been taken; after $d$ differences, $α = 2 - 2(δ + d)$ (Riley and Greenhall (2004); SP1065 §5.5.6). This package adds four steps:

  • For $m ≥ 2$ the limits between the FM labels are moved. A decimated record is a sampled continuous process, on which the estimate reads $α = 0$, −1.446 and −2.4 for white, flicker and random-walk FM, and after a third difference −3.0, −3.93 and −4.57 for random-walk, flicker-walk and random-run FM. The limits sit halfway between those readings. Rounding to the nearest integer, as the paper does and as is done here at $m = 1$, would split flicker FM almost evenly between its own label and random-walk FM.
  • A white-PM result at $m ≥ 4$ is confirmed with $R(n)$, the ratio of the modified to the plain Allan variance (SP1065 §5.5.2), because decimation whitens flicker PM.
  • Where fewer than 30 decimated points remain, the lag-1 test gives way to the $B_1$ ratio of the standard variance to the Allan variance of the $m$-averaged frequency, with $R(n)$ to separate the PM types (SP1065 §5.5.2), as in Stable32. That test stops at random-walk FM.
  • From $m = 16$ a PM result of either test stands only if $R(n)$ lies nearer its flicker-PM value than 1/2, its value on white FM (it is 27/40 on flicker FM and 33/40 on random-walk FM). One that fails is reported as white FM. $R(n)$ uses every sample where the tests read one in $m$, and with few decimated points they take FM for PM often: white FM in one record in four at 32 points. A PM label there makes the interval several times too narrow.

Samples farther than five standard deviations from the record mean are dropped before the analysis.

The function always returns a label. An estimate outside the labeled range counts as the nearest end of it, an averaging factor whose estimate fails takes the label of the nearest one in $\log m$ that succeeded, and a record with no usable estimate, such as a constant one, is reported as :WHFM.

Arguments

  • x: phase samples, or data, a PhaseData or FrequencyData record (frequency is integrated to phase).
  • m_values: averaging factors. With a record, any integer vector or a TauMode.

Keywords

  • dmin=0, dmax=2: the fewest and the most differences taken. Use dmax=2 for the Allan family and dmax=3 for the Hadamard family, as the estimators do.
  • remove_drift=true: remove a least-squares quadratic, that is a frequency offset and a linear frequency drift, from each decimated series before the test, as allantools does. false skips it.
  • b1_length=:averaged: the record length $N$ at which the expected $B_1(N, μ)$ of each noise type is evaluated in the test for fewer than 30 points. :averaged is the number of $m$-averaged frequency values the ratio is computed from, which is the $N$ of the definition (SP1065 §5.7). :record is the number of frequency samples in the whole record, which follows Stable32's labels more closely; at that length the limit for random-walk FM lies far above anything a few averaged values can reach.
Labels from fewer than 30 points

In simulation ($N = 1024$, 6 to 24 averaged values) :averaged finds random-walk FM in 60 to 74 % of records and flicker FM in 33 to 79 %, where :record finds them in 1 to 2 % and 23 to 46 %. White FM pays for it: 62 to 96 % against 86 to 100 %. With ten values or fewer, flicker FM and random-walk FM get their own label in a third to two thirds of records, so a label at the longest averaging times is weak evidence.

Below 6 averaged values :averaged reports the label found at the longest averaging factor that still has 6, because with 2 to 4 values the ratio is close to a coin toss. The labels still cost white FM something there: its bias-corrected total variance reads 4 % high with four averaged values left and 18 % high with two, where the uncorrected one is within 1 %. The value 6 comes from simulation, not from the literature.

SigmaTau 0.6

remove_drift was called detrend before version 0.6. detrend=false still works and gives a deprecation warning.

SigmaTau.noise_id_nnls — Function
noise_id_nnls(data, m_values; deviation=:mhdev, alphas, weights=:edf) → NamedTuple

Amount of each power-law noise type in a record, from one nonnegative least-squares fit of the measured variance to $σ^2(τ) ≈ \sum_i a_i\,(τ/τ_0)^{μ(α_i)}$ over the whole range of $τ$. Where identify_noise names the dominant type at each $τ$, this fit gives an amplitude for every type in the basis. It runs on the deviation without bias correction or noise identification, so it does not depend on that test.

For coefficients $h_α$ use powerlaw_fit, which fits the exact response of each component. The basis here is the asymptotic power law $τ^μ$ of each type, and the total estimators bend away from it as $τ$ approaches the record length, so prefer :mhdev when the longest averaging times matter.

Arguments

  • data: a PhaseData or FrequencyData record.
  • m_values: averaging factors. The default is the octave grid of the estimator from $m = 4$, because at $m = 1$ and 2 the estimators have not yet reached their power laws.

Keywords

  • deviation=:mhdev: the estimator fitted, one of :mdev, :hdev, :mhdev and :mhtotdev.
  • alphas: the exponents in the basis. The default is every type the estimator converges for: 2 to −2 for :mdev and 2 to −4 for the Hadamard family, which is also insensitive to drift. :hdev cannot tell white PM from flicker PM and takes one of them, white PM by default.
  • weights=:edf: :edf weights each point by $ν/(2σ^4)$, with the EDF $ν$ for the type that dominates a first fit there; :relative gives every point the same fractional weight; a vector gives the weights directly.

Returns

A named tuple with deviation, tau (the averaging times fitted), var (the measured variance), fit (the model at each $τ$), alphas, noise_types, amplitudes ($a_i ≥ 0$, the variance of each component at $τ_0$), contributions (a matrix, $τ$ by type), dominant (the largest contribution at each $τ$), residual and weights.

Examples

p = noise_gen(PhaseData, 8192, 1.0; sigma1=Dict(0 => 1e-12, -2 => 1e-14))
r = noise_id_nnls(p)
collect(zip(r.noise_types, r.amplitudes))   # amplitude of each type
r.dominant                                  # dominant type at each τ

SigmaTau.DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE — Constant
DEFAULT_CONFIDENCE

Default confidence level of the reported intervals, 0.683: the two-sided one-sigma level usual on stability plots (SP1065 §5.3). Pass confidence, for example confidence=0.95, to an estimator to change it. mtie and tierms report no interval.

SigmaTau.calculate_edf — Function
calculate_edf(method, devs, noises, m_values, taus, N, T) → Vector{Float64}

Equivalent degrees of freedom (EDF) of a variance estimate at each averaging factor: the $ν$ of the χ² distribution behind confidence_intervals. The estimators call it for the interval they report.

Arguments

  • method: the estimator, one of the symbols below. Any other gives NaN.
  • devs: the deviations. Only their number is used.
  • noises: the noise type at each point (:WHPM, :FLPM, :WHFM, :FLFM, :RWFM, :FWFM or :RRFM). Any other label counts as white FM.
  • m_values, taus: the averaging factor and averaging time of each point.
  • N, T: the number of phase samples and the record duration $(N - 1)τ_0$.

Models

  • :adev, :mdev, :hdev, :mhdev: the full version of the algorithm of Greenhall and Riley (2003), §3.6, for the overlapping estimator. The result is NaN where the variance does not converge for the noise type (flicker-walk and random-run FM with :adev and :mdev).
  • :totdev: $b\,T/τ - c$ on white, flicker and random-walk FM (Greenhall et al. (1999), Eq. (26), with the coefficients as rounded in SP1065 Table 7). On white and flicker PM, the model of Total estimators on PM noise. At $m = 1$, the EDF of :adev.
  • :mtotdev: $(T/τ)/(b_0 + b_1τ/T)$ from $τ = 16τ_0$ (Howe and Peppler (2001), Table 2), and the EDF of :mdev below it. SP1065 Table 8 gives a different approximation, which is not used.
  • :htotdev: the same form on white FM to random-run FM, with the coefficients of Howe et al. (2005), Eq. (7) and Table I. The paper states it for $τ ≥ 16τ_0$; it is applied here from $m = 2$. On white and flicker PM, the model of Total estimators on PM noise. At $m = 1$, the EDF of :hdev.
  • :mhtotdev: the model of MHTOTDEV bias and EDF, and the EDF of :mhdev at $m = 1$.
  • :theo1: the empirical formulas of SP1065 §5.2.15, Table 3, for white PM to random-walk FM.
  • :pdev: the approximation of Vernotte et al. (2021), Eqs. (16), (18) and (22) to (24), for white PM to random-walk FM, and the EDF of :adev at $m = 1$.

SigmaTau.bias_correction — Function
bias_correction(noises, var_type, m_values, N; published=false) → Vector{Float64}

Bias factor $B = E[\text{total variance}]/E[\text{parent variance}]$ of a total estimator at each averaging factor, for a record of N phase samples. The estimators divide a total deviation by $\sqrt{B}$, so $B < 1$ means that the uncorrected deviation reads low. noises gives the noise type at each entry of m_values; a label outside the seven power-law names counts as white FM. Below, $s = τ/T = m/(N-1)$.

  • var_type = :totvar (total variance against Allan variance): $B = 1 - a\,s$ with $a = 1/(3\ln 2)$ for flicker FM and $a = 3/4$ for random-walk FM (Greenhall et al. (1999), Eq. (25) and Table I; SP1065 §5.11, Eq. (52)), and $B = 1$ for white FM. On PM noise the estimator reads high: $B = 1 + (8/3)⌊m/2⌋/(N-2)$ for white PM, which is exact, and $B = 1 + (0.593 + 0.068\ln m)\,s$ for flicker PM.
  • :mtot (modified total against modified Allan): $B = 0.995$, 0.851, 0.771, 0.717 and 0.679 for white PM, flicker PM, white FM, flicker FM and random-walk FM, independent of $τ$ from $τ = 16τ_0$ (Howe and Peppler (2001), Table 2, which lists $a = B - 1$). At $m = 2, …, 15$ the exact expectation at that $m$ is used, up to 6 % from the published value, and $B = 1/2$ at $m = 1$, exactly.
  • :htot (Hadamard total against Hadamard): the same five values for white FM to random-run FM (Howe et al. (2005), Table I), with the same values below $m = 16$. On PM noise the estimator reads high: $B = 1.384 - 0.28/m$ (even $m$) or $1.370 - 0.82/m$ (odd $m$) for white PM, and $(5.15 + 1.44\ln m)/(4.75 + \ln m)$ less $0.11/m$ (even) or $0.40/m$ (odd) for flicker PM. $B = 1$ at $m = 1$, where the estimate is the Hadamard deviation.
  • :mhtot (modified Hadamard total against modified Hadamard): $B = 0.900$, 0.822, 0.792, 0.783, 0.787, 0.800 and 0.820 for white PM to random-run FM from $m = 16$, the exact value at each $m = 2, …, 15$, and 124/225 at $m = 1$.

A noise type not listed for a var_type, or any other var_type, gives $B = 1$.

The PM values of :totvar and :htot, every value below $m = 16$ and all of :mhtot have no published source: they are the expectation of each statistic, computed in this package (see Total estimators on PM noise and MHTOTDEV bias and EDF). published=true leaves them out. It gives $B = 1$ on PM for :totvar and :htot, and the published value of :mtot and :htot at every $m > 1$. :mhtot is unchanged.

SP1065 Table 11

SP1065 §5.12 lists modified total bias factors of 1.06 to 1.31 and describes them as the ratio of the expected modified total variance to the modified Allan variance. The estimator reads low, so that ratio is below 1, and those values are not used.

SigmaTau.confidence_intervals — Function
confidence_intervals(devs, edfs, confidence) → (lower, upper)

χ² confidence limits of each deviation $σ$ from its equivalent degrees of freedom $ν$ (SP1065 §5.3.2):

\[σ\sqrt{\frac{ν}{χ^2_{(1+p)/2}(ν)}} \quad\text{and}\quad σ\sqrt{\frac{ν}{χ^2_{(1-p)/2}(ν)}} ,\]

where $p$ is confidence and $χ^2_q(ν)$ is the $q$ quantile of the χ² distribution with $ν$ degrees of freedom. Every $ν > 0$ has an interval, however wide. A limit is NaN where the deviation is not finite or $ν$ is not positive.

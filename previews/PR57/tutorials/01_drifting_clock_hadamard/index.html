Characterizing a drifting clock with the Hadamard family

A rubidium standard or GPSDO in its first weeks after power-on has linear frequency drift on top of its white-FM noise floor while the physics package settles toward thermal equilibrium; drift at the 1e-10-per-day level is common. On such a record ADEV reports the drift, not the clock: the deterministic ramp dominates the random noise at long τ. The Hadamard family is designed for this case.

This tutorial synthesizes one day of 1 Hz data (calibrated WHFM noise plus a linear frequency drift), shows the drift ramp in adev and its removal in hdev, introduces mhdev and htdev, and compares mhdev with mhtotdev on a three-hour record.

using SigmaTau
using Random

One day of a drifting clock

noise_gen synthesizes a calibrated power-law mixture; here a single component, white frequency modulation (α = 0) at σy(1 s) = 1e-11, a typical rubidium / GPSDO level. `noisegenhas no drift parameter, because deterministic drift is not noise; the termD \, t` is added to the fractional-frequency series directly.

Random.seed!(20260609)

τ₀ = 1.0                    # 1 Hz sampling
N  = 86_400                 # one day of data
σ1 = 1.0e-11                # WHFM level: σ_y(τ = 1 s)
D  = 2.0e-10 / 86_400       # drift rate, 2e-10 per day, in 1/s

fd_noise = noise_gen(FrequencyData, N, τ₀; sigma1 = Dict(0 => σ1))

t  = (0:N-1) .* τ₀
y  = fd_noise.y .+ D .* t   # drift injected explicitly
fd = FrequencyData(y, τ₀)
FrequencyData{Float64}(86400 samples, τ₀=1 s)

We use one octave-spaced τ grid, 1 s to 8192 s (about 2.3 h), for every estimator in this tutorial, so the curves can be compared point for point. (With no grid argument each function picks its own octave grid up to its algorithmic τ_max, which differs between kernels.)

ADEV: the drift ramp

For a pure linear frequency drift, the Allan deviation is

\[\sigma_y(\tau) \;=\; \frac{D\,\tau}{\sqrt{2}},\]

where $D$ is the drift rate in fractional frequency per second and $\tau$ the averaging time. The second difference of phase does not annihilate a term linear in $t$ in $y(t)$; it passes it through as a residual proportional to $\tau$, so the drift appears as a rising τ⁺¹ ramp. The ramp crosses the WHFM floor $\sigma_1 \tau^{-1/2}$ where the two terms are equal:

τ_cross = (sqrt(2) * σ1 / D)^(2 / 3)
println("drift overtakes the WHFM floor near τ ≈ ", round(τ_cross; digits = 1), " s")
drift overtakes the WHFM floor near τ ≈ 334.2 s
r_adev = adev(fd, m_values)

using Plots

plot(r_adev;
     label  = "ADEV",
     xlabel = "Averaging time τ (s)",
     ylabel = "σ_y(τ)",
     title  = "ADEV of a drifting clock: the ramp masks the floor",
     legend = :bottomleft,
     lw     = 1.5)
plot!(taus, σ1 ./ sqrt.(taus); ls = :dash, label = "WHFM floor σ₁ τ^(-1/2)")
plot!(taus, D .* taus ./ sqrt(2); ls = :dot, label = "drift ramp Dτ/√2")

Past the crossover the curve tracks the drift line, not the clock's noise. Stable32 users handle this by detrending first (the Drift function) and re-running; the Hadamard family makes that step unnecessary.

HDEV: the third difference removes the drift

hdev replaces ADEV's second difference of phase with a third difference. A linear term in $y(t)$ is quadratic in phase, and a third difference annihilates any quadratic, so constant frequency drift contributes exactly zero without a detrending pass over the data.

r_hdev = hdev(fd, m_values)

plot(r_adev;
     label  = "ADEV",
     xlabel = "Averaging time τ (s)",
     ylabel = "σ_y(τ)",
     title  = "ADEV vs HDEV on the same drifting record",
     legend = :bottomleft,
     lw     = 1.5)
plot!(r_hdev; label = "HDEV", lw = 1.5)
plot!(taus, σ1 ./ sqrt.(taus); ls = :dash, label = "WHFM floor σ₁ τ^(-1/2)")

HDEV follows the τ⁻¹/² noise floor across the whole grid; the clock's noise is recovered without modifying the data.

MHDEV and HTDEV

mhdev adds an m-point phase average inside the third-difference window, as MDEV does inside ADEV's. The averaging changes the WHPM slope from −1 to −3/2 while leaving FLPM at −1, so MHDEV distinguishes white PM from flicker PM where HDEV (like ADEV) cannot, and it keeps the third difference's drift immunity. On this WHFM-dominated record MHDEV parallels HDEV; it matters on records where a drifting clock also has short-τ PM noise of either type.

htdev is the time-deviation analog: it rescales MHDEV by

\[\sigma_{x,\mathrm{HT}}(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\tau}{\sqrt{10/3}}\,\sigma_{y,\mathrm{MH}}(\tau),\]

where $\sigma_{y,\mathrm{MH}}$ is MHDEV. HTDEV has units of seconds (a σ_x quantity) and is to MHDEV what TDEV is to MDEV: a time-error summary built on the drift-immune third-difference kernel. It is original to this package; SP1065, IEEE 1139-2022 and NBS TN-1337 do not define it.

r_htdev = htdev(fd, m_values)

println("max |HTDEV − τ/√(10/3) · MHDEV| = ",
        maximum(abs.(r_htdev.dev .- r_mhdev.dev .* r_mhdev.tau ./ sqrt(10 / 3))))
max |HTDEV − τ/√(10/3) · MHDEV| = 2.5849394142282115e-26

Short records: MHTOTDEV

Suppose the record stops after three hours, a warm-up check before a deployment. At the longest reachable τ, mhdev has few independent third differences left and its χ² interval is wide. mhtotdev extends each analysis subsegment by detrended reflection, keeping every subsegment in play at long τ and raising the equivalent degrees of freedom. MHTOTDEV is original to this package, so its bias correction and EDF model were measured by Monte Carlo (valid for τ/τ₀ ≥ 16); see MHTOTDEV bias and EDF.

The total kernel is not drift-immune by itself (the boundary extension re-admits residual drift), so mhtotdev removes the record's least-squares frequency drift first (remove_drift = true). For a fair comparison the record is detrended once here, so that mhdev's noise identification also sees the underlying noise rather than the drift:

N_short  = 10_800   # three hours at 1 Hz
fd_short = detrend(FrequencyData(y[1:N_short], τ₀))   # detrended once, for the mhdev comparison
m_short  = [16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048]   # all ≥ the τ/τ₀ ≥ 16 floor

r_mh  = mhdev(fd_short, m_short)
r_mht = mhtotdev(fd_short, m_short)

From τ = 16 s through τ = 1024 s the extension raises the equivalent degrees of freedom by about 30 % and tightens the relative confidence interval accordingly. The gain vanishes at the kernel's reach limit (mhtotdev needs at least one subsegment, N − 4m + 1 ≥ 1, so τ cannot exceed T/4 for a record of duration T): at the last octave both estimators are down to an EDF of 2 to 3, and no extension recovers much independent information that close to the record length. The relative gain is uniform, so it matters most where the unextended interval is widest, the last usable decade. Plotted with CI bands:

plot(r_mh;

     lw      = 1.5)

Which estimator, when

All four Hadamard-family estimators reject linear frequency drift. Within the family:

SymptomReach for
Drift contaminates ADEV at long τhdev
Drift plus PM noise of unknown type (WHPM or FLPM) at short τmhdev
Time-error budget (σ_x) for a drifting clockhtdev
Drifting clock on a record too short for long-τ confidencemhtotdev

For records without drift the Allan-family counterparts (adev, mdev, tdev, mtotdev) are slightly more efficient (tighter CI per τ on the same record), so the Hadamard family is the tool for the drifting clock, not the default for every clock.

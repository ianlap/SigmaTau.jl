Total estimators on PM noise

The published bias and EDF tables of TOTDEV and HTOTDEV stop at white FM: TOTDEV's cover white, flicker and random-walk FM (Greenhall et al., 1999), (Riley, Jul 2008), HTOTDEV's white FM to random-run FM (Howe and Peppler, 2001). On white PM and flicker PM both estimators read high, and the EDF of their unextended parents is the wrong size for them. SigmaTau corrects both from the exact moments of the two statistics, computed by tools/total_pm_models.jl with no random numbers, in under a minute.

Method

Both statistics are quadratic forms x′Qx in the phase record. For Gaussian noise with phase covariance Σ = FF′,

\[E = \operatorname{tr}(F'QF), \qquad \mathrm{edf} = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(F'QF)^2}{\lVert F'QF\rVert^2} ,\]

the EDF of Greenhall and Riley (Greenhall and Riley, 2003). The bias factor B is E over the expectation of the parent, AVAR or HVAR, on the same noise. The tool builds Q from the definitions; the test suite extracts it from the package kernels and holds the shipped models to the same moments.

White PM is Σ = I. Flicker PM has no unique discrete form, and two are used:

  • the sampled process, a continuous 1/f phase noise read every τ₀, whose structure function D(k) is twice the second central difference of t² ln t at t = k. This is the model behind the Greenhall–Riley EDF of every other estimator in the package.
  • the filtered process, white noise through the half-order filter of noise_gen.

The two agree on the bias to 2 % from m = 16. On the EDF they do not: the filtered process has less power near the Nyquist frequency and up to 22 % more degrees of freedom. The shipped EDF follows the sampled process, as the rest of the package does, so on noise_gen flicker PM it reads up to 17 % low and the interval is up to 9 % wider than it need be.

TOTVAR reads high in proportion to τ/T, as it reads low on flicker and random-walk FM: 33 % in variance on white PM and 24 % on flicker PM at τ = T/4. The odd reflection about each end puts the end sample, doubled, into every extended term.

TOTHVAR's bias does not depend on the record length, as on FM. It is large at every τ: the variance reads 25 % high on white PM at m = 2, 37 % at m = 16 and 38 % beyond; on flicker PM 4 to 11 % at m = 2, 21 % at m = 16 and 28 % at m = 256. On PM the half-average slope removed from each 3m-point subsequence is set by three or four phase samples, and its error is as large as the noise being measured. Odd and even m follow different curves because the two half-averages share the middle phase sample only when 3m is even.

The white PM forms are within 0.1 % of the exact value at every m. The flicker PM forms are fitted to the mean of the two flicker models and are within 1.0 % of either for TOTVAR through τ = T/4 (1.6 % at T/2), and from m = 16 for TOTHVAR. Below that the two models part, and at m = 2 the TOTHVAR form is 3.5 % from each.

Here g(m) = m / (0.789 + 0.276 ln m + 0.204 ln² m) is N/edf of overlapping AVAR on sampled flicker PM for N ≫ m. In the white PM form, 140N is the interior of the record (second differences with covariances 6, −4, 1) and 64m² the samples shared through the reflected ends; 225m is fitted.

For TOTHVAR, g is 2.45 on white PM and g(m) on flicker PM, and (c, s₀, a, b) = (2.0, 0.125, 1.0, 0.03) and (2.5, 0.16, 0.6, 0.04). The constants were set by hand against the exact EDF at N = 512, 1024 and 2048. Residuals of the shipped EDF, low to high:

On white PM the reflected ends of TOTVAR are a few shared samples that dominate the variance at long τ, so its interval was far too narrow; TOTHVAR has about twice the degrees of freedom of HVAR, so its interval was too wide.

When the label is wrong

Stable32

Stable32 applies no bias factor on PM, so on rows labelled white or flicker PM the default TOTDEV and HTOTDEV are lower than its values, HTOTDEV by 10 to 15 %. correct_bias=:stable32 (or :published) leaves the PM rows uncorrected and matches Stable32's values there.

MHTOTDEV bias and EDF

MHTOTDEV (modified Hadamard total deviation) is original to SigmaTau, and no published reference gives its bias factor or its equivalent degrees of freedom (EDF). Every other total estimator takes a published table: TOTDEV from SP1065 (Riley, Jul 2008), MTOTDEV and HTOTDEV from Howe and Peppler (Howe and Peppler, 2001). For MHTOTDEV both are computed here, exactly, from the definition of the statistic. The computation is tools/mhtot_models.jl; it uses no random numbers and runs in about five minutes.

The statistic

For each window of 4m phase samples, MHTOTVAR removes the window's least-squares quadratic, extends it by mirror reflection at both ends to 12m samples, and averages the squared m-averaged third difference over the 8m + 1 positions of the extension. The record's value is the mean over all N − 4m + 1 windows.

The quadratic removal matches the third difference, which annihilates phase polynomials up to degree 2. It makes the statistic immune to a linear frequency drift on its own and lets it converge down to random-run FM (α = −4), like HDEV and MHDEV. The reference it is compared with is MHVAR, the square of mhdev, at the same averaging factor.

Method

Every step above is linear in the phase, so a window's statistic is a quadratic form w′Qw, and the record's is x′Q̄x with Q̄ the mean of the window forms. For Gaussian noise with phase covariance Σ,

\[E[\mathrm{MHTOTVAR}] = \operatorname{tr}(\bar Q\,\Sigma), \qquad \mathrm{edf} = \frac{2\,E^2}{\mathrm{Var}} = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\bar Q\,\Sigma)^2}{\operatorname{tr}\big((\bar Q\,\Sigma)^2\big)} ,\]

the same definition of the EDF as Greenhall and Riley use for the unextended estimators (Greenhall and Riley, 2003). Q annihilates quadratics, so any valid covariance of the samples gives the same value for every noise down to α = −4. The tool builds Σ = FF′ with F the response to white noise: the identity for white PM, Kasdin's half-order filter for flicker PM, and one cumulative sum for each step of 2 down in α.

The tool builds its matrices from the definition and never calls the package kernel. The test suite closes the loop the other way: it extracts F′Q̄F from the kernel itself and holds the shipped models to its exact moments.

MHTOTDEV reads low on every noise: removing a quadratic from each window absorbs part of that window's variance before the reflection. bias_correction divides by the exact value at each m from 2 to 15 and by the large-m constant in the last column from m = 16, where it is within 0.4 %. White PM dips at odd m, to 0.858 at m = 3. The flicker rows are the mean of the two flicker models of Total estimators on PM noise, which are within 1.3 % of it at m = 2 and 0.5 % from m = 3. At m = 1 the detrended four-point window leaves a single degree of freedom and B = 124/225 on every noise.

EDF

The exact EDF, computed for records of 512, 1024 and 2048 points, is a function of s = τ/T to two digits. The model is

\[\mathrm{edf} = \frac{b}{s} - c + d\,s + e\,s^3, \qquad s = \tau/T,\quad T = (N-1)\,\tau_0 ,\]

fitted by least squares on the relative residual over 16 ≤ m ≤ N/4, the three record lengths together:

The first two terms are the form Howe and Peppler use for the modified total variance (Howe and Peppler, 2001). They are not enough here: the exact EDF turns upward in the last octave, where the reflection of the few remaining windows does the most work, and two terms that fit the range below T/16 give about half the exact EDF at τ = T/4. The terms in s and s³ carry that rise.

At the reach limit τ = T/4 a single window is left. MHVAR has one degree of freedom there; MHTOTVAR has 4.0 on white PM, 2.7 on flicker PM, 2.2 on white FM, 1.9 on flicker FM, 1.6 on random-walk FM, 1.4 on flicker-walk FM and 1.2 on random-run FM.

Below m = 16

The model is fitted from m = 16 and is used down to m = 2. On the FM noises it is within 6 % of the exact EDF from m = 3 and reads 5 to 13 % low, the safe side, at m = 2. On flicker PM it reads 4 to 10 % high at m = 2 and 3, depending on the flicker model, and is within 4 % from m = 4. On white PM it would read high, by 5 % at m = 8, 15 % at m = 4, 35 % at m = 3 and 54 % at m = 2, so there the model is multiplied by the exact ratio at each m from 2 to 15 (0.65 at m = 2, 0.75 at m = 3, 0.88 at m = 4, 0.97 at m = 8).

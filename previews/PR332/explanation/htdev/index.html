The Hadamard time deviation

htdev is defined in this package: SP1065 and IEEE Std 1139-2022 give a time deviation for the modified Allan deviation only. This page derives the factor $\sqrt{10/3}$ of its definition from the rule that gives TDEV its $\sqrt{3}$, and shows what the two time deviations measure as errors of prediction.

Differences of averages

Let $\bar x(t)$ be the phase averaged over $τ = mτ_0$, the mean of $m$ consecutive samples starting at $t$, with second and third differences at spacing $τ$

\[\begin{aligned} Δ^2\bar x(t) &= \bar x(t+2τ) - 2\bar x(t+τ) + \bar x(t), \\ Δ^3\bar x(t) &= \bar x(t+3τ) - 3\bar x(t+2τ) + 3\bar x(t+τ) - \bar x(t). \end{aligned}\]

The modified Allan variance sums the second difference of single samples over $m$ adjacent starts (SP1065 §5.2.5, Eq. (14)), and that sum divided by $m$ is $Δ^2\bar x$. The modified Hadamard variance does the same with the third difference (SP1065 §5.2.10, Eq. (23)). With $\langle\cdot\rangle$ the mean over every start the record allows, the estimators of mdev and mhdev are therefore

\[\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_y^2(τ) = \frac{\langle (Δ^2\bar x)^2 \rangle}{2τ^2}, \qquad \mathrm{Mod}\,σ_H^2(τ) = \frac{\langle (Δ^3\bar x)^2 \rangle}{6τ^2} .\]

The factor 10/3

The time variance is $σ_x^2(τ) = τ^2\,\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_y^2(τ)/3$ (SP1065 §5.2.6, Eq. (15)), which is $\langle (Δ^2\bar x)^2 \rangle / 6$. The 3 comes from white PM. With variance $σ^2$ per sample, averages that do not overlap are independent with variance $σ^2/m$, and the coefficients $(1, -2, 1)$ of the second difference have squares that sum to 6:

\[\langle (Δ^2\bar x)^2 \rangle = \frac{6σ^2}{m}, \qquad \mathrm{Mod}\,σ_y^2(τ) = \frac{3σ^2}{mτ^2}, \qquad σ_x^2(τ) = \frac{σ^2}{m} .\]

The 3 is what the variance's own 2 leaves of the 6. Dividing it out makes TDEV on white PM the standard deviation of the $τ$-average of the phase (SP1065 §5.2.6).

The third difference has coefficients $(-1, 3, -3, 1)$, whose squares sum to 20:

\[\langle (Δ^3\bar x)^2 \rangle = \frac{20σ^2}{m}, \qquad \mathrm{Mod}\,σ_H^2(τ) = \frac{10}{3}\,\frac{σ^2}{mτ^2} .\]

So 10/3 takes the place of TDEV's 3, and the Hadamard time variance is

\[σ_{x,H}^2(τ) = \frac{τ^2\,\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_H^2(τ)}{10/3} = \frac{\langle (Δ^3\bar x)^2 \rangle}{20} .\]

HTDEV is then $σ/\sqrt{m}$ on white PM, as TDEV is, and the two can be read on one plot. On a drifting clock they part: a linear frequency drift is a quadratic in the phase, which the third difference removes and the second does not.

The same rule holds at any difference order $d$. The variance divides by $d!$ (SP1065 §5.2.10, Eq. (24)), the squared binomial coefficients sum to $\binom{2d}{d}$, and the time form divides by $\binom{2d}{d}/d!$, which is 3 at $d = 2$ and 10/3 at $d = 3$.

Two prediction errors

Each difference is a value less a prediction of it:

\[\begin{aligned} Δ^2\bar x(t) &= \bar x(t+2τ) - \bigl[\,2\bar x(t+τ) - \bar x(t)\,\bigr], \\ Δ^3\bar x(t) &= \bar x(t+3τ) - \bigl[\,3\bar x(t+2τ) - 3\bar x(t+τ) + \bar x(t)\,\bigr]. \end{aligned}\]

The first bracket is the line through two successive averages, carried one step on. The second is the parabola through three. With the two time variances above,

\[\mathrm{rms}\,Δ^2\bar x = \sqrt{6}\;σ_x(τ), \qquad \mathrm{rms}\,Δ^3\bar x = \sqrt{20}\;σ_{x,H}(τ) = \sqrt{6}\;τ\,\mathrm{Mod}\,σ_H(τ) .\]

$\sqrt{6}$ TDEV is the RMS error of predicting the next $τ$-average of the phase with the frequency learned from the two before it, and $\sqrt{20}$ HTDEV the error with the frequency and the drift learned from the three before it. The second needs no factor beyond the modified Hadamard variance's own 6. Holdover and the time deviation compares both with the measured holdover test, which predicts single readings from a least-squares fit.

In numbers

On white PM of 1 ns per sample, both time deviations follow $σ/\sqrt{m}$, here in nanoseconds:

using SigmaTau, Random

p = PhaseData(1e-9 .* randn(Xoshiro(1), 100_000), 1.0)
m = [1, 4, 16, 64]
round.(1e9 .* [1e-9 ./ sqrt.(m) tdev(p, m; ci = false).dev htdev(p, m; ci = false).dev];
       digits = 4)
4×3 Matrix{Float64}:
 1.0    0.9987  0.9976
 0.5    0.4996  0.4994
 0.25   0.2498  0.2496
 0.125  0.1283  0.1285

The third difference of the averages, over $\sqrt{20}$, is HTDEV on any record:

x̄ = [sum(p.x[j:j+15]) / 16 for j in 1:length(p.x)-15]
Δ³ = x̄[49:end] .- 3 .* x̄[33:end-16] .+ 3 .* x̄[17:end-32] .- x̄[1:end-48]
sqrt(sum(abs2, Δ³) / 20length(Δ³)) ≈ htdev(p, [16]; ci = false).dev[1]

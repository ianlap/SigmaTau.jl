Coming from allantools

allantools and SigmaTau implement the same SP1065 estimators; on the shipped fixture their raw deviations agree to 1e-13 or better (Validation). SigmaTau adds noise identification and confidence intervals to every call. This page maps allantools calls onto SigmaTau from Python or Julia, and lists where the numbers differ.

The call

allantools takes an array, a sample rate and τ in seconds, and returns four arrays. SigmaTau takes a typed record with its τ₀ and averaging factors m (τ = m·τ₀), and returns one result:

import allantools
taus, dev, err, n = allantools.oadev(x, rate=10.0, data_type="phase", taus="octave")

import sigmatau as st
r = st.adev(st.PhaseData(x, tau0=0.1))          # Octave grid by default
r.tau, r.dev, r.ci_lower, r.ci_upper, r.noise_type, r.edf
using SigmaTau
r = adev(PhaseData(x, 0.1))
allantoolsSigmaTau
data_type="phase" / "freq"PhaseData(x, tau0) / FrequencyData(y, tau0)

taus="octave", "decade", "all"Octave (default), Decade, AllTaus; also HalfOctave, QuarterOctave, HalfDecade
taus=[1.0, 10.0] (s)m=[10, 100] in Python, adev(p, [10, 100]) in Julia (factors of τ₀)

Function names

allantools' plain adev and hdev are the non-overlapping estimators; SigmaTau's are overlapping (IEEE 1139), so the names shift:

SigmaTau also has mhdev, htdev, mhtotdev, theoh, pdev, the dynamic deviations, Welch spectra and powerlaw_fit, none of which allantools provides.

Validation compares the two at every τ of the fixture with these settings.

Reading files

allantools reads arrays you load yourself. SigmaTau reads phase and frequency text files, Stable32 files, TimeLab exports and counter logs directly, with their τ₀ and time columns:

p = st.read_data("clock.dat")                 # or read_phase / read_frequency

Reading and saving data covers the formats, scaling to seconds, detrending and gap filling.

Validation

The results of this package rest on five kinds of evidence, described below. The second half of the page follows one record through a comparison with Stable32 and allantools at each averaging time. The cross-check report lists every comparison the test suite asserts, with its tolerance and the largest difference found.

Other programs

Related software describes the programs. allantools and AllanDeviations.jl compute in double precision, and the suite requires the deviations to agree with theirs within $10^{-9}$ ($10^{-8}$ for the modified Allan and time deviations). Stable32 prints five significant digits, and the suite requires agreement with its result files within $10^{-4}$ for the deviations and $10^{-3}$ for the confidence limits. The comparisons run on seven power-law records of 4096 points, from white PM to random-run FM, on the test record that Stable32 distributes and on a flicker PM record from its noise generator.

Where two programs follow different conventions the suite compares like with like. Against allantools, which returns the total deviations uncorrected, it calls mtotdev with correct_bias=false. The report states each convention. The differences that remain, such as the EDF each program gives the total deviations at short averaging times, are listed in the report and not asserted.

A continuous-integration job installs allantools 2024.6 and AllanDeviations.jl 0.3.0, regenerates their tables and repeats the comparison on every pull request, so that the stored tables cannot drift from the programs.

Published values

SP1065 §12.4 defines a 1000-point frequency record by a random number generator (its Eq. (73)) and lists its deviations in Table 31. The suite generates the record and reproduces the overlapping Allan, modified Allan, time, overlapping Hadamard, total and Hadamard total deviations of the table within $3 × 10^{-7}$, the resolution of the seven digits printed.

Definitions

test/stab/reference_kernels.jl holds an implementation of each variance written directly from its definition, sharing no code with the package. The optimized kernels agree with it within $10^{-12}$ on a 4096-point record of white PM and random-walk FM. For the estimators that no other program computes (mhdev, htdev, mhtotdev) this is the primary test.

Exact moments

A squared deviation is a quadratic form in the phase samples, so its mean and its variance on Gaussian noise of a given spectrum follow exactly, without simulation. Total estimators on PM noise and MHTOTDEV bias and EDF derive bias factors and EDF models this way, with tools that do not call the package. The tests work in the other direction: they extract the quadratic form from each kernel and hold the bias factors to its exact mean and the EDF models to its exact EDF.

Simulation

None of the four says how a default call behaves on noise of unknown type. The Background pages therefore give, in simulation, how often the noise identification is right (Noise identification), how much bias a default call leaves (Bias correction) and how often the interval holds the true value (Confidence intervals). Each statement gives the record length and the number of records. These runs are not part of the test suite.

One record in detail

test/fixtures/validation/stable32gen.DAT is a phase record of 8192 points at 1 s, in Stable32's file format. White PM dominates up to about 250 s, flicker FM and random-walk FM beyond. The fixtures hold the tables that Stable32 and allantools computed from it (stable32out/stable32_data_full.csv, allantools_out/allantools_data_full.csv).

Noise type

The EDF, and for the total deviations the bias factor, depend on the noise type, so the comparison starts there. Stable32 prints the same noise type for every overlapping estimator at a given averaging time:

This packagewhite PMflicker PMwhite FMwhite FMflicker FMflicker FMrandom-walk FM
Hadamard family, defaultwhite PMflicker PMwhite FMwhite FMflicker FMflicker FMflicker FM

The programs differ at three places.

  • 256 s. With 32 averaged points this is the longest averaging time at which both use the lag-1 autocorrelation. Stable32 reads white PM and this package white FM. identify_noise with remove_drift=false, which leaves the averaged series as it is, reads flicker PM. The Allan deviation falls as $τ^{-0.85}$ from 128 s to 256 s and as $τ^{-0.5}$ from 256 s to 512 s.
  • 1024 s and beyond. With fewer than 30 averaged points both programs turn to the $B_1$ ratio. This package compares it with $B_1(N, μ)$ at the number of averaged points and reads flicker FM at 1024 s and random-walk FM beyond; the Allan deviation is flat from 512 s to 1024 s and rises by a factor 1.47 to 2048 s. identify_noise with remove_drift=false and b1_length=:record returns white FM there, as Stable32 does. The labels at 2048 s and 4096 s come from six averaged points, the fewest the ratio is computed on (Noise identification).
  • The Hadamard family. By default hdev, htotdev and their modified forms identify the noise on the record less its frequency drift, and there without the $B_1$ ratio. From 512 s on, where fewer than 30 averaged points remain, their label is the one found at 273 s, the longest averaging time that has 30: flicker FM, to the end of their tables at 2048 s. With remove_drift=false they use the labels of the second row. The Hadamard deviation is the same either way. Its default interval at 2048 s is $[2.08, 6.64] × 10^{-3}$, and Stable32's, on white FM, $[2.13, 5.49] × 10^{-3}$.

Confidence limits

Both programs compute the EDF of the Allan and Hadamard families by the algorithm of Greenhall and Riley (2003), which Stable32 has used since version 1.41 according to that paper, and both take χ² limits. With Stable32's noise types stated, the 68.3 % limits differ by at most:

From $16τ_0$ the EDF that Stable32 gives the modified total deviation follows no published table (cross-check report). In a default call the limits also move wherever the noise type differs: at 256 s, where this package reads white FM, its interval for the Allan deviation is $[4.10, 5.06] × 10^{-3}$ against Stable32's $[4.45, 4.55] × 10^{-3}$.

Thêo1 with the bias removed

Stable32's ThêoH table for this record is in the fixtures. Up to 1024 s it gives the Allan deviation, as theoh does. Beyond, it gives the bias-removed Thêo1 at 1536 s and 3072 s. theo1 with correct_bias = :stable32 agrees with it to the five digits printed, and theo1 by default is 1.8 % lower:

The 1.8 % come from the samples used. Both take the ratio of the Allan variance to Thêo1 over averaging times up to half the record (SP1065 Eq. (33); IEEE Std 1139-2022, Eq. (A.37)). The default takes it from all 8192 samples, and Stable32 from at most 3000. For this record correct_bias = :stable32 takes every third sample and adds the term of Taylor and Howe (2010), Eq. (4) for it, 0.0011 in the ratio, which they derived for samples averaged in blocks. That Stable32 takes every third sample is inferred from this agreement: averages of three give values 6.5 % higher. On the 1001-point record of the cross-check report, which needs no such step, the agreement holds at each of 181 averaging factors, and the default is within 0.1 % of it.

correct_bias = :published is lower by a factor 2.4. The Allan deviation is $3.14 × 10^{-3}$ at 1024 s and $4.61 × 10^{-3}$ at 2048 s. The default values continue it, and those of :published do not: its 68.3 % interval at 1536 s, $[1.35, 2.59] × 10^{-3}$, lies below both.

The cause is the range over which the ratio is averaged. :published follows Howe (2006), Eq. (6) and averages it up to a tenth of the record, 819 s here, where PM noise dominates and the ratio is 0.2 to 0.7. Its mean is 0.355. Up to half the record FM noise dominates and the ratio reaches 3.4, and the mean over that range is 2.05. Either mean is then applied at every averaging time. The correction assumes one noise type over the whole range, and this record does not have one (Bias correction gives the size of the error in simulation, for both ranges).

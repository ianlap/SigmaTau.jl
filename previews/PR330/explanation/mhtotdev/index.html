MHTOTDEV bias and EDF

The modified Hadamard total deviation, mhtotdev, is the total form of the modified Hadamard deviation. It is constructed in this package. Vernotte and Howe (2000) outline a total form of the same third-difference variance, with a linear trend removed from each subsequence and a quadratic from the whole record, and give no bias or EDF for it. No published table covers the estimator defined here, so its bias factor and its EDF model are derived on this page, exactly, from the definition.

The computation is tools/mhtot_models.jl. It uses no random numbers and runs in about five minutes.

The statistic

For each window of $4m$ phase samples the estimator

  1. removes the least-squares quadratic of the window,
  2. extends the window at both ends by even reflection, to $12m$ samples,
  3. averages the squared third difference of $m$-sample phase means over the $8m + 1$ positions of the extension.

The variance is the mean over the $N - 4m + 1$ windows, divided by $6τ^2$.

The third difference annihilates a phase polynomial of degree 2, and the quadratic removed in step 1 is that polynomial. The estimate is therefore insensitive to frequency offset and to linear frequency drift, and it converges for every noise type down to random-run FM, like the Hadamard and modified Hadamard deviations. Its parent, the variance it is compared with, is the modified Hadamard variance, the square of mhdev, at the same averaging factor.

Method

Each step is linear in the phase. The statistic of one window is a quadratic form in its samples, and that of the record is $x^\mathsf{T}\bar{Q}x$, with $\bar{Q}$ the mean of the window forms. For Gaussian noise whose phase has covariance $Σ$,

\[E = \operatorname{tr}(\bar{Q}Σ), \qquad ν = \frac{2E^2}{\mathrm{Var}} = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\bar{Q}Σ)^2}{\operatorname{tr}\big((\bar{Q}Σ)^2\big)} ,\]

with the EDF as defined by Greenhall and Riley (2003), Eq. (1). Because $\bar{Q}$ annihilates quadratics, any valid covariance of the samples gives the same value, for every noise type down to random-run FM. The tool takes $Σ = F F^\mathsf{T}$, where $F$ is the response to white noise: the identity for white PM, the half-order filter of Kasdin and Walter (1992) for flicker PM, and one cumulative sum for each step of 2 down in $α$.

The tool builds its matrices from the definition and does not call the package. The test suite works in the other direction: it extracts the form from the package kernel and holds the models to its exact moments. Applied to the modified Hadamard variance itself, the same computation reproduces the EDF of the Greenhall and Riley algorithm within 0.3 % on the four noise types with even $α$, and within 0.7 % on the flicker types, where the algorithm assumes the other flicker model.

The estimator reads low on every noise type, by 10 to 22 % in variance: the quadratic removed from each window takes part of the window's variance with it. bias_correction returns the exact value at each $m$ from 2 to 15 and the constant of the last column from $m = 16$, where the two differ by less than 0.3 %. At $m = 1$ the window has four samples, and the quadratic leaves one degree of freedom. The variance is then 124/225 of the modified Hadamard variance term for term, on any record.

EDF

The exact EDF depends on the record length and the averaging factor through $s = τ/T$ alone, within 1 % for records of 512, 1024 and 2048 points. The model is

\[ν = \frac{b}{s} - c + d\,s + e\,s^3, \qquad s = \frac{τ}{T}, \quad T = (N-1)τ_0 ,\]

fitted by least squares on the relative residual over $16 ≤ m ≤ N/4$, the three record lengths together:

The first two terms are the form of the total variance EDF (Greenhall et al. (1999), Eq. (26)). They are not enough here. The exact EDF turns upward in the last octave, where the reflection does the most for the few windows that remain, and two terms fitted below $T/16$ give a third to a half of the exact EDF at $τ = T/4$. The terms in $s$ and $s^3$ carry the rise.

At $τ = T/4$, the longest averaging time, one window is left. The modified Hadamard variance has one degree of freedom there. The modified Hadamard total variance has 4.0 on white PM, 2.7 on flicker PM, 2.2 on white FM, 1.9 on flicker FM, 1.6 on random-walk FM, 1.4 on flicker-walk FM and 1.2 on random-run FM.

Below m = 16

The model is fitted from $m = 16$ and used from $m = 2$. On the FM noises it is within 6 % of the exact EDF from $m = 3$ and 4 to 13 % low at $m = 2$, which widens the interval. On flicker PM it is 2 to 10 % high at $m = 2$ and 3, depending on the flicker model, and within 4 % from $m = 4$. On white PM the fitted form would be high by 53 % at $m = 2$, 34 % at $m = 3$, 14 % at $m = 4$ and 3 % at $m = 8$, so it is multiplied by the exact ratio at each $m$ from 2 to 15, which brings it within 1.4 %. At $m = 1$ the EDF is that of mhdev.

Accuracy in simulation

In simulation (1024 points, 2000 records per noise type) with the noise type stated, the corrected variance averages within 2 % of the modified Hadamard variance up to $τ = T/8$ and within 4 % at $T/4$, on all seven noise types, and the 68.3 % interval holds the true value in 65 to 77 % of records (Bias correction, Confidence intervals).

Steering

The Steering page replays a steering loop on the record. The record is the clock running free against its reference, and the page shows what the loop would have made of it. A Kalman filter estimates the phase and the frequency of the clock from the readings, and each steer changes the frequency of the clock by a gain times each estimate. The loop is that of steer, which Steering describes.

Settings

SettingEffect
Gains fromTime constant, the gains of a critically damped loop with that time constant, by pole placement, or Cost, the gains that minimize a weighted sum of phase², frequency² and steer²
Time constantIn seconds. Empty, it is 1/128 of the record, the value that the field shows in gray
Integral gainA third gain, on the running sum of the estimated phase
Weights onWith Cost, the weights on phase², frequency² and steer². Those on phase² and steer² are above 0. A larger weight on steer² gives a slower loop with smaller steers
Steer everyA steer at every sample, or at every 2 to 64 samples. The gains are worked out for that interval
Clock model from the first1/8, 1/4 or 1/2 of the record, the stretch to which the clock model of the filter is fitted. The line below gives its length
Drift stateThe model also holds a frequency drift, which the loop steers out ahead
Plotσ(τ) or Record (below)

The clock model is fitted to the first stretch alone and keeps the flicker FM and the flicker PM that the fit finds, as that of the Kalman filter on the Holdover page. The loop runs over the whole record, and every result counts from the end of that stretch, so the loop is not judged on readings that its model was learned from.

The page computes when it is shown and again when the record, its preparation, its analysis range or a setting changes. It needs no run. Of the side panel it takes the τ grid alone. The stretch that the clock model is fitted to needs 65 samples or more, and the loop 16 after it: the record needs 520 samples with the first 1/8, 260 with the first 1/4 and 130 with the first 1/2.

The plot

With σ(τ) the plot gives the Allan deviation against the averaging time, on logarithmic axes.

CurveShows
Free-runningThe Allan deviation of the record
SteeredThat of the steered record
Predicted (clock model)That of the readings of the settled loop as the clock model gives it, the SteeringPrediction of steer(model, gains; readings=true). It is drawn for a steer at every sample

A dotted vertical line marks the longest time constant of the loop.

With Record the plot gives the steered phase over the whole record, and on the right axis the frequency correction in force. A dotted line marks the end of the stretch that the model was fitted to.

The card beside the plot lists the phase gain, the frequency gain and the integral gain if there is one, the time constants of the loop, which come from its poles, and how often it steers. Where the poles are complex, Rings with period gives the period of the overshoot. Below come the RMS of the steered phase and of the steers, each with the value that the model predicts in parentheses, the model itself, and a table of the three deviations at each averaging time.

Reading it

  • Well below the time constant the steered clock follows the free-running one. Well above it the steered deviation falls as $1/τ$, because the phase is held to the reference.
  • A shorter time constant holds the phase closer and steers harder. On the simulated record that the explorer starts with, time constants of 32, 128 and 512 s gave a steered phase of 7, 21 and 91 ps RMS, and steers of 4.9, 1.5 and 0.65 parts in 10¹⁵ RMS.
  • A steered curve above the predicted one says that the clock had more noise there than the model fitted to the first stretch.
  • Gains from a cost with no weight on frequency² give a loop that rings a little, and the card gives the period.
  • A frequency drift that the model does not hold leaves the steered phase off zero. Drift state removes the offset. An integral gain centers the filter's estimate of the phase and not the phase itself, as Steering explains.

Several clocks

With several clocks, the settings move to a bar above the plot, and each clock has one color. With σ(τ) its steered deviation is a solid line and its free-running one a dashed line, and the prediction is not drawn. With Record the plot gives the steered phase of each clock. The card lists the RMS of the steered phase and of the steers of each clock. A clock whose replay cannot be computed is named with the reason on a line above the plot and is left out of the card.

Total estimators on PM noise

The published bias factors and EDF of the total deviation cover white, flicker and random-walk FM (Greenhall et al. (1999), Table I; SP1065 §5.11), and those of the Hadamard total deviation cover the five FM noise types from $τ = 16τ_0$ (Howe et al. (2005), Table I). On white and flicker PM both estimators read high, and neither their own FM tables nor the EDF of their parents describe them. This page derives the factors and the EDF models that the package uses on PM noise, and the exact factors of the modified and Hadamard totals below $16τ_0$.

Every number in the derivation is computed without random numbers by tools/total_pm_models.jl, which runs in under a minute.

Method

A total variance is a quadratic form $x^\mathsf{T} Q x$ in the phase record $x$: the extension, the differences and the average are all linear. For Gaussian noise whose phase has covariance $Σ = F F^\mathsf{T}$, its mean and its EDF are

\[E = \operatorname{tr}(G), \qquad ν = \frac{2E^2}{\mathrm{Var}} = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(G)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(G^2)}, \qquad G = F^\mathsf{T} Q F ,\]

with the EDF as defined by Greenhall and Riley (2003), Eq. (1). The bias factor $B$ is $E$ over the expected value of the parent, the Allan or the Hadamard variance, on the same noise.

The tool builds $Q$ from the definitions of the estimators and does not call the package. The test suite works in the other direction: it extracts $Q$ from the package kernels and holds the models to the same moments.

Two models of flicker PM

White PM has $Σ = I$. Flicker PM has no unique discrete form, and two are used.

  • The sampled process is continuous $1/f$ phase noise averaged over each sampling interval. Its generalized autocovariance at lag $k$ is $-Δ^2[t^2 \ln t](k)$, where $Δ^2$ is the second central difference. It is the model of the EDF algorithm of Greenhall and Riley (2003), which the Allan and Hadamard families use.
  • The filtered process is white noise through the half-order filter of Kasdin and Walter (1992), the model of noise_gen.

On the bias the two agree within 2 % from $m = 16$, and the factors below are fitted to their mean. On the EDF they do not agree: at the same low-frequency level the filtered process has more power near the Nyquist frequency, and 2 to 22 % more degrees of freedom. The EDF models follow the sampled process, like the rest of the package. On records from noise_gen they are therefore up to 17 % low, and the interval is up to 9 % wider than it needs to be.

Total variance. The bias grows in proportion to $τ/T$, as it does with the opposite sign on flicker and random-walk FM. At $τ = T/4$ the variance reads 33 % high on white PM and, for a record of 1024 points, 24 % high on flicker PM. The cause is the extension: the odd reflection about each end puts the end sample, at twice its weight, into every term that reaches past the record. The white PM form is exact. The flicker PM form is within 1.0 % of both flicker models up to $τ = T/2$.

Hadamard total variance. The bias depends on $m$ and not on the record length, as it does on FM noise, and it is large at every $τ$. On white PM the variance reads 25 % high at $m = 2$, 37 % at $m = 16$ and 38 % beyond. On flicker PM it reads 4 % or 11 % high at $m = 2$, depending on the flicker model, 21 % at $m = 16$ and 28 % at $m = 256$. The cause is the drift removal: the slope taken from each subsequence of $3m$ frequency samples is the difference of two half-averages, which three or four phase samples determine, and on PM noise its error is as large as the noise being measured. Odd and even $m$ follow different curves because the half-averages share the middle phase sample only when $3m$ is even.

The white PM forms are within 0.1 % of the exact value at every $m$. The flicker PM form is within 1.0 % of both flicker models from $m = 16$. Below that the models part, and at $m = 2$ the form is 3.3 % above one and 3.7 % below the other.

Averaging factors below 16

The published factors of the modified total and Hadamard total variances hold from $τ = 16τ_0$ (Howe and Peppler (2001), Table 2; Howe et al. (2005), Table I). Below it the expectation depends on $m$, and bias_correction uses the exact ratio at each $m$ from 2 to 15, computed as above.

The Hadamard total variance is the modified total computation applied to the frequency samples in place of the phase samples. One table therefore serves both estimators: its rows are white PM to random-walk FM for the modified total and white FM to random-run FM for the Hadamard total. The ratio does not depend on the record length, so one window gives it. The phase of each FM noise is white or flicker PM summed once or twice, and the flicker rows are the mean of the two flicker models, which lie within 2.4 % of it in the first flicker row and 1.2 % in the second.

The values depart most from the published ones at the smallest $m$. On white PM the modified total factor is 0.938 at $m = 3$, where the published value is 0.995, and on white FM it is 0.814 at $m = 2$ against 0.771 (Bias correction has the table).

Here $g(m) = m/(0.789 + 0.276 \ln m + 0.204 \ln^2 m)$ is $N/ν$ of the overlapping Allan variance on sampled flicker PM for $N ≫ m$, fitted within 0.9 %.

In the white PM form of the total variance the terms have a meaning. The interior of the record contributes $140N$: second differences of white noise have covariances 6, −4 and 1 at lags 0, $m$ and $2m$, and $2(6^2 + 2·4^2 + 2·1^2) = 140$. The end samples shared through the reflections contribute $64m^2$, which is why the EDF falls so fast at long $τ$. The coefficient 225 is fitted.

For the Hadamard total, $g$ is 2.45 on white PM and $g(m)$ on flicker PM, and $(c, s_0, a, b)$ is (2.0, 0.125, 1.0, 0.03) and (2.5, 0.16, 0.6, 0.04). These constants were set by hand against the exact EDF for records of 512 and 1024 points. The model differs from the exact EDF by:

The Hadamard total variance has about twice the degrees of freedom of the Hadamard variance on both PM noises, and the parent's EDF would give an interval too wide.

On white PM the default removes the bias at every averaging time of the table. On flicker PM it removes all but 4 % of it down to 8 averaged points ($m = 128$). With 4 points flicker PM is labeled white FM in most records, and its bias stays. White FM pays up to 6 % in the total variance at the longest averaging times of the table, for the records labeled flicker or random-walk FM. Stating the noise type removes both effects:

htotdev(p; noise = :WHPM)

Other conventions

Stable32 and the published tables apply no factor on PM noise. correct_bias=:published and correct_bias=:stable32 do the same: on the rows labeled white or flicker PM they return the uncorrected deviation. The default is $\sqrt{B}$ times smaller: 10 to 15 % for the Hadamard total on white PM, and 7 % for the total on white PM at $τ = T/8$.

Spectra

One-sided Welch estimates of the frequency and phase spectra and the single-sideband phase noise, returned as a SpectralResult. S_x(f) = S_y(f) / (2πf)² and ℒ(f) = ½ (2π f_carrier)² S_x(f). Plotting shows them with power-law slope guides.

SigmaTau.psd_y — Function
psd_y(data::FrequencyData; nperseg=nothing, noverlap=nothing, taper=:hann) → SpectralResult
psd_y(data::PhaseData;     nperseg=nothing, noverlap=nothing, taper=:hann) → SpectralResult

One-sided power spectral density of the fractional frequency y(t), S_y(f) in units of 1/Hz (IEEE Std 1139-2022 §3.4), estimated by Welch's method at sample rate fs = 1/τ₀.

PhaseData input is differenced to fractional frequency (y[k] = (x[k+1] − x[k])/τ₀) first. nperseg defaults to the largest power of two up to N ÷ 4 (at least 16), noverlap to half of it; raise nperseg for finer low-frequency resolution, lower it for more averaging. taper is :hann, :hamming, or :rectangular.

The estimate is variance-preserving: Σ S_y(f)·Δf ≈ var(y) over [0, fs/2].

SigmaTau.psd_x — Function
psd_x(data::PhaseData;     nperseg=nothing, noverlap=nothing, taper=:hann) → SpectralResult
psd_x(data::FrequencyData; nperseg=nothing, noverlap=nothing, taper=:hann) → SpectralResult

One-sided power spectral density of the phase (time) residual x(t), S_x(f) in units of s²/Hz (IEEE Std 1139-2022 §3.3), estimated by Welch's method at sample rate fs = 1/τ₀.

FrequencyData input is integrated to phase (x[k] = τ₀·Σⱼ y[j]) first. The estimators are related by S_x(f) = S_y(f) / (2πf)²; the two agree under that identity at low frequency, where the discrete integrator matches the continuous one. Keyword arguments match psd_y.

SigmaTau.ssb_phase_noise — Function
ssb_phase_noise(data::PhaseData;     f_carrier, nperseg=nothing, noverlap=nothing, taper=:hann) → SpectralResult
ssb_phase_noise(data::FrequencyData; f_carrier, nperseg=nothing, noverlap=nothing, taper=:hann) → SpectralResult

Single-sideband phase noise ℒ(f) in dBc/Hz (IEEE Std 1139-2022 §3.5).

Converts the seconds-valued phase residual to phase in radians at the carrier f_carrier (Hz, required), S_φ(f) = (2π·f_carrier)²·S_x(f), and reports ℒ(f) = (1/2)·S_φ(f) on a decibel scale, 10·log₁₀(ℒ_linear). Useful for direct comparison against oscillator-vendor phase-noise plots.

The DC bin (f = 0) is dropped, since ℒ(0) is not meaningful and the dB conversion would be -Inf. Keyword arguments otherwise match psd_y.

SigmaTau.integrated_jitter — Function
integrated_jitter(s::SpectralResult; f_carrier, band=nothing) → (; phase, time, residual_fm)
integrated_jitter(f, S; quantity=:L, f_carrier, band=nothing) → (; phase, time, residual_fm)

Integrated phase jitter and residual FM over the offset band band = (f₁, f₂) in Hz (default: the whole spectrum), NIST SP1065 §5.2.19 (Riley, Jul 2008) and IEEE Std 1139-1999 eq. (A.16) (IEEE Standards Coordinating Committee 27, 1999). Between neighbouring points the phase spectrum S_φ(f) is taken as the power law K·f^x through them, a straight line on a log-log plot, and integrated exactly:

  • phase: Δφ = √∫ S_φ(f) df, rms radians;
  • time: Δφ / (2π·f_carrier), rms seconds (S_x = S_φ/(2πν₀)², IEEE 1139 eq. A.14) (Howe and Tasset, 2003);
  • residual_fm: Δf = √∫ f²·S_φ(f) df, rms hertz.

s is a ssb_phase_noise, psd_x or psd_y result. The second form takes spectrum points, such as a phase-noise analyzer's ℒ(f) in dBc/Hz (quantity=:L, S_φ = 2·ℒ, IEEE 1139 eq. A.12), or :Sx (s²/Hz) or :Sy (1/Hz); f must increase. f_carrier is the carrier in Hz.

julia> f = [1e3, 1e4, 1e5];  # ℒ falling 10 dB a decade: x = -1

julia> j = integrated_jitter(f, [-100.0, -110.0, -120.0]; f_carrier=10e6);

julia> round(j.phase; sigdigits=4), round(j.time * 1e12; sigdigits=4), round(j.residual_fm; sigdigits=4)
(0.0009597, 15.27, 31.62)

Power-law model

The power-law model S_y(f) = Σ h_α f^α links the two domains. powerlaw_fit fits the hα to a record's σ(τ) or to a spectrum, including a datasheet's ℒ(f) points. [`powerlawsigma](@ref) and [powerlawpsd`](@ref) turn a model into σ(τ) or into Sy(f), S_x(f) or ℒ(f).

f = powerlaw_fit([1, 10, 100, 1e3, 1e4], [-95, -125, -140, -150, -152];
                 quantity=:L, f_carrier=10e6)
powerlaw_sigma(f, [1.0, 10.0]; tau0=1e-3)    # ADEV sampled at 1 ms
SigmaTau.powerlaw_fit — Function
powerlaw_fit(data [, m_values]; deviation=:mhdev, alphas=2:-1:-2) → PowerLawFit
powerlaw_fit(s::SpectralResult; alphas=2:-1:-2, f_carrier=nothing) → PowerLawFit
powerlaw_fit(f, S; quantity=:Sy, alphas=2:-1:-2, f_carrier=nothing) → PowerLawFit

Fit the power-law coefficients hα of `Sy(f) = Σ h_α f^α` by nonnegative least squares, either to a record's σ(τ) or to a spectrum.

From a record (PhaseData or FrequencyData, NaN gaps allowed): the deviation (:adev, :mdev, :hdev or :mhdev, without bias correction) is computed on the octave grid, or at m_values, and each α contributes its exact σ²(τ) for a record sampled at τ₀ (see powerlaw_sigma). The response is exact down to m = 1, so every point is fitted, and white and flicker PM stay apart even under ADEV. The weights are the χ² ones, νₖ/(2σ⁴ₖ), with σ²ₖ from the model and the EDF from each point's dominant component and its term count, so a τ that gaps cost terms counts for less. The Allan pair takes α from −2 to 2; the Hadamard pair, which also tolerates a linear frequency drift, from −4 to 2.

From a spectrum: a SpectralResult of psd_y, psd_x or ssb_phase_noise (which needs f_carrier), or points f, S such as a datasheet's phase noise table, where quantity is :Sy (1/Hz), :Sx (s²/Hz) or :L (dBc/Hz). A Welch estimate is fitted with the sampled spectrum h_α (sin(πfτ₀)/(πτ₀))^α, points with h_α f^α. Every point has the same fractional weight. The DC bin is left out.

The coefficients cannot go negative, so a component the record does not have comes out small but positive, at a few percent of its neighbours' expense. The long-τ octaves carry few samples: h₋₂ from a σ(τ) fit scatters by about 20 % on 65 536 samples. Compare fit with y before trusting a small hα. [`noiseidnnls`](@ref) fits pure power laws to name the dominant noise type; this fit gives the calibrated hα.

Examples

using SigmaTau
p = noise_gen(PhaseData, 65536, 1.0; h = Dict(2 => 1e-22, 0 => 1e-24, -2 => 1e-30))
f = powerlaw_fit(p)                 # from MHDEV
powerlaw_sigma(f, [1.0, 100.0])     # ADEV the model predicts at 1 s and 100 s

SigmaTau.powerlaw_sigma — Function
powerlaw_sigma(h, tau; deviation=:adev, tau0) → Vector{Float64}

σ(τ) that the power-law model h gives in deviation (:adev, :mdev, :hdev or :mhdev) for a record sampled at tau0. h is a PowerLawFit or a ClockModel (whose tau0 is the default), or a Dict of hα keyed by α or noise type, as in [`noisegen](@ref). Every τ must be a whole multiple oftau0`.

The model is the spectrum of a sampled record, h_α (sin(πfτ₀)/(πτ₀))^α up to the Nyquist frequency fh = 1/(2τ₀), integrated through the estimator's transfer function. Well above m = 1 this gives SP1065's closed forms, such as h₀/(2τ) for white FM in ADEV, 2 ln 2 · h₋₁ for flicker FM and (2π²/3) h₋₂ τ for random-walk FM. White and flicker PM depend on the bandwidth, here fh; 3 f_h h₂/(4π²τ²) holds for white PM at every m.

Examples

using SigmaTau
powerlaw_sigma(Dict(:WHFM => 2e-24), [1.0, 10.0, 100.0]; tau0=1.0)   # ≈ 1e-12 τ^-1/2

Interactive fit

noise_fit builds the model by hand, one noise type at a time: pick a type and the τ where it dominates, and its level is fitted there with the slope fixed, against what the other components leave. This is the way to go when you know which noise sits where, or when powerlaw_fit spends a component on a bump.

f = noise_fit(mhdev(p))
add_component!(f, :WHPM, 1:2)
add_component!(f, :WHFM, 6:9)
noise_residual(f)                  # σ² the model leaves at each τ
undo!(f)
SigmaTau.noise_fit — Function
noise_fit(r::StabilityResult) → NoiseFit

Start an interactive power-law fit on r, an ADEV, MDEV, HDEV or MHDEV result or one of their total forms (which are fitted with the parent's response). Add components with add_component! and take the last one back with undo!.

Each component's σ²(τ) is the exact response of a sampled record, as in powerlaw_fit. Its level is the weighted mean of log(residual / response) over its own τ range, the residual being the measured σ² less every other component, with the slope fixed and each point weighted by its confidence interval, 1/log(hi/lo)². Without intervals the points weigh the same. After each change every level is refitted against the others until they settle, so the model does not depend on the order the components were added in, and a component is not inflated by a neighbour that was added after it.

Examples

using SigmaTau
p = noise_gen(PhaseData, 65536, 1.0; h = Dict(2 => 1e-22, 0 => 1e-24, -2 => 1e-30))
f = noise_fit(mhdev(p))
add_component!(f, :WHPM, 1:3)      # white PM on the first three τ
add_component!(f, :WHFM, 4:9)
add_component!(f, :RWFM, 11:14)
undo!(f)                           # take random-walk FM back out
clock_model(f)

Clock model

clock_model turns a power-law fit into the state-space ClockModel the Kalman filters run on: white PM becomes the measurement noise, white, random-walk and random-run FM drive the phase, frequency and drift states. The clock model derives the mapping and the matrices.

m = clock_model(powerlaw_fit(p; alphas=[2, 0, -2]))
state_transition(m, 1.0), process_noise(m, 1.0)
powerlaw_sigma(m, [1.0, 100.0])    # σ(τ) the model stands for
SigmaTau.ClockModel — Type
ClockModel(; tau0, R=0, q1=0, q2=0, q3=0, drift=q3 > 0)

Discrete clock model with state [phase, frequency], or [phase, frequency, drift] when drift = true. Each state is driven by white noise with the diffusion coefficients of Zucca & Tavella (2005):

fieldnoiseS_y(f)unit
Rwhite PM, phase variance per sample at tau0h₂ = 8π² τ₀ Rs²
q1white FM, phase diffusionh₀ = 2 q1s
q2random-walk FM, frequency diffusionh₋₂ = q2 / 2π²1/s
q3random-run FM, drift diffusionh₋₄ = q3 / 8π⁴1/s³

R is the noise of a reading, taken as white; q1–q3 are process noise, see process_noise. Build one from a noise fit with clock_model, and pass it to powerlaw_sigma or powerlaw_psd to see the σ(τ) or spectrum it stands for.

  • tau0::Float64: Sample interval in seconds, to which R refers.

  • R::Float64: White PM phase variance per sample (s²).

  • q1::Float64: White FM diffusion coefficient (s).

  • q2::Float64: Random-walk FM diffusion coefficient (1/s).

  • q3::Float64: Random-run FM diffusion coefficient (1/s³).

  • drift::Bool: Whether the state carries a frequency drift.

SigmaTau.clock_model — Function
clock_model(fit::PowerLawFit; drift=…) → ClockModel
clock_model(h::AbstractDict; tau0, drift=…) → ClockModel

The ClockModel of a power-law model: white FM (α = 0), random-walk FM (−2) and random-run FM (−4) drive the states, and the PM is the reading noise R. drift defaults to whether h₋₄ is positive. h takes α or noise names as keys, as in powerlaw_sigma.

White PM (α = 2) is R = h₂/8π²τ₀. Flicker PM (α = 1) is not white and has no state; the filter averages it like white PM over its own response time and follows the slower part as if it were the clock. It is folded into R as the measurement variance of (Van Dierendonck et al., Nov 1984), the PM at the frequencies the tracker does not follow: R is the white PM with the model's PM Allan variance at the lag where PM hands over to FM. Leaving it out would make the filter trust its readings too much.

Flicker FM and flicker walk (α = −1, −3) have no finite-state form and are left out. Compare powerlaw_sigma(model, τ) with powerlaw_sigma(fit, τ) to see what that costs over the τ you care about.

Examples

using SigmaTau
p = noise_gen(PhaseData, 65536, 1.0; h = Dict(2 => 1e-22, 0 => 1e-24, -2 => 1e-30))
m = clock_model(powerlaw_fit(p; alphas = [2, 0, -2]))
process_noise(m, 10.0)        # Q over a 10 s step

SigmaTau.process_noise — Function
process_noise(m::ClockModel, δ) → Matrix

Covariance Q(δ) of the noise the clock picks up over a step of δ seconds, the white-noise drives integrated over the step (Greenhall 2003, §2):

Q₁₁ = q1 δ + q2 δ³/3 + q3 δ⁵/20    Q₁₂ = q2 δ²/2 + q3 δ⁴/8    Q₁₃ = q3 δ³/6
Q₂₂ = q2 δ + q3 δ³/3               Q₂₃ = q3 δ²/2              Q₃₃ = q3 δ

Without drift, Q is the upper-left 2×2 block. It composes over steps: Q(a + b) = Φ(b) Q(a) Φ(b)ᵀ + Q(b).

Two channels

cross_psd_y and cross_psd_x average the cross-spectrum of two simultaneous records of one device; cross_adev is the matching cross Allan variance. Two-channel measurements shows when and how to use them.

SigmaTau.cross_psd_y — Function
cross_psd_y(a, b; nperseg=nothing, noverlap=nothing, taper=:hann) → CrossSpectralResult

Cross power spectral density of the fractional frequency of two simultaneous records of the same device, a and b (PhaseData or FrequencyData, same τ₀ and length, both the same kind). Welch's method as in psd_y, with the averaged cross-periodogram conj(A)·B in place of |A|².

Noise present in both channels survives the average; noise in only one (each reference, each channel's electronics) averages toward zero, leaving a residual of about floor = √(S_aa S_bb / K), K the number of independent averages; the full distribution of the estimate (a variance-gamma for two channels) is derived in (Baudiquez et al., 2020) and, for more instruments, (Baudiquez et al., 2022). Measuring a device against two independent references gives its own spectrum below either reference's noise; splitting one signal into two channels gives the source's noise below the channels' floor.

CrossSpectralResult

SigmaTau.cross_adev — Function
cross_adev(a, b, m_values=Octave) → CrossStabilityResult

Cross Allan variance of two simultaneous records of the same device: the overlapping Allan variance with the square of each second difference replaced by the product of the two channels' second differences,

σ²_ab(τ) = Σᵢ Δ²x_a(i) Δ²x_b(i) / (2 τ² (N − 2m)),   τ = m τ₀.

It estimates the Allan variance of the noise the channels share; noise in only one channel averages toward zero, to within the uncertainty reported per τ. With b = a it is exactly the overlapping Allan variance. Because the estimate can go negative, covar keeps the sign and dev is NaN there. a, b are two PhaseData or two FrequencyData with the same τ₀ and length; m_values is a vector of averaging factors or a TauMode grid.

source
SigmaTau.CrossStabilityResult — Type
CrossStabilityResult

Return type of cross_adev.

  • tau::Vector{Float64}: Analysis intervals τ in seconds.

  • covar::Vector{Float64}: Cross Allan variance σ²_ab(τ); negative where the common noise is below the floor.

  • dev::Vector{Float64}: √covar where covar > 0, else NaN: the deviation common to both channels.

  • uncertainty::Vector{Float64}: 1σ statistical uncertainty of covar: √((σ²_a σ²_b + covar²)/ν), the variance of a cross variance of Gaussian records with ν the geometric mean of the two channels' Allan-variance EDF for their identified noise types (Vernotte and Lantz, 2019). Where the channels share nothing this is the floor; covar well above it is resolved.

  • dev_a::Vector{Float64}: Allan deviation of channel a alone.

  • dev_b::Vector{Float64}: Allan deviation of channel b alone.

  • neff::Vector{Int64}: Number of overlapping second differences averaged at each τ.

  • tau0::Float64: Sample interval τ₀ in seconds.

  • n::Int64: Number of phase samples per channel.

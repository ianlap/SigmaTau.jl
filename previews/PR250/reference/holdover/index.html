Holdover

When a clock loses its reference it runs on what it learned while it had one. holdover measures how well that works on a phase record: it learns the frequency, and with drift = true the drift, from a training stretch, lets the clock run from the last reading of the stretch, and compares with what the record did next,

\[e(\tau) = x(t+\tau) - x(t) - \hat y\,\tau - \tfrac12 \hat D\,\tau^2 ,\]

the time interval error x(t + τ) − x(t) of ITU-T G.810, clause 4.5.14 (ITU-T, Aug 1996), less the learned frequency and drift. Every sample the record allows is a start, the sliding test of Delporte et al. (Delporte et al., 2000), so the statistics at each holdover time come from the whole record: the RMS, the quantile at a chosen confidence, the worst case and the mean.

h = holdover(p; train=86400, tau=[60, 3600, 14400], limit=1e-6)
h.rms              # RMS time error at each h.tau
h.quantile         # the error 95 % of the starts stay within
h.time_to_limit    # holdover time at which that reaches 1 µs

No clock model enters. The learned frequency is the slope of a least-squares line through the stretch, or of a parabola at its last sample, and what limits it depends on the noise. For white FM q1 and random-walk FM q2 (see ClockModel) and a line over T seconds,

\[\sigma_e^2(\tau) = q_1\tau\left(1 + \frac{6\tau}{5T}\right) + q_2\left(\frac{\tau^3}{3} + \frac{13\,T\tau^2}{35}\right).\]

In each bracket the first term is the clock's own noise over τ and the second the error of the learned frequency: a longer stretch averages white FM down but lets the frequency walk away, so there is a best train, √(42 q1 / 13 q2) for this pair, and trying a few shows it on a real record. A mean error that grows as τ² under a line is drift the line cannot follow.

With anchor = false the fit's own end value replaces the last reading, which is the extrapolated fit Delporte et al. analyze.

Predicted from a noise model

The same call on a noise model predicts what the record should show: a PowerLawFit, a ClockModel, or a Dict of h_α such as a data sheet's levels.

fit = powerlaw_fit(p)
m = holdover(p;   train=86400, limit=1e-6)      # measured
q = holdover(fit; train=86400, limit=1e-6)      # predicted
q.sigma            # against m.rms
sqrt.(q.budget)    # what each noise type adds, one column per q.alphas

The error is a weighted sum of phase samples, so its variance follows exactly from the spectrum of the sampled record, the one powerlaw_sigma uses. Summing the weights once turns them into weights on the frequency samples, twice into weights on the frequency steps; that leaves white FM and random-walk FM as plain sums of squares and the flicker noises as a quadratic form with the kernel 1/(1 − 4k²), the covariance of a sequence whose spectrum is sin(πfτ₀). The flicker noises are therefore predicted on the same footing as the rest, with no state model standing in for them.

For long stretches the prediction gives the closed forms of Delporte et al. (their eqs 11 to 13 for a parabola and 17 to 19 for a line, on white, flicker and random-walk FM, all with anchor = false); the tests hold it to them within 0.4 % at 2048 samples in the stretch, and hold the measured test to the prediction on all five noise types. The first term of each bracket above, q₁τ and q₂τ³/3, is τσ_y(τ), the error of the best possible predictor on that noise (Allan, 1987): what remains is the price of learning the frequency from a finite stretch.

The prediction holds noise only. A drift D that a line does not follow adds the mean ½ D τ (τ + T). holdover_error gives the error a ClockModel predicts from a Kalman filter's last estimate instead of a fitted line.

SigmaTau.holdover — Function
holdover(data::PhaseData; train, tau, drift=false, anchor=true, limit=nothing, confidence=0.95)
    → HoldoverResult

How far the clock wanders once its reference is gone, measured on the record itself. A line is fitted to train seconds of phase (a parabola with drift = true) and the clock is then left to run on the fit's frequency, and drift, from the last reading of the stretch:

e(τ) = x(t + τ) − x(t) − ŷ τ − ½ D̂ τ²

the time interval error of ITU-T G.810 less what the stretch taught. The stretch then moves on one sample, and so on through the record, as in Delporte et al. (2000). The result holds the RMS, the confidence quantile, the worst case and the mean of e at each holdover time.

train defaults to a quarter of the record and tau to the octave multiples of τ₀ up to train; both are whole multiples of τ₀. With limit (s), time_to_limit is the holdover time at which the quantile reaches it: 0 when it is passed at the first tau already, Inf when it is not reached at the last.

With anchor = false the fit's own value at the last sample replaces the reading x(t): the extrapolated fit of Delporte et al., which averages white PM down but on a clock with random-walk FM starts from the fit's misfit.

For a clock with white FM q1 and random-walk FM q2 (see ClockModel), a line over T seconds gives the variance

q1 τ (1 + 6τ/5T) + q2 (τ³/3 + 13 T τ²/35)

the first term of each bracket the clock's own noise over τ, the second the error of the learned frequency. A line suits a clock without drift, such as caesium, and then holds better than a parabola; with drift, the line's mean error grows as τ².

The error includes the white PM of the readings. NaN samples are gaps: a start is skipped when gaps take more than half its training stretch, the reading it would be compared with or, when anchored, its last reading.

Examples

using SigmaTau
p = noise_gen(PhaseData, 65536, 1.0; h = Dict(0 => 2e-22, -2 => 1e-30))
h = holdover(p; train = 4096, limit = 1e-9)
h.rms                 # at h.tau
h.time_to_limit       # seconds within 1 ns, in 95 % of the trials
source
holdover(model; train, tau, tau0, drift=false, anchor=true, limit=nothing, confidence=0.95)
    → HoldoverPrediction

The same test predicted from a noise model: the standard deviation of the error holdover measures on a record, for a clock with the power-law noise of model. model is a PowerLawFit or a ClockModel (whose tau0 is the default), or a Dict of hα keyed by α or noise type, as in [`powerlawsigma](@ref).train` has no default here.

The error is a weighted sum of phase samples, so its variance follows exactly from the spectrum of the sampled record, h_α (sin(πfτ₀)/(πτ₀))^α up to 1/(2τ₀), flicker noise included; budget holds each noise type's share. For long stretches it gives the closed forms of Delporte et al. (2000). The quantile is the Gaussian one, 1.96 σ at 95 %. A line takes α from 2 to −2; a parabola (drift = true) also −3 and −4.

A drift D that the model does not hold adds the mean ½ D τ (τ + T) under a line over T seconds, and nothing under a parabola.

Examples

using SigmaTau
h = holdover(Dict(:WHFM => 2e-22, :FLFM => 1e-27); tau0 = 1.0, train = 3600, limit = 1e-9)
h.sigma               # at h.tau
sqrt.(h.budget)       # white FM and flicker FM, side by side

SigmaTau.HoldoverResult — Type
HoldoverResult

Output of holdover: the statistics of the time error at each holdover time, over every start the record allows.

  • tau::Vector{Float64}: Holdover times in seconds, from the last training sample.

  • rms::Vector{Float64}: RMS time error (s).

  • quantile::Vector{Float64}: The |error| (s) that confidence of the trials stay within.

  • worst::Vector{Float64}: Largest |error| (s) of any trial.

  • mean::Vector{Float64}: Mean error (s); away from zero when the fit lacks a term the clock has, such as drift.

  • n::Vector{Int64}: Number of trials.

  • train::Float64: Length of the training stretch in seconds.

  • drift::Bool: Whether the fit is a parabola (frequency drift) rather than a line.

  • anchor::Bool: Whether each trial starts from the last reading rather than the fit's value there.

  • confidence::Float64: Level of quantile.

  • limit::Float64: The limit asked for (s); NaN when none.

  • time_to_limit::Float64: Holdover time (s) at which quantile reaches limit; NaN when none.

SigmaTau.HoldoverPrediction — Type
HoldoverPrediction

Output of holdover on a noise model: the time error the model predicts at each holdover time, and what each noise type adds to it.

  • tau::Vector{Float64}: Holdover times in seconds, from the last training sample.

  • sigma::Vector{Float64}: Standard deviation of the time error (s).

  • quantile::Vector{Float64}: The |error| (s) the clock stays within with probability confidence.

  • alphas::Vector{Int64}: Exponents α of the model's noise types, highest first.

  • budget::Matrix{Float64}: Variance (s²) each noise type adds: one row per tau, one column per α. The rows sum to sigma.^2.

  • train::Float64: Length of the training stretch in seconds.

  • drift::Bool: Whether the fit is a parabola (frequency drift) rather than a line.

  • anchor::Bool: Whether the clock starts from the last reading rather than the fit's value there.

  • confidence::Float64: Level of quantile.

  • limit::Float64: The limit asked for (s); NaN when none.

  • time_to_limit::Float64: Holdover time (s) at which quantile reaches limit; NaN when none.

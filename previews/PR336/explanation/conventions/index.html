Conventions

The quantities, symbols and units used by every function of the package and every page of this documentation. They follow SP1065 and IEEE Std 1139-2022.

Phase and frequency

A signal of nominal frequency $ν_0$ with phase deviation $φ(t)$ in radians has the time deviation $x(t)$, in seconds, and the fractional frequency $y(t)$, which is dimensionless (SP1065 §3.1):

\[x(t) = \frac{φ(t)}{2πν_0}, \qquad y(t) = \frac{ν(t) - ν_0}{ν_0} = \frac{dx}{dt} .\]

This documentation calls $x(t)$ the phase. A measurement gives it as the reading of the clock under test less the reading of the reference. The other sign changes no deviation and no spectrum, only the sign of a fitted drift.

A record holds samples at a uniform interval $τ_0$:

RecordSamplesUnit
PhaseData$x_i = x(t_1 + (i-1)τ_0)$, $i = 1, …, N$seconds
FrequencyData$y_i = (x_{i+1} - x_i)/τ_0$, $i = 1, …, N-1$none

A frequency sample is the average of $y(t)$ over its interval, with no dead time between samples, so $N$ phase samples and $N - 1$ frequency samples describe the same record. Every estimator works on phase and integrates a frequency record first, from $x_1 = 0$. A missing sample is NaN (Timestamps and gaps).

FrequencyData(p) and PhaseData(f) convert a record to the other kind. The estimators do not need it.

Averaging time

A deviation is evaluated at averaging times $τ = mτ_0$, where the integer $m$ is the averaging factor. Every estimator takes the factors in one of three forms:

adev(p)                # octave spacing, m = 1, 2, 4, …
adev(p, Decade)        # a named grid, a TauMode
adev(p, [1, 10, 100])  # the factors themselves

A named grid stops at the largest factor the estimator supports on the record. For $N$ phase samples that is

EstimatorsLargest $m$
adev, pdev$⌊(N-2)/2⌋$
totdev$⌊(N-1)/2⌋$
mdev, tdev, htotdev$⌊(N-1)/3⌋$
hdev$⌊(N-2)/3⌋$
mtotdev, ttotdev$⌊N/3⌋$
mhdev, htdev$⌊(N-1)/4⌋$
mhtotdev$⌊N/4⌋$
mtie, tierms$N-1$
theo1the largest even $m ≤ N-1$

The Allan, Hadamard and parabolic estimators keep at least two terms at their longest $τ$; the total estimators keep one subsequence, which the extension turns into a usable estimate. tau_values returns the factors of a grid, and TauMode lists the grids.

Thêo1 is the one estimator whose averaging time is not $mτ_0$: a result at factor $m$ is reported at $τ = 0.75\,mτ_0$ (SP1065 §5.2.15).

What an estimator returns

Each estimator returns a StabilityResult with one entry per $τ$:

FieldContent
tauaveraging time, in seconds
devthe deviation, the square root of the variance estimate
noise_typethe power-law noise type used for the interval and the bias factor
cithe confidence interval (lo, hi), at 68.3 % unless confidence says otherwise
edfthe equivalent degrees of freedom behind the interval
neffthe number of terms averaged

Deviations of fractional frequency, written $σ_y(τ)$, are dimensionless. The time deviations tdev, htdev and ttotdev, written $σ_x(τ)$, and mtie and tierms are in seconds.

adev and hdev are the overlapping estimators. The standard recommends the overlapping form of the Allan deviation (IEEE Std 1139-2022, A.3.2); overlapping=false gives the non-overlapped forms.

Noise types

Noise is modeled as a sum of power laws, $S_y(f) = \sum_α h_α f^α$, and each term has a label:

LabelNoise$α$
:WHPMwhite phase modulation (PM)2
:FLPMflicker PM1
:WHFMwhite frequency modulation (FM)0
:FLFMflicker FM−1
:RWFMrandom-walk FM−2
:FWFMflicker-walk FM−3
:RRFMrandom-run FM−4

The Allan variance converges down to $α = -2$. The last two labels exist for the Hadamard family, whose third difference converges down to $α = -4$ (SP1065 §3.4). Power-law noise gives the slopes and the conversions.

Spectra

Spectral densities are one-sided, over the Fourier frequency $f$ in hertz (IEEE Std 1139-2022, 3.1 and A.2):

QuantitySymbolUnitRelation
fractional frequency$S_y(f)$1/Hz
time deviation$S_x(f)$s²/Hz$S_x = S_y/(2πf)^2$
phase$S_φ(f)$rad²/Hz$S_φ = (2πν_0)^2 S_x$
single-sideband phase noise$ℒ(f)$dBc/Hz$ℒ = S_φ/2$, quoted as $10\log_{10}ℒ$
